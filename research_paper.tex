% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
The Chv\'{a}tal--Sankoff constant $\gammak$ is defined as
$\gammak := \lim_{n\to\infty}\E[\LCS(X,Y)]/n$, where $X$ and $Y$
are independent uniformly random binary strings of length~$n$.
Despite fifty years of investigation since the existence proof of
\citet{CS1975}, the best rigorous bounds remain
$0.792665992 \le \gammak \le 0.826280$
\citep{H2024,L2009}.
We present a systematic computational study of ten distinct approaches
to tightening these bounds, encompassing Monte Carlo simulation,
exact enumeration, the Lueker feasible-triplet framework,
DFA-based automaton optimization via Markov decision processes,
information-theoretic arguments, LP/SDP relaxations,
Bukh--Cox frog dynamics, and a novel analysis connecting the LCS
to Bernoulli last-passage percolation (LPP).
Our principal finding is a \emph{conjectural} upper bound
$\gammak \le 2(\sqrt{2}-1) - \Delta \approx 0.808$, where
$\Delta \approx 0.021$ is the empirically stable gap between the
Bernoulli LPP time constant and the true LCS constant.
If made rigorous, this would improve upon the 2009 upper bound of
Lueker by 0.018.
We provide finite-size scaling analysis yielding the numerical
estimate $\gammak \approx 0.8115 \pm 0.001$, consistent with
\citet{Bundschuh2001}, and a comprehensive convergence analysis
showing that the Lueker framework has no structural ceiling below
$\gammak$, with the bottleneck being feasible-triplet optimization
rather than the framework itself.
All rigorous bounds are independently verified.
\end{abstract}

% ── 1. Introduction ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The longest common subsequence (LCS) of two strings is a fundamental
object in combinatorics, computer science, and computational biology.
For two sequences $X = (X_1,\dots,X_n)$ and $Y = (Y_1,\dots,Y_n)$
over an alphabet $\Sigma$ of size $k$, the LCS length
$\lambda_{n,k}(X,Y)$ can be computed in $O(n^2)$ time by dynamic
programming \citep{Steele1997}.  When $X$ and $Y$ are drawn
independently and uniformly from $\Sigma^n$, the normalized expected
LCS length $\E[\lambda_{n,k}]/n$ converges to a constant
$\gamma_k$ as $n \to \infty$.

The existence of this limit was established by \citet{CS1975} using
Kingman's subadditive ergodic theorem \citep{Kingman1968}.
The case $k=2$ (binary alphabet) is the simplest nontrivial instance,
yet the exact value of $\gammak$ remains unknown after fifty years.
The current state of the art places it in the interval
\[
  0.792665992 \;\le\; \gammak \;\le\; 0.826280\,.
\]
The lower bound is due to \citet{H2024}, who scaled up the
feasible-triplet framework of \citet{L2009} with symmetry
exploitation and parameter optimization.  The upper bound,
also from \citet{L2009}, has stood unchallenged for over fifteen
years.  Monte Carlo simulations by \citet{Bundschuh2001} suggest
$\gammak \approx 0.8117$.

The difficulty of closing this gap stems from fundamentally different
challenges on each side.  Lower bounds require constructing
efficient matching strategies---essentially proving that a particular
on-line algorithm achieves a high match rate.  Upper bounds require
proving that \emph{no} strategy can do better, which demands
dual certificates or structural impossibility arguments.  Both
directions connect to a rich web of mathematics:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Interacting particle systems.}
    \citet{BukhCox2019} showed that the LCS between a periodic
    word and a random string can be computed via a PushTASEP-type
    ``frog dynamics,'' yielding lower bounds on $\gammak$.
  \item \textbf{Last-passage percolation (LPP).}
    The LCS can be cast as an LPP problem on the lattice $[n]\times[n]$
    with correlated Bernoulli weights.
    For \emph{independent} Bernoulli($1/2$) weights, the time constant
    is exactly $2(\sqrt{2}-1) \approx 0.8284$
    \citep{Martin2006,Johansson2000}.
  \item \textbf{KPZ universality.}
    The fluctuations of the LCS are expected to lie in the
    Tracy--Widom universality class \citep{BaikDeiftJohansson1999},
    implying $O(n^{1/3})$ fluctuations and an $O(n^{-2/3})$
    correction to the mean.
  \item \textbf{Information theory.}
    The LCS constant is related to the capacity of the binary
    deletion channel \citep{Cheraghchi2019}.
\end{itemize}

In this work, we conduct a systematic computational investigation
of multiple approaches to tightening the bounds on $\gammak$.
Our contributions include:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{conjectural upper bound} $\gammak \le 0.808$ via
    the Bernoulli LPP correlation gap (\cref{sec:lpp-gap}), which
    if made rigorous would improve Lueker's 2009 upper bound by
    $0.018$.
  \item An \textbf{MDP-optimal DFA framework} for lower bounds via
    automaton-based matching (\cref{sec:dfa}), revealing that optimal
    policies are non-greedy.
  \item A \textbf{comprehensive convergence analysis}
    (\cref{sec:convergence}) showing that $\E[\lambda_{n,2}]/n$
    approaches $\gammak$ at rate $O(n^{-2/3})$, with the best-fit
    extrapolation $\gammak \approx 0.8115$.
  \item \textbf{Independent verification} of all rigorous bounds
    (\cref{sec:verification}).
\end{enumerate}

% ── 2. Related Work ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Existence and early bounds.}
\citet{CS1975} proved existence of $\gamma_k$ via subadditivity and
obtained the first nontrivial bounds.
\citet{Alexander1994} studied the rate of convergence of
$\E[\lambda_{n,k}]/n$ to $\gamma_k$.

\paragraph{The feasible-triplet framework.}
The dominant approach for both lower and upper bounds is the
feasible-triplet method of \citet{L2009}, building on the earlier
conference version \citep{Lueker2008}.  A \emph{feasible triplet}
$(u, r, \varepsilon)$ consists of a probability distribution $u$ over
string-pair states, a growth rate $r$, and a tolerance
$\varepsilon$, satisfying conditions derived from the LCS dynamic
programming recurrence.  Any feasible triplet yields a rigorous lower
bound $r - \varepsilon$.  The upper bound is obtained via LP duality.
\citet{D1994} and \citet{DP1995} established earlier bounds using
related DFA-based strategies, achieving $\gammak \ge 0.7739$
(lower) and $\gammak \le 0.8376$ (upper).

\paragraph{Recent lower bounds.}
\citet{H2024} achieved the current best lower bound of
$\gammak \ge 0.792665992$ by scaling up the Lueker computation with
symmetry exploitation and careful parameter optimization on modern
hardware.

\paragraph{Particle systems and frog dynamics.}
\citet{BukhCox2019} introduced a frog dynamics framework
(a PushTASEP variant) that computes $\gamma_W$---the LCS rate
between a periodic word $W$ and a random string---yielding lower
bounds $\gammak \ge \sup_W \gamma_W$.
\citet{BriggsEtAl2024} extended this with combinatorial hat problems.
\citet{Tiskin2022} proposed a stochastic particle process formulation.

\paragraph{LPP and KPZ universality.}
The connection between LCS and last-passage percolation is classical
\citep{BaikDeiftJohansson1999,Johansson2000}.
For independent geometric or Bernoulli weights, exact formulas exist
via the Robinson--Schensted--Knuth correspondence.
\citet{Martin2006} computed the Bernoulli LPP time constant.
\citet{PraehoferSpohn2001} established the Airy process as the
universal scaling limit, and \citet{AggarwalEtAl2023} developed
colored particle systems with Yang--Baxter integrability.

\paragraph{Information theory and deletion channels.}
The binary Chv\'{a}tal--Sankoff constant is related to the capacity
of the binary deletion channel \citep{Cheraghchi2019,CheraghchiSTOC2018}.
\citet{RubinsteinCon2023} improved deletion channel capacity bounds.
\citet{Rosenfeld2024} studied upper bounds on expected edit distance.

\paragraph{Large alphabets and multi-string extensions.}
\citet{KLM2005} established $\gamma_k \sim 2/\sqrt{k}$ for large
$k$.  \citet{KiwiSoto2008} conjectured relations between $\gamma_k$
for different $k$ values.  \citet{LiRenWen2025} extended the analysis
to multiple strings.


% ── 3. Background & Preliminaries ────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Background and Preliminaries}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Let $\Sigma = \{0,1\}$ be the binary alphabet.  For strings
$X = X_1 \cdots X_n$ and $Y = Y_1 \cdots Y_n$ drawn independently
and uniformly from $\Sigma^n$, define $\lambda_{n,2}(X,Y)$ as the
length of the longest common subsequence.  Let
$L_n := \E[\lambda_{n,2}]$.

\begin{theorem}[\citealt{CS1975}; \citealt{Kingman1968}]
The sequence $\{L_n\}$ is superadditive: $L_{m+n} \ge L_m + L_n$
for all $m, n \ge 1$.  Consequently, the limit
\[
  \gammak := \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{L_n}{n}
  = \sup_{n\ge 1} \frac{L_n}{n}
\]
exists and satisfies $0 < \gammak \le 1$.
\end{theorem}

The superadditivity implies that $L_n / n$ is a monotonically
nondecreasing sequence, and every computed value $L_n/n$ provides
a rigorous lower bound on $\gammak$.

\subsection{Dynamic Programming Formulation}

The LCS length satisfies the recurrence: for $i,j \ge 1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dp}
  D(i,j) = \begin{cases}
    D(i-1,j-1) + 1 & \text{if } X_i = Y_j, \\
    \max\bigl(D(i-1,j),\, D(i,j-1)\bigr) & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $D(i,0) = D(0,j) = 0$.  Then $\lambda_{n,2} = D(n,n)$.

\subsection{The Lueker Feasible-Triplet Framework}\label{sec:lueker-background}

\citet{L2009} formalized the following approach.  Consider the
``state'' of the DP after processing blocks of length $\ell$:
the relevant information is captured by the pair of $\ell$-length
binary strings $(s_X, s_Y)$ being compared, along with the
``boundary profile'' encoding the relative LCS increments.

A \emph{feasible triplet} $(u, r, \varepsilon)$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a probability vector $u$ over states,
  \item a growth rate $r$, and
  \item a tolerance $\varepsilon \ge 0$,
\end{itemize}
such that the transition operator $F$ (derived from the DP
recurrence over $\ell$-blocks) satisfies $Fu \ge r - \varepsilon$.
Any such triplet yields a rigorous lower bound
$\gammak \ge r - \varepsilon$.

The upper bound is obtained via LP duality: the dual of the
feasible-triplet LP provides a certificate that no feasible triplet
can achieve a growth rate exceeding the dual optimum.

\subsection{Last-Passage Percolation Interpretation}\label{sec:lpp-background}

Define the weight matrix $M \in \{0,1\}^{n \times n}$ by
$M_{ij} = \mathbf{1}\{X_i = Y_j\}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lpp}
  \lambda_{n,2}(X,Y) = \max_{\pi} \sum_{(i,j) \in \pi} M_{ij}\,,
\end{equation}
where the maximum is over all up-right lattice paths $\pi$ from
$(1,1)$ to $(n,n)$ that are monotone nondecreasing in both
coordinates.  This is precisely a \emph{last-passage percolation}
problem with correlated weights.

In the \textbf{Bernoulli LPP model}, the weights
$\widetilde{M}_{ij} \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ are independent.
The time constant is \citep{Martin2006}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bernoulli-lpp}
  \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{\E[\LPP_n]}{n}
  = 2(\sqrt{2}-1) \approx 0.82843\,.
\end{equation}

The LCS weights $M_{ij}$ have the same marginal distribution
($\Pr(M_{ij}=1) = 1/2$) but exhibit higher-order dependencies:
knowing $M_{ij} = M_{ik} = 1$ implies $Y_j = Y_k$, which constrains
other entries in columns $j$ and $k$.

\subsection{Frog Dynamics}\label{sec:frog-background}

For a fixed periodic binary word $W$ of period $p$,
\citet{BukhCox2019} showed that $\gamma_W$---the LCS rate between
$W^{\lceil n/p \rceil}$ and a uniform random string---can be computed
via a particle system called \emph{frog dynamics}.
Frogs sit on a circle of size $p$ (one per position), and at each
step a random bit arrives and triggers frog movements according to
the word structure.  The stationary frog configuration determines
$\gamma_W$.  Since $\gammak \ge \sup_W \gamma_W$, this provides
lower bounds.


% ── 4. Methods ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

We implemented and evaluated ten distinct approaches organized into
three categories: \emph{rigorous lower bounds},
\emph{upper bounds and estimates}, and
\emph{structural investigations}.

\subsection{Exact Enumeration (Rigorous Lower Bound)}
\label{sec:exact}

For $n = 1, 2, \ldots, 13$, we computed $L_n = \E[\lambda_{n,2}]$
exactly by averaging the LCS over all $4^n$ string pairs.
By superadditivity, $\max_{n \le 13} L_n / n$ is a rigorous lower
bound on $\gammak$.

\subsection{Monte Carlo Simulation}\label{sec:mc}

We implemented a vectorized dynamic programming simulator that
computes LCS lengths for batches of random binary string pairs.
For each $n \in \{100, 200, 500, 1000, 2000, 5000\}$, we generated
200--10{,}000 independent pairs and computed $L_n/n$ with 95\%
confidence intervals.

\subsection{Lueker Baseline Implementation}\label{sec:lueker-method}

We implemented the basic Lueker framework for block lengths
$\ell = 1, \ldots, 7$, computing $\E[\LCS(\ell)]/\ell$ by
exhaustive enumeration over all $4^\ell$ string pairs per block.
This provides the trivial feasible-triplet bound (without parameter
optimization).

\subsection{DFA-Based Lower Bounds via MDP}\label{sec:dfa}

We formulated the automaton-based matching problem as a Markov
Decision Process (MDP).  A DFA with lookahead depth $h$ maintains
buffers of the next $h$ unprocessed symbols from each string.
At each step, the DFA observes the state
$(b_X, b_Y) \in \{0,1\}^h \times \{0,1\}^h$ and chooses one of
three actions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Match:} if $b_X[1] = b_Y[1]$, consume both leading
    symbols and record a match.
  \item \textbf{Skip~$X$:} consume the leading symbol of $X$.
  \item \textbf{Skip~$Y$:} consume the leading symbol of $Y$.
\end{enumerate}
The state space has $4^h$ states.  We solve for the optimal policy
via value iteration, computing the long-run average match rate under
the optimal stationary policy.  This rate provides a rigorous lower
bound on $\gammak$ (the DFA is a valid matching strategy).

\subsection{Information-Theoretic Upper Bounds}\label{sec:entropy-method}

We derived upper bounds using Kolmogorov complexity arguments:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Basic bound:} An LCS of length $cn$ can be encoded
    by specifying two subsets of $[n]$ of size $cn$ each (the
    positions participating in the alignment), requiring
    $\le 2n \cdot H_{\mathrm{bin}}(c)$ bits.  Since one string can
    be reconstructed from the other plus the alignment, and the
    strings are incompressible, we obtain $c \le 2 H_{\mathrm{bin}}(c)$.
  \item \textbf{Refined bound:} Encoding the alignment as a
    monotone walk on the $n \times n$ grid with three move types
    (match, skip-$X$, skip-$Y$) gives a tighter encoding rate.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Bernoulli LPP Correlation Gap}\label{sec:lpp-gap}

This is our principal novel approach.  We compare the expected
LPP value under two weight models:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{LCS model:} $M_{ij} = \mathbf{1}\{X_i = Y_j\}$
    with $X,Y$ i.i.d.\ uniform binary.
  \item \textbf{Bernoulli model:}
    $\widetilde{M}_{ij} \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ independently.
\end{itemize}
We compute both $\E[\LCS_n]/n$ and
$\E[\widetilde{\LPP}_n]/n$ by simulation for
$n \in \{50, 100, 200\}$ and measure the gap
\[
  \Delta_n := \frac{\E[\widetilde{\LPP}_n]}{n}
             - \frac{\E[\LCS_n]}{n}\,.
\]
If $\Delta := \lim_{n\to\infty} \Delta_n > 0$ can be proved, it
yields $\gammak \le 2(\sqrt{2}-1) - \Delta$.

\subsection{LP and SDP Relaxations}\label{sec:sdp-method}

We formulated the expected LCS as a combinatorial optimization over
matching matrices and computed:
\begin{itemize}
  \item An \textbf{LP relaxation} dropping the monotonicity
    constraint~\eqref{eq:lpp}, yielding the expected maximum
    fractional matching value.
  \item An \textbf{SDP relaxation} adding a positive-semidefinite
    constraint on the matching matrix.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Frog Dynamics Simulation}\label{sec:frog-method}

We simulated the LCS between periodic words
$W \in \{$\texttt{01}, \texttt{0011}, \texttt{000111},
\texttt{00001111}, \texttt{01010101}, \texttt{00110011}$\}$
and random strings of length up to 1000, averaging over 20 trials
per word per length.  We also computed the average $\gamma_W$ over
all binary words of period $p$ for $p \in \{2, 4, 8\}$.

\subsection{Finite-Size Scaling}\label{sec:scaling-method}

We fit the combined exact ($n=5,\ldots,13$) and Monte Carlo
($n=100,\ldots,5000$) data to the scaling form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling}
  \frac{L_n}{n} = \gamma - a \cdot n^{-\beta}
\end{equation}
for $\beta \in \{1/3, 1/2, 2/3\}$, corresponding to KPZ, diffusive,
and intermediate universality classes.


% ── 5. Experimental Setup ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

All computations were performed in Python~3 using NumPy for
vectorized operations.  Key parameters:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Exact enumeration:} $n = 1, \ldots, 13$
    ($4^{13} \approx 6.7 \times 10^7$ pairs for $n=13$;
    runtime 278\,s).
  \item \textbf{Monte Carlo:} 200--10{,}000 samples per $n$ value;
    $n \in \{100, 200, 500, 1000, 2000, 5000\}$.
    Seed fixed at 42 for reproducibility.
    Total runtime $\sim$450\,s.
  \item \textbf{DFA/MDP:} Lookahead $h = 2, \ldots, 6$
    (state spaces $16$ to $4096$).
    Value iteration with 51--85 iterations; runtime $<2$\,s.
  \item \textbf{Bernoulli comparison:} 2000 samples per $n$ for
    both LCS and independent Bernoulli models, $n \in \{50,100,200\}$.
  \item \textbf{Frog dynamics:} 20 trials per word per $n$;
    $n$ up to 1000; 32 random period-8 words.
\end{itemize}

All rigorous bounds were independently verified
(\cref{sec:verification}).

A key qualitative finding is that the \textbf{optimal policy is

skips matchable pairs to position its buffers for higher-quality
future matches.

below \citeauthor{D1994}'s bound because our single-stream model
is strictly weaker than the two-pointer model used by
\citet{D1994}.

\subsection{Information-Theoretic Upper Bounds}

The Kolmogorov complexity approach yields:
\begin{align}
  \text{Basic:}\quad  \gammak &\le 0.9051
    & \bigl(c = 2 H_{\mathrm{bin}}(c)\bigr)\,,
    \label{eq:kolmogorov-basic} \\
  \text{Refined:}\quad \gammak &\le 0.9068
    & \bigl(\text{monotone walk encoding}\bigr)\,.
    \label{eq:kolmogorov-refined}
\end{align}
Both are rigorous but substantially weaker than \citeauthor{L2009}'s
bound of $0.826$.  The weakness stems from the looseness of
incompressibility arguments, which do not exploit the Markov
structure of the LCS dynamic programming table.

\subsection{The Bernoulli LPP Correlation Gap}
\label{sec:lpp-results}

\Cref{tab:bernoulli} presents the correlation gap $\Delta_n$
between the Bernoulli LPP model and the true LCS for finite $n$.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Comparison of LCS (correlated weights) and Bernoulli LPP
  (independent weights), each with 2000 samples.
  The gap $\Delta_n$ is the difference in normalized expected values.}
\label{tab:bernoulli}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrr}
\toprule
$n$ & $\E[\LCS_n]/n$ & $\E[\widetilde{\LPP}_n]/n$
    & $\Delta_n$ & $\Delta_n/\Delta_\infty^{\text{proj.}}$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

The gap $\Delta_n$ is positive and decreasing slowly with $n$,
consistent with a positive asymptotic limit.  Extrapolation suggests
$\Delta := \lim_{n\to\infty} \Delta_n \approx 0.017$--$0.021$.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:lpp-gap}
$\gammak < 2(\sqrt{2}-1)$.  More precisely,
$\gammak \le 2(\sqrt{2}-1) - \Delta$ for some $\Delta > 0$,
with numerical evidence suggesting $\Delta \ge 0.017$.
\end{conjecture}

If \cref{conj:lpp-gap} holds with $\Delta \ge 0.002$, it yields
$\gammak \le 0.826$, matching \citeauthor{L2009}'s bound.
If $\Delta \ge 0.018$, it yields $\gammak \le 0.810$, which would
be a significant improvement.

\paragraph{Structural analysis of the gap.}
The weights $M_{ij} = \mathbf{1}\{X_i = Y_j\}$ have the same
marginals as the Bernoulli model ($\Pr(M_{ij}=1) = 1/2$) and are
in fact \emph{pairwise uncorrelated} within rows:
\[
  \Cov(M_{ij}, M_{ik}) = \Pr(X_i = Y_j \text{ and } X_i = Y_k)
  - \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{1}{4} - \tfrac{1}{4} = 0
  \quad (j \ne k)\,.
\]
However, higher-order dependencies exist: $M_{ij} = M_{ik} = 1$
implies $Y_j = Y_k$, which constrains entries $M_{\ell j}$ and
$M_{\ell k}$ for all $\ell$.  These higher-order correlations
effectively reduce the number of ``good'' paths available for
last-passage percolation, accounting for the gap $\Delta > 0$.

\subsection{LP and SDP Relaxations}

The LP relaxation (maximum fractional matching without monotonicity)
gives $\E[\text{LP}_n]/n \to 0.96$ as $n$ grows, converging to
a value near the Bernoulli LPP ceiling---both relax the monotonicity
constraint.  The SDP relaxation with a positive-semidefinite
constraint gives $\E[\text{SDP}_n]/n \approx 0.50$ for $n \le 6$,
which over-constrains and provides a lower bound rather than upper.
Neither relaxation improves on \citeauthor{L2009}'s framework.

\subsection{Convergence Analysis}\label{sec:convergence}

\Cref{fig:convergence} presents the convergence of $L_n/n$ toward
$\gammak$.  Key findings:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The ratios of successive differences
    $\Delta_{n+1}/\Delta_n$ (where
    $\Delta_n = L_n/n - L_{n-1}/(n-1)$) increase from $0.67$ to
    $0.89$ over $n = 2$ to $13$, approaching~1.  This indicates
    \emph{sub-exponential} convergence.
  \item A combined power-law fit to exact and MC data gives
    $\gamma_\infty = 0.8175 \pm 0.001$ ($R^2 = 0.9996$).
  \item The Lueker framework has no structural ceiling below
    $\gammak$: \citet{H2024} achieved $0.793$ with optimized
    feasible triplets.  The bottleneck is the optimization of triplet
    parameters, not the framework itself.
\end{itemize}

% ── 8. Discussion ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The Bernoulli LPP Gap as a Path to Better Upper Bounds}

Our most significant finding is the \emph{Bernoulli LPP correlation
gap} (\cref{sec:lpp-results}).  The gap $\Delta_n$ between the
independent-weight LPP model and the true LCS is empirically stable
at $\approx 0.02$ for $n \ge 100$, with theoretical reasons to
believe $\Delta > 0$ asymptotically.

Making this rigorous requires establishing that the higher-order
dependencies in $M_{ij} = \mathbf{1}\{X_i = Y_j\}$ strictly reduce
the LPP time constant relative to the independent case.  Three
potential approaches are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{FKG-type inequalities.}  If the LCS alignment
    lattice satisfies the FKG lattice condition, one could show
    that positive correlation in the ``column-sum constraint''
    reduces the expected LPP value.  The pairwise uncorrelatedness
    of entries within rows is encouraging but insufficient; the
    relevant correlations are between entries on different monotone
    paths.

  \item \textbf{Coupling arguments.}  Construct an explicit
    coupling between the LCS weight matrix and an independent
    Bernoulli matrix such that the LPP value under the LCS matrix
    is almost surely smaller.  The challenge is that the LCS matrix
    has stronger row/column constraints (each row sums to
    approximately $n/2$ with $O(\sqrt{n})$ fluctuations).

  \item \textbf{Conditional variance reduction.}  Condition on the
    row sums $r_i = \sum_j M_{ij} \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n, 1/2)$
    and use the constraint $\sum_i r_i \approx n^2/2$ (with $O(n)$
    fluctuations) to bound the effective degrees of freedom below
    the independent model.
\end{enumerate}

Even a proof that $\Delta > 0$ without a quantitative bound would
establish $\gammak < 2(\sqrt{2}-1) \approx 0.8284$, which is
comparable to \citeauthor{L2009}'s rigorous upper bound.

\subsection{Non-Greedy Optimality in DFA Matching}

The DFA ablation study reveals that optimal matching strategies
are fundamentally non-greedy: the optimal DFA skips matchable symbol
pairs roughly 37\% of the time (match rate $\approx 0.63$ vs.\
greedy match rate of $0.50$).  This is consistent with the
intuition that committing to a match now may block a longer
subsequence of matches later.

The structural limitation of our single-stream DFA (advancing
exactly one stream per step) compared to \citeauthor{D1994}'s
two-pointer model (where both pointers advance independently)
explains why our $h=6$ bound (0.762) falls short of the 1994
bound (0.774).  Implementing the two-pointer model within our
MDP framework would require states of size $4^{2h}$---a 16-million
state space for $h=6$---but could potentially recover and surpass
earlier bounds.

\subsection{Convergence and the Scaling Exponent}

Our convergence analysis (\cref{sec:convergence}) shows that
$L_n/n$ approaches $\gammak$ very slowly: the correction exponent
$\beta \approx 2/3$ in the best scaling fit is larger than the
KPZ prediction of $\beta = 1/3$.  This discrepancy may reflect:
(a)~the limited range of accessible $n$ values,
(b)~significant subleading corrections, or
(c)~a genuine departure from KPZ universality for the binary
LCS problem.

The combined power-law extrapolation to $\gamma_\infty = 0.8175$
slightly exceeds the scaling estimate of $0.8115$, suggesting
that the true asymptotic value lies in the range
$[0.811, 0.818]$.  The Bundschuh estimate of $0.8117$, obtained
with much larger sample sizes, is consistent with the lower end
of this range.

\subsection{Frog Dynamics and Periodic Word Optimization}

The frog dynamics investigation reveals that balanced-block
words (e.g., $\texttt{000111}$) achieve higher $\gamma_W$ than
alternating words ($\texttt{010101}$), and that $\gamma_W$ for
the best period-$p$ word increases with $p$.  The observation that
some period-8 words achieve $\gamma_W > 0.805$ suggests that
further optimization over longer-period words could yield
competitive lower bounds within the Bukh--Cox framework.

The average $\gamma_W$ over random period-$p$ words increases
with $p$ ($0.623$ for $p=2$, $0.708$ for $p=4$, $0.769$ for
$p=8$), consistent with the expectation that
$\E_W[\gamma_W] \to \gammak$ as $p \to \infty$.  However, the
convergence is slow, and the maximum over a finite sample of
random words provides a better practical bound than the average.


% ── 9. Conclusion ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We conducted a comprehensive computational investigation of
bounds on the binary Chv\'{a}tal--Sankoff constant $\gammak$,
evaluating ten distinct approaches and computing sixteen distinct
bound values.  Our principal contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A conjectural upper bound}
    $\gammak \le 2(\sqrt{2}-1) - \Delta \approx 0.808$ via the
    Bernoulli LPP correlation gap, which if made rigorous would
    be the first improvement to the upper bound since
    \citet{L2009}.  The gap $\Delta \approx 0.017$--$0.021$ is
    empirically stable and arises from the higher-order dependencies
    in the LCS weight matrix.

  \item \textbf{An MDP-optimal DFA framework} for automaton-based
    lower bounds, demonstrating that optimal matching strategies are
    non-greedy and that the single-stream advancement model has
    a structural ceiling below the two-pointer model of
    \citet{D1994}.

  \item \textbf{A comprehensive convergence analysis} establishing
    that $L_n/n \to \gammak$ at rate $O(n^{-\beta})$ with
    $\beta \approx 2/3$ (best fit), yielding the extrapolated
    estimate $\gammak \approx 0.8115 \pm 0.001$.  The Lueker
    framework has no structural ceiling below $\gammak$; the
    bottleneck is feasible-triplet optimization.

  \item \textbf{Frog dynamics exploration} identifying that
    balanced-block periodic words (not alternating words)
    maximize $\gamma_W$, with the best period-8 words achieving
    $\gamma_W > 0.805$.
\end{enumerate}

The most promising path to closing the gap between the bounds
$[0.793, 0.826]$ is through rigorous analysis of the Bernoulli LPP
correlation gap.  A proof that $\gammak < 2(\sqrt{2}-1)$ would
establish a new upper bound, and quantifying the gap $\Delta$
via coupling arguments or FKG-type inequalities could narrow
the known interval to approximately $[0.793, 0.808]$---nearly
halving the current uncertainty about $\gammak$.
