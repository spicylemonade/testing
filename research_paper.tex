\begin{abstract}
We present a publication-oriented evaluation of a minimal, deterministic gravity simulator that progresses from a direct-force Euler baseline to two advanced method families: fixed-step symplectic integration and Barnes--Hut force approximation.
The baseline implementation is reproducible at the artifact level (hash-identical repeated runs for fixed seeds), but it fails key fidelity and runtime thresholds.
Across seeded two-body experiments, symplectic stepping reduces median maximum energy drift from 1.8696\% to 0.000800\% (99.96\% reduction) at near-equal runtime.
In a chaotic three-body setup, symplectic updates still reduce median drift by 71.05\% (from 1.764\,$\times\,10^6$\% to 5.106\,$\times\,10^5$\%), but absolute stability remains poor.
For high-$N$ random systems, Barnes--Hut achieves substantial throughput gains, with best speedups of 7.38$\times$ at $N=512$ and 5.80$\times$ at $N=1024$, while offering tunable error through the opening angle $\theta$.
Robustness sweeps show strong sensitivity of drift to timestep and softening, and the runtime target of $\leq 1.0$ ms/step at baseline $N=256$ remains unmet (observed 287.924 ms/step).
These results validate the minimal simulator as a reproducible research scaffold and identify concrete paths for hybrid and adaptive follow-up work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Minimal N-body simulators are useful for method prototyping, educational reproducibility, and controlled algorithmic comparisons before scaling to production astrophysical systems.
However, a minimal implementation still needs to satisfy three scientific constraints: deterministic reproducibility, physically meaningful diagnostics, and transparent speed--accuracy tradeoffs.

This study targets the repository objective defined in the project problem statement: build a deterministic Newtonian simulator and evaluate it against explicit thresholds on energy drift, angular momentum drift, orbit error, and runtime per step.
The repository evolution in git history reflects this staged design: deterministic baseline implementation, metric instrumentation, symplectic evaluation, Barnes--Hut scaling, controlled experiment matrix execution, statistical analysis, and final figure synthesis.

The main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A deterministic baseline pipeline with canonical JSON output and manifest-linked provenance.
    \item A controlled comparison of baseline Euler and symplectic stepping across two-body, three-body, and random-$N$ scenarios.
    \item A Barnes--Hut scaling study that quantifies throughput improvement and final-position error tradeoff for multiple $\theta$ values.
    \item A statistical and sensitivity analysis with bootstrap confidence intervals, effect sizes, and explicit threshold pass/fail checks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
The method choices are motivated by three established threads in the literature.

First, hierarchical force approximation methods (Barnes--Hut treecodes and beyond) are the canonical route to reducing the $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ cost of direct pairwise force evaluation.
Barnes and Hut established the core tree approximation framework \citep{barnes1986}, while fast multipole methods characterize the asymptotic frontier \citep{greengard1987}.
Production-scale systems such as GADGET-2 and PKDGRAV3 demonstrate the practical importance of force-approximation throughput under real simulation workloads \citep{springel2005,potter2017}.

Second, geometric and symplectic integrators are a standard strategy for reducing secular drift in long-horizon gravitational dynamics \citep{yoshida1990,forest1990,wisdom1991}.
Modern open-source ecosystems continue this line through high-performance implementations (for example WHFast) and high-accuracy adaptive alternatives such as IAS15 \citep{reintamayo2015,reinspiegel2015}.

Third, hybrid and adaptive approaches address close encounters and mixed stiffness regimes, where fixed-step methods can struggle \citep{duncan1998,chambers1999,rein2019}.
This study does not implement those hybrid policies yet, but uses them as a discussion baseline for future work.

\section{Background \& Preliminaries}
The simulator is a 2D, float64, Newtonian N-body model with softened gravity and fixed timestep integration.
For bodies $i$ and $j$ at positions $\mathbf{r}_i$ and $\mathbf{r}_j$, mass $m_j$, gravitational constant $G$, and Plummer softening $\epsilon$, acceleration is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{a}_i = \sum_{j \neq i} G m_j \frac{\mathbf{r}_j - \mathbf{r}_i}{\left(\lVert \mathbf{r}_j - \mathbf{r}_i \rVert^2 + \epsilon^2\right)^{3/2}}.
\end{equation}

The key diagnostics follow the preregistered protocol:
\begin{itemize}
    \item max energy drift (\%): $100\,\max_t |E(t)-E(0)|/|E(0)|$,
    \item max angular momentum drift (\%),
    \item center-of-mass drift,
    \item runtime per step and throughput.
\end{itemize}

Thresholds from the problem statement are: energy drift $\leq 0.10\%$, angular drift $\leq 0.01\%$, two-body orbit error $\leq 1.0\%$, and baseline runtime $\leq 1.0$ ms/step at $N=256$.

\section{Method}
\subsection*{Implementation architecture}
The simulator supports three methods via a single CLI entry point:
\texttt{baseline}, \texttt{symplectic}, and \texttt{barnes\_hut}.
Core state includes positions, velocities, masses, timestep metadata, and per-frame diagnostics.

\subsection*{Numerical methods}
\textbf{Baseline (Euler + direct force):}
explicit Euler updates with direct pairwise force accumulation.

\textbf{Symplectic:}
a fixed-step kick-drift symplectic Euler variant (single force evaluation per step).

\textbf{Barnes--Hut:}
a quadtree approximation with opening-angle control $\theta$, leaf-size control, and direct-force fallback below \texttt{bh\_min\_n}.
In current code, Barnes--Hut uses the Euler stepping path, which introduces a known force/integrator confound.

\subsection*{Provenance from repository history}
Key implementation milestones are visible in git history: baseline simulator and smoke reproducibility, metric pipeline, symplectic evaluation, Barnes--Hut implementation, experiment matrix execution, and final analysis/figures.
Artifacts used in this paper include commit-pinned metadata in \texttt{results/research/*.json} and \texttt{results/experiments/*.json}, plus per-run hashes in \texttt{results/experiments/raw/run\_manifest.jsonl}.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection*{Scenarios and matrix}
The controlled matrix evaluates three methods (baseline, symplectic, Barnes--Hut) over three scenarios:
two-body ($N=2$), three-body ($N=3$), and random ($N=64$), each with seeds $\{42,43,44,45,46\}$.
This yields 45 total runs with complete method--scenario--seed coverage.

For scalability, separate random-$N$ sweeps use $N\in\{256,512,1024\}$ with fixed $dt=8\times10^{-4}$ and $\theta\in\{0.5,0.8,1.2\}$.

\subsection*{Sensitivity and statistics}
Robustness sweeps vary baseline random-$N64$ timestep ($dt\in\{5\times10^{-4},10^{-3},2\times10^{-3}\}$) and softening ($\epsilon\in\{5\times10^{-4},10^{-3},2\times10^{-3}\}$) over five seeds.
Statistical summaries use bootstrap median confidence intervals (95\%) and paired effect sizes against baseline.

\subsection*{Environment and reproducibility controls}
Artifacts report Python 3.10.17 and Linux platform metadata, along with command strings, commit hash, and SHA-256 artifact checksums.
Baseline deterministic replay checks show hash equality for repeated two-body and random-$N64$ runs at fixed seed.

\subsection*{Sensitivity and threshold gap}
Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity-runtime} reports two key outcomes.
First, drift is sensitive to both timestep and softening: median baseline random-$N64$ energy drift rises from 3197\% to 8826\% as $dt$ increases from $5\times10^{-4}$ to $2\times10^{-3}$, and decreases from 13708\% to 1223\% as softening increases from $5\times10^{-4}$ to $2\times10^{-3}$.
Second, the runtime threshold remains far from satisfied: baseline $N=256$ mean step time is 287.924 ms/step versus a 1.0 ms/step target.

\section{Discussion}
The findings support a mixed conclusion.
The simulator successfully demonstrates two expected method-level behaviors from prior literature: (i) substantial invariant-drift improvement from symplectic stepping in low-body settings \citep{wisdom1991,yoshida1990,forest1990}, and (ii) strong high-$N$ throughput gains with Barnes--Hut and a controllable error tradeoff \citep{barnes1986,springel2005,potter2017}.

At the same time, the study exposes critical limits that align with known challenges in N-body integration.
Absolute three-body drift remains very large, random-$N64$ symplectic energy drift is not uniformly better than baseline, and runtime remains far from the project threshold.
These gaps are consistent with the absence of adaptive close-encounter handling and with integrator/force confounding in the current Barnes--Hut path \citep{duncan1998,chambers1999,rein2019}.

From a reproducibility perspective, the work is strong on deterministic artifacts and provenance but still has portability gaps in replay command conventions.
A next-phase priority is to unify metric contracts across scripts and introduce a hybrid symplectic-Barnes--Hut ablation to deconfound force and integrator effects.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper documents a complete evidence chain for a minimal gravity simulator, from deterministic baseline construction to statistical evaluation and figure-backed claims.
Symplectic stepping provides major two-body drift gains at runtime parity, and Barnes--Hut provides substantial high-$N$ speedups with tunable error.
However, project-level acceptance remains incomplete because absolute stability in difficult regimes and baseline runtime targets are not yet met.

The current artifact set is therefore best viewed as a reproducible foundation rather than a finalized high-fidelity simulator.
Future work should prioritize metric-contract unification, hybrid/deconfounded ablations, adaptive close-encounter control, and long-horizon expanded scenario coverage.
