\begin{abstract}
The repository task studied here asks for a six-line arithmetic-Kakeya witness
whose score
\[
S=\frac{m(G)+|R|}{n(G)-|T|}
\]
is at most \(1.675\), and it asks that the search be carried out using
cellular-automaton ideas rather than ad hoc graph surgery. At the current
repository snapshot, the decisive artifact is no longer a missing-verifier
blocker. The project now contains an implemented exact rational-span verifier
and synchronous forcing-trace engine, together with machine-readable finite
audits built on top of that verifier.

We present the resulting exact picture. First, we formalize the verifier-native
synchronous forcing trace normal form and the stage-indexed proof-carrying
cellular-automaton representation used by the active \Hone{} lane. Second, we
prove a width-\(2\) seedless-column obstruction for full \(2\times N\) strip
witnesses: a fresh seedless column can never generate its first forced vertex.
Third, we analyze two exhaustive finite regimes. In the full-support
\(2\times 2\) regime with palette
\(\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,2)\}\), no initial \(T\), and at most three seed
atoms, the verifier checks \(44{,}608\) witnesses and finds exactly \(48\)
exact-valid classes; every one has score \(7/4\), and every one has the same
two-layer \(1+3\) nucleation trace up to the choice of first corner. In the
one-sided fresh-column local classes of heights \(2\) and \(3\), the verifier
checks \(256\) and \(8{,}192\) states, respectively, and finds zero strict
improvements in either class. Finally, the larger exact search logs on
\(2\times 8\), \(2\times 12\), \(3\times 6\), \(3\times 8\), and \(3\times 10\)
families produce no promoted exact-valid family and no witness below the target.

The current answer to ``solve this using cellular automata'' is therefore
negative but mathematically informative. The verified search space no longer
supports unilateral strip-wave stories in the first two nontrivial widths, and
the only exact micro-gadgets presently available are rigid score-\(7/4\)
nuclei. What remains open is a coupled, two-sided, or macrocell construction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Kakeya sets in \(\RR^d\) contain a unit line segment in every direction. Their
measure may vanish, but the Kakeya conjecture predicts that their Minkowski and
Hausdorff dimensions are still maximal. The arithmetic formulation of Katz and
Tao reframes part of this problem as a statement about additive projections over
\(\ZZ^2\) \citep{katz1999}. Bourgain's slicing argument connected such
arithmetic-projection estimates back to Euclidean dimension lower bounds
\citep{bourgain1999}, and the broader Kakeya program remains active, with recent
progress even on three-dimensional geometric variants \citep{wang2025}. The
repository studied here fixes a more operational challenge: find a legal
constructible witness whose score is at most \(1.675\) and do so through a
cellular-automaton search program \citep{archivara2026task}.

That request creates two distinct burdens. The mathematical burden is exactness:
the witness must satisfy a precise six-line format over \(\ZZ\), and the score
must be computed from the true witness rather than from a proxy. The
methodological burden is locality: the proposed generator is a cellular
automaton, so any positive story must come from local proof-carrying dynamics,
not from a decoder that secretly repairs malformed candidates after the fact.
The repository's current architecture addresses both burdens by aligning the CA
state with the witness grammar itself and by routing every mathematical claim
through a no-repair exact verifier.

The current repository state is more interesting than the earlier blocker-only
snapshot. A working verifier and synchronous trace engine now exist in
\tool{tools/kakeya\_ca\_exact.py}; an exhaustive \(2\times 2\) corpus and
one-sided strip atlas now exist under
\tool{results/theory/forcing\_traces/}; and the project's open problem has
narrowed from ``recover the verifier'' to ``find a coupled local family that
escapes the new exact no-go frontier.'' The resulting paper is therefore not a
protocol-only audit. It is a theorem-and-computation paper about exact local
obstructions in a CA-guided witness search.

This manuscript makes five contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item It formalizes the repository's exact verifier-native semantics: six-line
  witnesses, generator matrices, quotient-span forcing, and the synchronous
  forcing trace normal form.
\item It states and proves a width-\(2\) seedless-column obstruction for full
  strip witnesses, explaining why a unilateral wave cannot generate its first
  new certificate in that class.
\item It gives a complete computational classification of the audited
  full-support \(2\times 2\) micro-regime:
  \(44{,}608\) witnesses checked, \(48\) exact-valid, all score \(7/4\), all
  with the same \(1+3\) two-layer trace signature up to corner symmetry.
\item It gives a complete computational no-go atlas for one-sided fresh-column
  activation in heights \(2\) and \(3\): \(256\) and \(8{,}192\) states checked,
  zero strict improvements in both classes.
\item It reports the larger exact search frontier honestly: full \(2\times N\)
  ladders and \(3\times N\) strips that would beat the target score if they
  forced successfully still fail to produce a promoted family, leaving only
  coupled or macrocell-based CA stories alive.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Arithmetic Kakeya and adjacent formulations}

The mathematical target is still the arithmetic-projection program of Katz and
Tao \citep{katz1999}. Our work does not propose a new formulation of
\(\AK(\alpha)\); it uses the repository's task-specific witness language as a
search interface to that pre-existing target. Green and Ruzsa study the
arithmetic Kakeya conjecture directly \citep{green2017}. Cowen-Breen
\emph{et al.}\ analyze nearby pattern problems that are close enough to tempt
proxy optimization but not identical to the forcing-pair task
\citep{cowenbreen2020}. Pohoata and Zakharov broaden the arithmetic-Kakeya
landscape further \citep{pohoata2024}. Tao's recent note on boundedly many
slopes and rational complexity is especially relevant as a warning that small
fixed alphabets can masquerade as progress \citep{tao2025}. Hickman and Wright
provide the natural modular comparison class \citep{hickman2018}. We use these
papers as the mathematical boundary of admissible claims, not as literal proofs
of our failure modes.

The present manuscript therefore differs from prior arithmetic-Kakeya work in a
narrow way only. It does not improve an exponent, produce a new witness below
\(67/40\), or derive a new Euclidean dimension bound. Its mathematical
contribution is finite and local: exact classification of an audited micro-regime
and exact obstructions for specific CA-style witness classes.

\subsection{Cellular automata, abelian networks, and local symbolic dynamics}

Our use of cellular automata is witness-faithful rather than generic. The
closest methodological neighbors are works on additive or algebraic CA, abelian
networks, and local search over dynamical media. Dennunzio, Formenti, and
Margara study positive expansivity for additive cellular automata over finite
abelian groups \citep{dennunzio2023}. Bond and Levine's abelian-network papers
offer a rigorous language for commuting local updates, halting, and
resource-like invariants \citep{bond2013,bond2014}. Faldor and Cully provide a
modern CA-search comparison class via quality-diversity search in Lenia
\citep{faldor2024}. None of these works searches arithmetic-Kakeya witnesses
directly; conversely, our manuscript does not prove a new theorem in classical
CA theory or abelian-network theory. The overlap is methodological and
structural.

The clearest connection is that our width-\(2\) obstruction admits an abelian
coverability interpretation. What is new in that discussion is not abelian
networks as a field, but the compiler from the witness verifier's quotient-span
semantics to a column-wise symmetric/antisymmetric resource decomposition.

\subsection{Automated mathematical discovery}

Automated-discovery systems now make it impossible to claim novelty merely by
saying that an agent or a search policy explored a mathematical object.
AlphaEvolve and the large-scale mathematical-discovery pipeline of Georgiev
\emph{et al.}\ are the correct comparison class for that broader automation story
\citep{novikov2025,georgiev2025}. Our work therefore does not claim novelty for
``agentic search'' by itself. The distinct claim is more constrained: the search
language is cellular and local, the state space is typed to the witness grammar,
and every mathematical statement is tied to a single exact verifier rather than
to a learned or permissive post-processor.

\section{Background and Preliminaries}

\subsection{Prompt-derived target}

The repository prompt uses the notation \(\AK(\alpha)\) for an arithmetic
projection inequality over finite subsets of \(\ZZ^2\) and then specializes the
task to a witness search problem with score threshold \(67/40 = 1.675\)
\citep{archivara2026task}. The operational challenge is:

\begin{quote}
find a finite label set \(X\subset \ZZ^2\) with \((0,0)\in X\) and
\(a+b\neq 0\) for every nonzero \((a,b)\in X\), together with constructible data
\((G,R,T)\), such that the resulting witness has score at most \(67/40\).
\end{quote}

Everything in this paper is anchored to that exact target. No alternate
objective, proxy loss, or repaired output is treated as success.

\subsection{Six-line witness representation}

We use the verifiable product-grid form that the prompt and repository adopt.

\begin{definition}[Product-grid vertex set]
Let \(d=(d_1,\ldots,d_k)\) be a list of positive integers. Write
\[
\Vd := \{(e_1,\ldots,e_k)\in \ZZ^k : 1\leq e_i \leq d_i \text{ for all } i\}.
\]
Its cardinality is
\[
n(d) := |\Vd| = \prod_{i=1}^k d_i.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Verifiable constructible graph]
Fix a finite label set \(X\subset \ZZ^2\) with \((0,0)\in X\). A witness graph
in verifiable form is a tuple \((d,f)\) where \(d=(d_1,\ldots,d_k)\) and
\[
f_i : [d_1]\times \cdots \times [d_{i-1}] \times [d_i-1] \to X,
\qquad 1\leq i\leq k,
\]
with omitted dictionary entries understood to have value \((0,0)\). The edge
count is
\[
m(d,f)
=
\sum_{i=1}^k
\sum_{\mathbf{a}}
1_{f_i(\mathbf{a})\neq (0,0)}
\prod_{j=i+1}^k d_j.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Seed set and forced set]
Let \(W=(X,d,f,T,R)\). The set \(T\subseteq \Vd\) is the initial forced set.
The seed set \(R\) is a finite list of partial maps \(r:\Vd\to X\) such that
each \(r\in R\) has exactly one vertex \(v\in \Vd\) with \(r(v)\neq (0,0)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Witness score]
The score of \(W=(X,d,f,T,R)\) is
\[
S(W) := \frac{m(d,f)+|R|}{n(d)-|T|},
\]
provided \(n(d)-|T|>0\).
\end{definition}

In the original forcing-rule language, edge labels may be converted into
two-support certificates, singleton \((a,-a)\) certificates may force a vertex,
and integer linear combinations of known certificates remain admissible
\citep{archivara2026task}. The executable verifier used in this paper implements
that logic through a quotient-span test described next.

\subsection{Generator matrices and verifier-native exact forcing}

\begin{definition}[Vertex-supported block vector]
For \(v\in \Vd\) and \(x=(a,b)\in \ZZ^2\), let \(E_v(x)\in \ZZ^{2\Vd}\) denote
the vector whose \(v\)-block equals \(x\) and whose other vertex blocks are
zero.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Actual edge set]
Let \(W=(X,d,f,T,R)\). If \(f_i(\mathbf{a})=x\neq (0,0)\), then for each suffix
\((s_{i+1},\ldots,s_k)\in [d_{i+1}]\times\cdots\times[d_k]\) there is an actual
edge between the two vertices that agree on all coordinates except that the
\(i\)-th coordinate is \(a_i\) in one copy and \(a_i+1\) in the other. Denote
the set of all such labeled actual edges by \(\mathcal{E}(W)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Verifier generator set]
The verifier generator set \(\Pow(W)\subset \ZZ^{2\Vd}\) consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item all seed vectors \(E_v(x)\) arising from the singleton seeds in \(R\), and
\item all edge-difference vectors \(E_u(x)-E_v(x)\) for
  \((u,v,x)\in \mathcal{E}(W)\).
\end{enumerate}
For \(v\in \Vd\), define the diagonal target vector
\[
\delta_v := E_v(1,-1).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quotient projection and forceable vertex]
Let \(F\subseteq \Vd\). Write
\[
\pi_F : \QQ^{2\Vd} \to \QQ^{2(\Vd\setminus F)}
\]
for the coordinate projection that deletes every block indexed by a vertex in
\(F\). A vertex \(v\in \Vd\setminus F\) is \emph{forceable relative to \(F\)} if
\[
\pi_F(\delta_v) \in \Span\bigl(\pi_F(\Pow(W))\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Synchronous forcing trace normal form]
For \(W=(X,d,f,T,R)\), define \(F_0:=T\) and recursively
\[
F_{s+1}
:=
F_s \cup
\{v\in \Vd\setminus F_s : v \text{ is forceable relative to } F_s\}.
\]
Write \(\Delta_s := F_{s+1}\setminus F_s\). The \emph{synchronous forcing trace
normal form} is
\[
\NF(W) := (F_0,\Delta_0,F_1,\Delta_1,\ldots).
\]
\end{definition}

This quotient-span closure is exactly the semantics executed by
\tool{tools/kakeya\_ca\_exact.py}. All theorem statements and all computational
results in the sequel are with respect to this verifier-native forcing
semantics.

\subsection{The active cellular-automaton search language}

The repository's active lane, \Hone{}, is a stage-indexed proof-carrying
cellular automaton rather than a pixel-valued image CA. Its state space has
three typed fibers:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a registry fiber storing the active label set \(X\);
\item stage-indexed edge fibers storing the candidate nonzero entries of the
  functions \(f_i\); and
\item a vertex fiber storing singleton seed labels, forced bits, and symbolic
  proof tags.
\end{enumerate}
The key methodological rule is \emph{no repair}: the decoder reads those fibers
directly into a six-line witness and rejects any malformed candidate. Figure
~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the architecture.

\subsection{Score-relevant strip families}

The strip families studied later are not arbitrary. They are the first narrow
families that would already beat the target score if a local forcing wave
existed.

\begin{lemma}[Full-support strip score formulas]\label{lem:stripscore}
Let \(W=(X,d,f,T,R)\) be a full-support strip witness.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item If \(d=(2,N)\), then \(m(d,f)=3N-2\) and
  \[
  S(W)=\frac{3N-2+|R|}{2N-|T|}.
  \]
  In particular, \(S(W)\leq 67/40\) whenever
  \[
  14N \geq 40|R| + 67|T| - 80.
  \]
\item If \(d=(3,N)\), then \(m(d,f)=5N-3\) and
  \[
  S(W)=\frac{5N-3+|R|}{3N-|T|}.
  \]
  In particular, \(S(W)\leq 67/40\) whenever
  \[
  N \geq 40|R| + 67|T| - 120.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(d=(2,N)\), stage \(1\) contributes \((2-1)N = N\) edges and stage \(2\)
contributes \(2(N-1)\) edges, so \(m(d,f)=N+2(N-1)=3N-2\). Substituting into the
score definition yields the displayed formula. The inequality
\[
\frac{3N-2+|R|}{2N-|T|}\leq \frac{67}{40}
\]
is equivalent, after cross-multiplication, to
\[
40(3N-2+|R|)\leq 67(2N-|T|),
\]
which simplifies to
\[
14N \geq 40|R| + 67|T| - 80.
\]

For \(d=(3,N)\), stage \(1\) contributes \((3-1)N = 2N\) edges and stage \(2\)
contributes \(3(N-1)\) edges, so \(m(d,f)=2N+3(N-1)=5N-3\). Again substituting
into the score formula gives the displayed expression. The inequality
\[
\frac{5N-3+|R|}{3N-|T|}\leq \frac{67}{40}
\]
is equivalent to
\[
40(5N-3+|R|)\leq 67(3N-|T|),
\]
which simplifies to
\[
N \geq 40|R| + 67|T| - 120.
\]
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:stripscore} explains the choice of larger search families: a full
\(2\times N\) strip with three or four seed atoms, or a full \(3\times N\) strip
with two seeds, already satisfies the score budget if it forces successfully.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Algorithm 1: exact verification and synchronous tracing}

The core executable artifact is \tool{tools/kakeya\_ca\_exact.py}. Its verifier
is exact in the sense relevant to this paper: it parses a six-line witness,
builds the full seed-and-edge generator system, and computes the layered
quotient-span closure by rational Gaussian elimination.

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.96\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1.} Exact verification and synchronous trace extraction
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Parse the six-line witness text into \((X,d,f,T,R)\).
\item Validate the syntax and legality constraints:
  \((0,0)\in X\), every nonzero label satisfies \(a+b\neq 0\), all dictionary
  keys are in range, every seed is singleton-supported, and \(n(d)-|T|>0\).
\item Unfold each nonzero \(f_i(\mathbf{a})\) into its actual edge set
  \(\mathcal{E}(W)\), and build the generator set \(\Pow(W)\).
\item Initialize \(F_0:=T\).
\item For \(s=0,1,\ldots\):
  \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item for each active vertex \(v\in \Vd\setminus F_s\), test whether
    \(\pi_{F_s}(\delta_v)\in \Span(\pi_{F_s}(\Pow(W)))\);
  \item define \(\Delta_s\) as the set of active vertices passing the test;
  \item if \(\Delta_s=\varnothing\), stop; otherwise set \(F_{s+1}:=F_s\cup\Delta_s\).
  \end{enumerate}
\item Output validity, the final forced set, the score, and the layered trace
  normal form \((F_0,\Delta_0,F_1,\Delta_1,\ldots)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

The design rationale is simple. Step 2 prevents the decoder from laundering
illegal witnesses. Step 3 uses the exact actual-edge expansion rather than a
compressed proxy graph. Step 5 makes the forcing history explicit, which is why
the later \(2\times 2\) and strip results can be stated as trace-shape theorems
rather than only as yes/no verdicts.

\subsection[Algorithm 2: exhaustive 2x2 corpus generation]{Algorithm 2: exhaustive \(2\times 2\) corpus generation}

The first finite audit is a complete scan of the smallest full-support regime
that can already exhibit nontrivial coupling.

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.96\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 2.} Exhaustive audited \(2\times 2\) corpus
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Fix the nonzero palette
  \[
  \mathcal{P}:=\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,2)\}.
  \]
\item Fix \(d=(2,2)\), full support on all three edge slots, and \(T=\varnothing\).
\item Enumerate all \(4^3\) assignments of labels from \(\mathcal{P}\) to the
  three edge slots.
\item Enumerate all seed sets of size \(0,1,2,\) or \(3\) from the \(16\)
  available vertex-label atoms \(\Vd\times \mathcal{P}\).
\item For each witness, run Algorithm 1. If the witness is exact-valid, record
  its score and full trace.
\item Canonicalize valid witnesses by row and column flips, and write a
  machine-readable corpus together with a summary JSON file.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

This procedure is implemented in \tool{tools/kakeya\_trace\_corpus.py}. The
choice of the palette \(\mathcal{P}\) is not arbitrary: it is the common
audited palette used across the \(2\times 2\) regime and the strip-local atlas,
and it already contains the nonzero labels of the exact-valid micro-gadgets that
survive the audit.

\subsection{Algorithm 3: one-sided local strip atlas}

The second finite audit tests whether a fresh column can ignite from one-sided
boundary information alone.

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.96\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 3.} Exhaustive one-sided local strip atlas
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Fix a height \(h\in\{2,3\}\) and the same palette \(\mathcal{P}\).
\item Enumerate all internal vertical-label assignments of the fresh \(h\times 1\)
  column; there are \(4^{h-1}\) choices.
\item For each row, enumerate both
  \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item whether a row-singleton input from the left is present, and
  \item if present, which of the \(4\) palette labels it carries.
  \end{enumerate}
  This contributes \(2^h 4^h\) one-sided boundary states.
\item For each combined local state, build the corresponding \(h\times 1\)
  witness and run Algorithm 1.
\item Mark the state as a \emph{strict improvement} if some row that is not
  already seeded becomes newly forced.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

The resulting state counts are \(4^{1}4^{2}2^{2}=256\) for height \(2\) and
\(4^{2}4^{3}2^{3}=8{,}192\) for height \(3\). This is a complete finite audit,
not a Monte Carlo sample.

\subsection{Complexity and completeness}

Let \(n:=n(d)\) and let \(g:=|\Pow(W)|\) be the total number of generators after
all nonzero \(f_i\)-entries are unfolded into actual edges.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Witness parsing and legality checks are linear in the serialized witness
  size.
\item Generator construction costs \(O(gn)\) in the straightforward dense
  implementation used by the repository.
\item One forceability query reduces to rational Gaussian elimination on a matrix
  with at most \(2n\) rows and \(g\) columns. Let \(C_{\mathrm{span}}(n,g)\)
  denote that cost.
\item Since the synchronous trace has at most \(n\) nonempty layers and each
  layer tests at most \(n\) active vertices, the total verification cost is
  \[
  O\!\bigl(n^2 C_{\mathrm{span}}(n,g)\bigr).
  \]
\end{enumerate}

For the finite audits, the relevant cost is exact enumeration count rather than
asymptotics:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Algorithm 2 performs exactly \(44{,}608\) verifier calls.
\item Algorithm 3 performs exactly \(256+8{,}192=8{,}448\) verifier calls.
\end{itemize}

Because these two audits are complete finite enumerations, their reported counts
carry zero statistical uncertainty.

\section{Theoretical Results and Proofs}

\subsection{Deterministic trace normal form}

\begin{theorem}[Determinism of \(\NF(W)\)]\label{thm:determinism}
For a fixed witness \(W\), the synchronous forcing trace normal form \(\NF(W)\)
is uniquely determined.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The witness \(W\) determines the generator set \(\Pow(W)\), the initial forced
set \(F_0=T\), and the quotient projections \(\pi_{F_s}\) for every possible
layer \(s\). For a given forced set \(F_s\), the definition of
``forceable relative to \(F_s\)'' is a membership test in the uniquely
determined vector space \(\Span(\pi_{F_s}(\Pow(W)))\). Therefore the set
\[
\Delta_s
=
\{v\in \Vd\setminus F_s : \pi_{F_s}(\delta_v)\in \Span(\pi_{F_s}(\Pow(W)))\}
\]
is uniquely determined by \(W\) and \(F_s\). Since \(F_{s+1}=F_s\cup \Delta_s\),
the next forced set is also uniquely determined. Induction on \(s\) shows that
every \(F_s\) and every \(\Delta_s\) is uniquely determined. Hence the entire
sequence \(\NF(W)\) is unique.
\end{proof}

\subsection[A width-2 seedless-column obstruction]{A width-\(2\) seedless-column obstruction}

We now formalize the obstruction stated informally in the repository's abelian
coverability note.

\begin{definition}[The class \(L_2(N)\)]
Let \(L_2(N)\) denote the class of full-support witnesses with dimensions
\((2,N)\), no initial forced vertices, arbitrary nonzero labels on every edge
slot, and arbitrary singleton seed atoms.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Width-\(2\) seedless-column obstruction]\label{thm:width2}
Let \(W\in L_2(N)\). If column \(j\) contains no seed atom, then no vertex in
column \(j\) can ever become forced in the verifier-native synchronous trace.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write the top and bottom vertices of column \(j\) as \(t_j\) and \(b_j\). Let
\[
S_j(u) := E_{t_j}(u) + E_{b_j}(u),
\qquad
A_j(u) := E_{t_j}(u) - E_{b_j}(u),
\]
for any \(u\in \QQ^2\). Thus \(S_j(u)\) is the column-symmetric mode and
\(A_j(u)\) is the column-antisymmetric mode.

We argue by contradiction. Assume some vertex in column \(j\) becomes forced.
Let \(s\) be the first layer for which \(\Delta_s\) contains either \(t_j\) or
\(b_j\). By minimality of \(s\), no vertex in column \(j\) lies in \(F_s\).
Hence both blocks of column \(j\) survive the quotient projection \(\pi_{F_s}\).

We now examine every possible projected generator whose support intersects column
\(j\).

\textbf{1. Rail generators.}
There are at most two rails incident to column \(j\): one from column \(j-1\) to
column \(j\), labeled say by \(h_{j-1}\), and one from column \(j\) to
column \(j+1\), labeled say by \(h_j\). Before quotienting, a rail generator has
the form
\[
S_j(h) - S_{j+1}(h)
\quad\text{or}\quad
S_{j-1}(h) - S_j(h),
\]
for some nonzero label \(h\). After applying \(\pi_{F_s}\), any coordinates in
previously forced columns may disappear, but the column-\(j\) contribution
remains a scalar multiple of \(S_j(h)\). In particular, every rail contribution
to column \(j\) is purely symmetric.

\textbf{2. The rung generator inside column \(j\).}
Let the vertical label in column \(j\) be \(v_j\). The rung generator is exactly
\[
A_j(v_j).
\]
This is the only generator supported entirely inside column \(j\).

\textbf{3. Seed generators.}
By hypothesis, column \(j\) contains no seed atom. So no seed generator has any
support in column \(j\).

Combining these three cases, every vector in the projected span
\(\Span(\pi_{F_s}(\Pow(W)))\), when restricted to column \(j\), has
antisymmetric part lying in the one-dimensional space \(\Span\{A_j(v_j)\}\).
Indeed, rail generators contribute only symmetric terms, and the rung
contributes only multiples of \(A_j(v_j)\).

Now consider the two possible forcing targets in column \(j\):
\[
\delta_{t_j}=E_{t_j}(1,-1),
\qquad
\delta_{b_j}=E_{b_j}(1,-1).
\]
These decompose as
\[
\delta_{t_j}
=
\frac{1}{2}S_j(1,-1) + \frac{1}{2}A_j(1,-1),
\qquad
\delta_{b_j}
=
\frac{1}{2}S_j(1,-1) - \frac{1}{2}A_j(1,-1).
\]
So any forceability of \(t_j\) or \(b_j\) would require the antisymmetric vector
\(A_j(1,-1)\) to lie in \(\Span\{A_j(v_j)\}\). Because \(A_j\) is linear and
injective in the label argument, that would imply
\[
(1,-1)\in \Span\{v_j\}.
\]
Equivalently, \(v_j\) would be a rational multiple of \((1,-1)\). But every
nonzero label in the witness satisfies \(a+b\neq 0\), whereas every nonzero
multiple of \((1,-1)\) has coordinate sum \(0\). So \(v_j\) cannot be parallel
to \((1,-1)\).

This contradiction shows that neither \(t_j\) nor \(b_j\) can lie in
\(\Delta_s\). Since \(s\) was the first layer at which a vertex of column \(j\)
was forced, no such layer exists. Therefore no vertex in column \(j\) can ever
become forced.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No unilateral width-\(2\) strip wave]\label{cor:width2}
No one-sided left-to-right cellular-automaton rule whose fresh column is
seedless can propagate forcing through a full-support \(2\times N\) witness.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In a unilateral fresh-column update, the next column receives information only
from already processed columns and from its own internal edges; by definition it
contains no initial seed atom. Theorem~\ref{thm:width2} therefore applies to
every fresh column individually and forbids its first forced vertex. Without a
first forced vertex, no unilateral wave can propagate.
\end{proof}

\subsection[Computational classification of the audited 2x2 regime]{Computational classification of the audited \(2\times 2\) regime}

\begin{proposition}[Exhaustive \(2\times 2\) rigidity]\label{prop:2x2}
Fix the audited regime consisting of:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item dimensions \(d=(2,2)\);
\item full support on all three edge slots;
\item nonzero palette \(\mathcal{P}=\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,2)\}\);
\item no initial forced vertices; and
\item at most three seed atoms.
\end{enumerate}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item exactly \(44{,}608\) raw witnesses are enumerated;
\item exactly \(48\) witnesses are exact-valid;
\item every exact-valid witness has score \(7/4\); and
\item every exact-valid witness has a two-layer trace of the form
  \(1+3\): one corner in \(\Delta_0\), followed by the remaining three vertices
  in \(\Delta_1\). Each of the four choices of first corner occurs exactly
  \(12\) times.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
There are three edge slots, each of which may take any of the four nonzero
labels in \(\mathcal{P}\), so there are \(4^3=64\) edge-label assignments.

There are \(4\) vertices and \(4\) nonzero labels, hence \(16\) possible
seed atoms. The seed set may have size \(0,1,2,\) or \(3\), and repeated atoms
are disallowed. Therefore the total number of seed sets is
\[
\binom{16}{0}+\binom{16}{1}+\binom{16}{2}+\binom{16}{3}
=
1+16+120+560
=
697.
\]
Algorithm 2 inspects every pair of an edge-label assignment and a seed set, so
the total number of raw witnesses checked is
\[
64\cdot 697 = 44{,}608.
\]
This proves part (1).

The execution of Algorithm 2 is exhaustive, and each witness is evaluated by the
exact verifier of Algorithm 1. The machine-readable summary produced by that run
records:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \tool{exact\_valid\_witnesses} \(=48\),
\item \tool{score\_histogram} \(=\{\texttt{7/4}:48\}\), and
\item \tool{trace\_signature\_histogram}\ consisting of four signatures, each of
  multiplicity \(12\), where the only variation is the identity of the first
  forced corner.
\end{enumerate}
Because the enumeration is complete and the verifier is exact, these summary
values are the theorem statement itself. This proves parts (2), (3), and (4).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No audited \(2\times 2\) witness beats the prompt target]
In the audited regime of Proposition~\ref{prop:2x2}, no witness has score
\(\leq 67/40\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:2x2}, every exact-valid witness in the regime has score
\(7/4\). Since \(7/4 = 1.75 > 1.675 = 67/40\), none reaches the target.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Computational no-go atlas for one-sided fresh columns}

\begin{proposition}[Exhaustive one-sided no-go in heights \(2\) and \(3\)]\label{prop:nogo}
Fix the one-sided local strip class over the palette \(\mathcal{P}\) in which a
fresh \(h\times 1\) column, with \(h\in\{2,3\}\), may use only:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item its internal vertical labels from \(\mathcal{P}\), and
\item row-singleton inputs arriving from the column immediately to its left.
\end{enumerate}
Then the exhaustive verifier-native local atlas has:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \(256\) states when \(h=2\) and \(8{,}192\) states when \(h=3\); and
\item zero strict-improvement states in both heights.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a fresh \(h\times 1\) column, there are \(h-1\) internal vertical edges, so
there are \(4^{h-1}\) choices of internal labels from \(\mathcal{P}\). There are
\(h\) potential row-singleton inputs from the left. For each row, one chooses
whether an input is present and, if it is present, which of the \(4\) labels it
carries. Therefore the total number of one-sided boundary states is \(2^h4^h\).
Hence the total number of local states is
\[
4^{h-1}4^h2^h.
\]
For \(h=2\), this equals \(4^14^22^2 = 256\). For \(h=3\), this equals
\(4^24^32^3 = 8{,}192\). This proves part (1).

Algorithm 3 enumerates exactly those local states and runs the exact verifier on
the corresponding \(h\times 1\) witness in each case. The resulting summary JSON
records \tool{strict\_improvement\_cases} \(=0\) for both \(h=2\) and \(h=3\).
Because the state spaces are finite and exhaustively enumerated, those counts
prove part (2).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What remains open after the local audits]
Any cellular-automaton witness family that beats the score target over the
audited palette must leave the unilateral one-sided strip class of heights \(2\)
and \(3\). In particular, it must use at least one of:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item two-sided or multi-boundary coupling,
\item internal seeds inside a target block, or
\item a wider block or macrocell construction.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:width2}, a unilateral seedless width-\(2\) wave is
impossible. By Proposition~\ref{prop:nogo}, the audited one-sided fresh-column
classes in heights \(2\) and \(3\) contain zero strict-improvement states.
Therefore any successful family over the audited palette must leave those ruled
out classes. The only remaining possibilities are the listed alternatives.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Proof boundary]
The theorem and propositions above are exact for the verifier-native semantics
implemented by the repository. We do not prove here a full metatheorem equating
that executable semantics with every formulation of the original forcing-pair
definition. All positive and negative claims in this paper should therefore be
read at the level of the repository's exact verifier.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What has been solved and what has not}

The repository has \emph{not} solved the prompt target. It has not produced a
witness with score \(\leq 67/40\), and it has not established a CA advantage over
matched non-CA baselines. Those claims remain open because the current larger
search artifacts record only negative frontier results, not a complete
benchmark-and-control block.

What has been solved is narrower but real:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item the verifier exists and is exact at the level of the repository's
  quotient-span semantics;
\item the audited \(2\times 2\) micro-regime is now completely classified;
\item a genuine theorem blocks seedless unilateral width-\(2\) propagation; and
\item exhaustive finite atlases rule out one-sided fresh-column ignition in the
  first two nontrivial heights over the audited palette.
\end{enumerate}

That is enough to replace vague CA optimism with a sharply narrowed search
frontier.

\subsection{Limitations}

Three limitations matter.

\textbf{First,} the paper is exact only relative to the repository's executable
verifier semantics. As noted after Corollary~\ref{cor:width2}, we do not prove
here a full equivalence theorem between that rational-span implementation and
every formulation of the original forcing-pair definition.

\textbf{Second,} the larger search artifacts are weaker than the finite audits.
They preserve family-level maxima but not full candidate-level distributions,
random seeds, or matched-baseline comparisons. Consequently, they support
negative frontier statements but not quantitative CA-superiority claims.

\textbf{Third,} the repository still contains stale blocker-era files that speak
as if the exact verifier were missing. This manuscript resolves that conflict by
treating the 2026-03-19 exact-trace artifacts and executables as source of
truth, and by relegating the earlier blocker files to historical status and
pre-registered control plans.

\subsection{Failure modes and open questions}

The present exact results clarify, rather than remove, the main open questions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Can the rigid \(2\times 2\) nucleus be coupled?}
  The micro-gadget bank exists, but every audited element has score \(7/4\) and
  the same two-layer trace skeleton. The open problem is whether coupled slabs
  can reuse that nucleus without paying its seed cost everywhere.
\item \textbf{Does the local obstruction extend beyond heights \(2\) and \(3\)?}
  The exhaustive atlas stops at the first two nontrivial heights. Wider
  one-sided classes or genuinely two-sided local updates remain open.
\item \textbf{Can macrocell structure defeat the obstruction honestly?}
  A typed macrocell might import the missing antisymmetric content through a
  coupled interior rather than a unilateral boundary. No exact macrocell family
  has yet been verified.
\item \textbf{Can the search side catch up with the verifier side?}
  The exact verifier is now ahead of the search loop. The next scientifically
  correct step is a benchmark-complete exact comparison block with decoder-matched
  baselines, not another round of uncontrolled exploration.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why the negative result still matters}

Negative exact results are often more valuable than positive heuristic signals
when the search object is rigid and the evaluator is unforgiving. Here the
verifier removes an enormous space of plausible but false stories: generic
strip-wave intuition, geometry-only activation narratives, and proxy-heavy local
dynamics. The remaining open directions are fewer, more expensive, and much
clearer mathematically.

\subsection{Societal implications}

The direct societal implications are limited because the domain is pure
mathematics. The broader methodological implication concerns AI-assisted research
practice. Once a faithful evaluator exists, exact negative results can be more
scientifically valuable than visually rich but unverifiable search behavior.
This repository's strongest procedural lesson is that local AI search should be
judged by the strictest exact artifact in the loop, not by the most appealing
intermediate visualization.

\section{Conclusion}

The repository set out to ``solve this using cellular automata'' for a
prompt-defined arithmetic-Kakeya witness target with score at most \(1.675\).
The current exact answer is not a solved witness. It is a narrower and more
useful piece of mathematics:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item an implemented exact rational-span verifier and synchronous trace engine;
\item an exhaustive classification of the audited \(2\times 2\) regime;
\item a proved width-\(2\) seedless-column obstruction;
\item an exhaustive no-go atlas for one-sided fresh-column propagation in heights
  \(2\) and \(3\); and
\item a negative larger-family frontier that leaves only coupled or macrocell
  CA stories alive.
\end{enumerate}

This is not a theorem about \(\AK(1)\), and it is not an empirical CA victory.
It is an exact narrowing of the search space. In a difficult open-problem
setting, that is the correct and scientifically honest contribution.

\section{Implementation Details and Sensitivity}

The exhaustive finite audits have no hyperparameters in the usual machine-learning
sense. Their only inputs are the audited palette, geometry, and seed-budget
limits, all of which are stated explicitly in Section~6. The larger search
frontier does involve design choices, but the stored artifacts expose only the
family maxima rather than a full hyperparameter sweep. We therefore do not
fabricate a sensitivity appendix. The scientifically correct replacement is to
state the next exact sensitivity variables:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item geometry \((2\times N)\), \((3\times N)\), and wider blocks;
\item seed budget and initial \(T\)-budget;
\item audited palette versus held-out legal alphabets;
\item one-sided versus two-sided local interfaces; and
\item decoder-matched baseline family.
\end{enumerate}
