\section{Introduction}

The geometric Kakeya conjecture asks whether every bounded set in $\R^d$ that contains a unit line segment in every direction must have full Hausdorff and Minkowski dimension $d$. Katz and Tao introduced an arithmetic projection formalism strong enough to imply geometric Kakeya lower bounds, and Green and Ruzsa later gave a clean modern account of the arithmetic Kakeya conjecture in that finite-set language \citep{katz1999,green2017}. In the notation used throughout this paper, the conjectural endpoint is $\AK(1)$: finitely many oblique projections should control the anti-diagonal projection with exponent $1$, not merely the trivial exponent $2$. Via Bourgain's slicing argument and subsequent Szemer\'edi-type transfer machinery, progress on $\AK(\alpha)$ yields dimension lower bounds for geometric Kakeya sets \citep{bourgain1999,leng2024}.

The benchmark studied in this repository packages that program into an exact finite search problem. Given the verifier-side forcing formalism from the task statement, one asks for a legal certificate $(X;d_i;f_i;T;R)$ with score
\[
\frac{m(G)+|R|}{n(G)-|T|}\leq 1.675,
\]
where $m(G)$ counts nonzero edge labels, $n(G)$ counts vertices, $R$ is the initial singleton-generator set, and $T$ is the initial solved set \citep{epochai2025arithmetickakeya,archivara_phase1_plan}. The user request that launched the repo was explicit: \emph{solve this using cellular automata}. The present manuscript shows why that slogan has to be rejected, at least for the audited routes saved in the repository.

The difficulty is not only mathematical; it is epistemic. Bootstrap percolation, critical cellular automata, abelian networks, chip-firing systems, and decoder-style local rules all offer natural metaphors for forcing and propagation \citep{bollobas2014,hartarsky2018,bond2013,bond2014,bond2015,sipser1996,hemenway2013,kubica2018}. But in arithmetic Kakeya the source of truth is not a rollout picture. It is the original forcing semantics: a certificate counts only if an exact verifier can derive anti-diagonal singleton relations by legal integer-span operations. The repository therefore treats cellular-automaton language only as a proposal mechanism for candidate certificates, never as evidence in its own right \citep{archivara_prior_art_gap,archivara_benchmark_report}.

At the current repository endpoint, every tested cellular-automaton-positive lane has either failed exactly or collapsed to parity with matched direct search. The Phase 4 corridor pilot already killed the frozen \textsc{H1} route by a one-seed obstruction and found only small direct controls at score $2.0$ \citep{archivara_h1_obstruction,archivara_h1_frontier,archivara_h2}. Phase 6 pushed the negative story further. \textsc{H4} typed residue interfaces yield $0$ forcing width-$4$ words out of $39$ canonical candidates and therefore never reach a nontrivial width-$8$ composition test \citep{archivara_h4_typed_interfaces}. The width-$8$ complexity ledger shows that every matched rerun with fixed budget is empty, so the saved exploratory $29/14$ witness is not part of a valid matched frontier \citep{archivara_complexity_frontier}. The \textsc{H5} shell audit finds no forcing shell word across $117$ canonical checks, while matched direct search on low-height asymmetric families produces exact certificates of score $13/7$, $13/7$, and $14/7$ \citep{archivara_h5_affine_shell}. The \textsc{H6} schedule family, exhaustive controller search, and metastability summaries recover the same best direct object rather than a new mechanism \citep{archivara_h6_defect_transport,archivara_boundary_controller,archivara_metastability}. The honest endpoint is therefore negative: the best surviving exact rows are direct certificates, not cellular-automaton-derived ones.

Our contributions are the following.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formalize the low-height grid specialization used throughout the repository and prove that the exact rational-span verifier is sound and complete for this family. This theorem covers every saved Phase 4 and Phase 6 search row in the paper.
  \item We prove a general one-seed obstruction theorem and apply it to the frozen \textsc{H1} corridor pilot, recovering the repository's exact Phase 4 route kill from first principles \citep{archivara_h1_obstruction,archivara_h1_program}.
  \item We prove a stronger passive-middle row-quotient obstruction theorem for the height-$3$ lift of the typed-interface family. Unlike the one-seed theorem, it allows arbitrary boundary seeds and arbitrary width inside that family \citep{archivara_geometry_lift,archivara_boundary_controller}.
  \item We synthesize the saved Phase 6 route audits: \textsc{H4} interfaces fail before composition, the matched width-$8$ ledger stays empty, \textsc{H5} bounded shell words lose to direct search, and \textsc{H6} schedules, controller search, and metastability show no lift over the direct frontier \citep{archivara_h4_typed_interfaces,archivara_complexity_frontier,archivara_h5_affine_shell,archivara_h6_defect_transport,archivara_boundary_controller,archivara_metastability}.
  \item We identify the actual positive frontier currently present in the repository: direct low-height asymmetric width-$4$ certificates at scores $13/7$, $13/7$, and $14/7$, all still above the target $1.675$ \citep{archivara_h5_affine_shell}.
  \item We position the work honestly relative to prior art: it is an exact benchmark-and-falsification paper inside the existing arithmetic-Kakeya certificate setting, not a new theorem line, not a new formulation, and not a validated cellular-automaton mechanism \citep{green2017,cowenbreen2020,pohoata2024,tao2025,archivara_prior_art_gap,archivara_verification}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Arithmetic Kakeya and Neighboring Theorem Lines}

The closest mathematical line is the arithmetic Kakeya program itself. Katz and Tao introduced the projection framework that motivates the benchmark studied here \citep{katz1999}. Green and Ruzsa gave a modern formulation and several equivalent perspectives on the conjecture \citep{green2017}. Cowen-Breen, Karangozishvili, Varadarajan, and Wang developed related pattern problems inside the same finite-certificate universe \citep{cowenbreen2020}. Pohoata and Zakharov extended the theorem-level landscape with a generalized arithmetic Kakeya statement \citep{pohoata2024}. Tao's analysis of bounded-many-slopes sets and rational complexity is especially relevant because it warns that tiny fixed-$X$ or low-complexity families can look active without meaningfully approaching the real conjectural frontier \citep{tao2025}. Lemm's counterexample line is an additional caution against over-trusting overly symmetric or homogeneous search spaces \citep{lemm2014}.

This paper is not another theorem in that line. It proves no new arithmetic Kakeya exponent, no new implication, and no new equivalent formulation. The mathematical object is always the original verifier-side certificate. The contribution is operational and negative: a collection of exact compiler, verifier, obstruction, and benchmark results that eliminate several cellular-automaton-adjacent routes inside the existing certificate setting \citep{archivara_prior_art_gap,archivara_benchmark_report}.

\subsection{Bootstrap Growth, Abelian Networks, and Decoder Overlap}

The natural nearby languages all come from local propagation. Bootstrap percolation and critical cellular automata suggest droplet, threshold, or interface heuristics \citep{bollobas2014,hartarsky2018}. Abelian networks and chip-firing suggest commutative invariant packages based on rank, torsion, and recurrent-state structure \citep{bond2013,bond2014,bond2015}. Decoder and expander-code ideas suggest local correction, message passing, or sweep-style transport heuristics \citep{sipser1996,hemenway2013,kubica2018}. The repository tested all three types of overlap language, but only in the limited role justified by the verifier. H2 used abelian-style descriptors only as a screening package; H4 and H5 froze local interface and gate alphabets and asked whether those alphabets produced legal exact certificates; H6 treated phase schedules, controllers, and metastability metrics as finite audited proposal banks \citep{archivara_h2,archivara_h4_typed_interfaces,archivara_h5_affine_shell,archivara_h6_defect_transport,archivara_boundary_controller,archivara_metastability}. None of those routes yielded a publication-safe positive mechanism claim.

\subsection{Novelty Boundary and the Corrupted Watchlist}

The repository's novelty audit records an additional complication: early automated watchlists were corrupted by TeX-token overlap such as $\mathbf{P}^{n_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{P}^{n_s}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$, producing false positives in vector bundles, Lie theory, automorphic forms, and topology \citep{archivara_prior_art_watchlist,archivara_prior_art_gap}. Those papers are irrelevant to the arithmetic benchmark and are cited only as evidence that keyword silence is not novelty evidence. The real novelty burden comes from the arithmetic Kakeya line and from the danger of rebranding a failed direct-search regime as a successful local mechanism.

\section{Background and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Arithmetic Kakeya and the Verifier-Side Task}

\begin{definition}[Arithmetic Kakeya exponent]
Let $\alpha\geq 1$. For a finite set $G\subset \Z^2$ and a vector $\mathbf{x}=(x_1,x_2)\in \Z^2$, define
\[
\mathbf{x}\cdot G=\{x_1g_1+x_2g_2:(g_1,g_2)\in G\}.
\]
We say that $\AK(\alpha)$ holds if, for every $\varepsilon>0$, there exists a finite set $X\subset \Z^2$ such that $x_1+x_2\neq 0$ for every nonzero $\mathbf{x}\in X$ and
\[
\lvert (1,-1)\cdot G\rvert \ll_{\varepsilon} \max_{\mathbf{x}\in X}\lvert \mathbf{x}\cdot G\rvert^{\alpha+\varepsilon}
\]
for every finite $G\subset \Z^2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{conjecture}[Arithmetic Kakeya conjecture]
\label{conj:ak}
The endpoint statement $\AK(1)$ holds.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}[Geometric consequence of $\AK(\alpha)$]
\label{thm:kakeya-dimension}
If $\AK(\alpha)$ holds, then every Kakeya set $E\subset \R^d$ has Hausdorff dimension at least
\[
\alpha^{-1}d+1-\alpha^{-1}.
\]
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:kakeya-dimension} is background motivation rather than a result of this paper; it follows from Bourgain's slicing argument together with later progress that upgrades the Hausdorff-dimension conclusion \citep{bourgain1999,leng2024}.

The benchmark studied here uses the verifier-side certificate language supplied by the task.

\begin{definition}[Verifier-side certificate]
Fix a finite label set $X\subset \Z^2$ with $(0,0)\in X$ and no nonzero label on the anti-diagonal line $\langle(1,-1)\rangle$. A verifier-side certificate over $X$ consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item positive integers $d_1,\dots,d_k$ and a vertex set
  \[
  V=[d_1]\times \cdots \times [d_k], \qquad [m]:=\{1,\dots,m\};
  \]
  \item edge-label functions
  \[
  f_i:[d_1]\times\cdots\times[d_{i-1}]\times[d_i-1]\to X
  \]
  for $1\leq i\leq k$;
  \item a set $T\subseteq V$ of initially solved vertices;
  \item a finite set $R$ of singleton-supported functions $r:V\to X$, meaning that each $r\in R$ is nonzero at exactly one vertex of $V$.
\end{enumerate}
For a nonzero value $f_i(a_1,\dots,a_i)=x$, the verifier interprets this as an edge joining the two vertices whose first $i-1$ coordinates equal $(a_1,\dots,a_{i-1})$ and whose $i$th coordinates are $a_i$ and $a_i+1$, with label $x$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Forcing and score]
The verifier repeatedly applies the following operations:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item add the signed two-vertex relation contributed by any graph edge;
  \item if a relation is supported outside the current solved set on exactly one vertex $u$ and has value $(a,-a)$ there with $a\neq 0$, then add $u$ to the solved set;
  \item add any integer linear combination of already available relations.
\end{enumerate}
If all vertices are eventually solved, the certificate is \emph{forcing}. Its score is
\[
S(X,G,R,T)=\frac{m(G)+|R|}{n(G)-|T|},
\]
where $n(G)=|V|$ and $m(G)$ counts the nonzero graph edges induced by the $f_i$.
\end{definition}

The repo benchmark asks for a certificate with score at most $1.675$ \citep{epochai2025arithmetickakeya,archivara_phase1_plan}. No such certificate appears in the saved artifacts.

\subsection{Low-Height Grid Specialization}

All saved search rows in the repository lie in a two-coordinate specialization of the verifier language.

\begin{definition}[Grid instance]
\label{def:grid}
A \emph{grid instance} is a tuple
\[
C=(H,W,X,v,h,R,T)
\]
with the following data.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item integers $H\geq 2$ and $W\geq 1$;
  \item a finite label set $X\subset \Z^2$ as above;
  \item a vertical profile $v=(v_1,\dots,v_{H-1})\in X^{H-1}$;
  \item horizontal row data $h=(h_{r,c})_{1\leq r\leq H,\ 1\leq c\leq W-1}$ with each $h_{r,c}\in X$;
  \item a finite set $R$ of singleton seeds, each specified by a vertex $u\in [H]\times[W]$ and a nonzero label $x\in X$;
  \item an initial solved set $T\subseteq [H]\times[W]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Compiled grid certificate]
\label{def:compiled-grid}
Given a grid instance $C=(H,W,X,v,h,R,T)$, define $d_1=H$ and $d_2=W$. Set
\[
f_1(i)=v_i \qquad (1\leq i\leq H-1),
\]
and
\[
f_2(r,c)=h_{r,c} \qquad (1\leq r\leq H,\ 1\leq c\leq W-1).
\]
Each singleton seed in $R$ becomes a singleton-supported function on $[H]\times [W]$, and the set $T$ is copied verbatim. The resulting verifier-side certificate is the \emph{compiled} certificate associated to $C$.
\end{definition}

This specialization allows different horizontal labels on different rows, but the vertical label between two adjacent rows is constant across columns. The Phase 4 corridor family is the special case $H=2$.

\subsection{Generator Vectors and Target Vectors}

Fix a grid instance $C$ with vertex set $V=[H]\times [W]$ and write $n=HW$. Enumerate $V$ in row-major order:
\[
(1,1),(1,2),\dots,(1,W),(2,1),\dots,(H,W).
\]
Each available verifier relation is identified with a vector in $\Z^{2n}$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a singleton seed at vertex $u$ with label $(a,b)$ contributes $(a,b)$ in the two coordinates of $u$ and zero elsewhere;
  \item an edge relation on vertices $u$ and $w$ with label $(a,b)$ contributes $(a,b)$ at $u$, $(-a,-b)$ at $w$, and zero elsewhere.
\end{itemize}

If $S\subseteq V$ is the current solved set, write $U=V\setminus S$ for the unsolved vertices and let $M_U$ be the matrix whose columns are the current generator vectors projected to the coordinates indexed by $U$. For an unsolved vertex $u\in U$, let $e_u\in \Q^{2|U|}$ denote the anti-diagonal target vector supported only at $u$:
\[
e_u=(0,\dots,0,1,-1,0,\dots,0).
\]
Rule~2 can solve $u$ precisely when a nonzero integer multiple of $e_u$ lies in the integer column span of $M_U$.

\begin{remark}[Scope]
\label{rem:scope}
Every theorem proved below concerns the grid specialization from Definition~\ref{def:grid}. We do not claim a general sound-and-complete verifier theorem for arbitrary constructible graphs beyond this family.
\end{remark}

The paper uses only saved repository artifacts as experimental evidence. No new search row is run for the manuscript. The only new computations performed during writing are figure regeneration and LaTeX compilation. This boundary is deliberate: the verification artifacts explicitly recommend \emph{REVISE} rather than a new experimental pass, provided the paper narrows every claim to the saved evidence \citep{archivara_verification,archivara_benchmark_report}.

\subsection{Exact Verifier}

The verifier used throughout the repo is exact. It never accepts a candidate because of rollout occupancy, closure size alone, or any approximate local statistic. At a given solved set $S$, it asks whether any anti-diagonal target $e_u$ lies in the rational column span of the projected generator matrix $M_U$. If the answer is yes, the corresponding vertex may be solved. Otherwise the certificate fails.

\begin{center}
\fbox{
\begin{minipage}{0.94\textwidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1: Exact verifier for a compiled grid certificate}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Input a compiled grid certificate and initialize the solved set $S\leftarrow T$.
  \item While $S\neq V$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item set $U\leftarrow V\setminus S$ and build the projected matrix $M_U$;
    \item for each $u\in U$, test whether $e_u\in \SpanQ(\mathrm{columns}(M_U))$;
    \item if no such $u$ exists, return failure;
    \item otherwise solve one such $u$ and continue.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item Return the forcing order.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
}
\end{center}

The exact implementation uses rational linear algebra for paper-facing decisions. The repo also contains fast closure heuristics, based on floating-point rank tests or modular filters, but those are used only inside proposal generation. Any exact score, exact hit rate, exact frontier row, or exact forcing claim in this paper is backed by Algorithm~1 rather than by a surrogate \citep{archivara_benchmark_report,archivara_h5_affine_shell}.

\subsection{Greedy Seed Search and Direct Baselines}

Most positive rows in the repository come from fixed geometry plus a direct search over seeds and, in some phases, over edge labels. For a fixed choice of $H$, $W$, $X$, vertical profile $v$, horizontal labels $h$, seed budget, initial-$T$ budget, and boundary band, the repo uses a greedy proposal rule.

\begin{center}
\fbox{
\begin{minipage}{0.94\textwidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 2: Greedy seed search on a fixed grid family}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Enumerate candidate boundary vertices according to the boundary band.
  \item For each allowed initial solved set $T_0$ of the prescribed size:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item initialize the seed set $R_0\leftarrow \varnothing$;
    \item if the current certificate is already forcing, return it;
    \item for each remaining seed slot:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item evaluate every admissible new singleton seed using a fast closure oracle;
      \item keep the seed that maximizes closure size;
      \item if exact verification now succeeds, return the exact forcing certificate.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{enumerate}
  \item If no forcing certificate is found, return failure.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
}
\end{center}

Random direct search wraps Algorithm~2 in an outer loop that samples the edge labels themselves. Deterministic reruns fix the edge pattern and use Algorithm~2 only for seeds and initial solved vertices. In either case, a candidate is recorded only if Algorithm~1 certifies forcing.

\subsection{Route-Specific Audit Protocols}

\paragraph{Phase 4 corridor pilot.}
The Phase 4 corridor pilot froze a same-sum width-$2$ family. The \textsc{H1} representative used a single singleton seed, empty initial solved set, one nonzero top-row label, zero bottom row, and a constant vertical label. \textsc{H2} then computed an abelian-style invariant package on the four family IDs that mattered to the pilot: \textsc{H1} level $1$, \textsc{H1} level $2$, the width-$4$ direct control, and the width-$6$ direct control \citep{archivara_h1_program,archivara_h1_obstruction,archivara_h2}. These rows remain useful because they establish the initial corridor obstruction and a local null result for the imported H2 feature package.

\paragraph{H4 typed residue interfaces.}
The Phase 6 champion route fixes one low-height asymmetric family
\[
X=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(2,-1)\},
\]
one interface alphabet
\[
\{\mathrm{closed},\mathrm{top},\mathrm{bottom},\mathrm{gate\_up},\mathrm{gate\_down}\},
\]
and one exact extractor. A canonicalization quotient identifies words up to horizontal reversal and the row-swap involution top$\leftrightarrow$bottom, gate\_up$\leftrightarrow$gate\_down, leaving $39$ canonical width-$4$ interface words of length $3$. Each word is compiled to an $H=2$, $W=4$ grid, passed through Algorithm~2 with seed budget $4$, initial-$T$ budget $1$, and boundary band $1$, and then checked by Algorithm~1. Level-$2$ composition is allowed only if level-$1$ forcing words exist; composition must use the same alphabet, the same extractor, and no repair pass \citep{archivara_h4_typed_interfaces}.

\paragraph{Complexity-accounted width-$8$ ledger.}
The width-$8$ ledger re-runs one deterministic pattern for each of four family types: same-palette, low-height asymmetric, bounded-slope, and slowly growing $X$. Every row uses the same geometry ($H=2$, $W=8$), the same seed budget $3$, initial-$T$ budget $1$, and boundary band $1$. The ledger records not only score but also $|X|$, coordinate height, grammar length, active state count, extractor complexity, and boundary initialization. The saved exploratory $29/14$ witness is retained only as a legacy row outside that matched ledger \citep{archivara_complexity_frontier}.

\paragraph{H5 affine-core / nonlinear-shell audit.}
This audit keeps the H4 extractor fixed and splits the interface alphabet into an affine core
\[
\{\mathrm{closed},\mathrm{top},\mathrm{bottom}\}
\]
and a nonlinear shell
\[
\{\mathrm{gate\_up},\mathrm{gate\_down}\}.
\]
For each of the three low-height asymmetric families $\mathrm{asym\_a}$, $\mathrm{asym\_b}$, and $\mathrm{asym\_c}$, the repo checks all $39$ canonical shell words on a width-$4$ grid with seed budget $4$, initial-$T$ budget $1$, and boundary band $1$. The matched direct comparator runs $20$ exact trials at each of two seeds for the same family and budgets \citep{archivara_h5_affine_shell}.

\paragraph{Passive-middle geometry lift.}
The height-$3$ lift keeps the H4 extractor but inserts a passive middle row. Horizontal labels are allowed only on the top and bottom rows; the middle row has no horizontal edges. Both vertical layers use the same label $c$. Seeds and initial solved vertices are restricted to the top and bottom boundary rows. The audit checks all $39$ canonical width-$4$ route words for each low-height asymmetric family and compares them to unrestricted direct search with $10$ exact trials at each of two seeds \citep{archivara_geometry_lift}.

\paragraph{H6 defect transport, controller parity, and metastability.}
The H6 family fixes one asymmetric label set and one width-$4$ motif alphabet $\{S,D,P\}$. The periodic word is $\mathrm{SDS}$, the aperiodic word is $\mathrm{SDP}$, and the reversed word is $\mathrm{PDS}$. The route is tested at $H=2$ and $H=3$, with seed budget $3$, initial-$T$ budget $1$, and a thinned-seed robustness check at budget $2$ \citep{archivara_h6_defect_transport}. A separate exhaustive controller solver enumerates all $75{,}276$ programs in the frozen controller bank and compares the best controller certificate to the matched direct archive \citep{archivara_boundary_controller}. Finally, the metastability audit scans all $27$ words in the same bank and records the best closure-time program for each word, together with exact score and a dependency-hypergraph summary \citep{archivara_metastability}.

\subsection{Complexity and Search-Space Accounting}

Let $n=HW$ be the number of vertices and let $g=m+r$ be the number of initial generators in a compiled grid instance. At one verifier stage with $u$ unsolved vertices, the projected matrix $M_U$ has $2u$ rows and $g$ columns. Dense exact elimination on $M_U$ has polynomial cost in $u$, $g$, and coefficient size; a coarse dense bound is $O(u^2g+u^3)$ arithmetic operations per span test. Testing all vertices across all stages therefore yields a worst-case bound polynomial in $n$ and $g$, with the saved repo rows remaining tiny enough that exact auditability is preferable to asymptotic scale.

The route audits are finite and explicit. Table~\ref{tab:setup} records the exact search spaces, trial budgets, and seeds. The critical point is methodological rather than asymptotic: the repo freezes and reports the proposal bank itself. A route cannot claim lift by silently enlarging $X$, adding interface symbols, changing the extractor, or loosening the boundary budget \citep{archivara_benchmark_report,archivara_complexity_frontier}.

\section{Theoretical Results and Proofs}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Compiler and Verifier Correctness}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:compiler}
Let $C=(H,W,X,v,h,R,T)$ be a grid instance. Its compiled object from Definition~\ref{def:compiled-grid} is a legal verifier-side certificate. The compiled graph has
\[
n=HW
\]
vertices and
\[
m=W\sum_{i=1}^{H-1}1_{v_i\neq (0,0)}+\sum_{r=1}^{H}\sum_{c=1}^{W-1}1_{h_{r,c}\neq (0,0)}
\]
nonzero edges.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The compiled certificate has $d_1=H$ and $d_2=W$, so its vertex set is $[H]\times [W]$ and therefore has size $HW$.

We next check legality of the edge-label functions. For $f_1$, the domain is $[H-1]$, and $f_1(i)=v_i\in X$ by definition of a grid instance. For $f_2$, the domain is $[H]\times [W-1]$, and $f_2(r,c)=h_{r,c}\in X$. Hence every nonzero edge label used by the compiled certificate belongs to $X\setminus\{(0,0)\}$, as required.

Now consider the singleton seeds. Every element of $R$ is, by definition of a grid instance, supported on a single vertex $u\in [H]\times [W]$ and takes a nonzero value in $X$. Therefore each compiled seed is a legal singleton-supported verifier function. The initial solved set $T$ is a subset of the vertex set, so it is also legal.

It remains to count nonzero edges. For each inter-row layer $i\in\{1,\dots,H-1\}$ and each column $c\in\{1,\dots,W\}$, the label $v_i$ contributes one vertical edge exactly when $v_i\neq (0,0)$. Summing over columns gives the term
\[
W\sum_{i=1}^{H-1}1_{v_i\neq (0,0)}.
\]
For horizontal edges, each pair $(r,c)$ with $1\leq r\leq H$ and $1\leq c\leq W-1$ contributes one nonzero edge exactly when $h_{r,c}\neq (0,0)$. Summing these indicators gives
\[
\sum_{r=1}^{H}\sum_{c=1}^{W-1}1_{h_{r,c}\neq (0,0)}.
\]
Adding the vertical and horizontal contributions yields the stated formula for $m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:int-vs-rat}
Let $M$ be an integer matrix and let $e$ be a rational vector. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item there exists a nonzero integer $a$ such that $ae\in \SpanZ(\mathrm{columns}(M))$;
  \item $e\in \SpanQ(\mathrm{columns}(M))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Assume (1). Then $ae=Mz$ for some integer vector $z$. Dividing by the nonzero integer $a$ gives
\[
e=M\left(\frac{1}{a}z\right),
\]
so $e$ lies in the rational column span of $M$. Thus (1)$\Rightarrow$(2).

Assume (2). Then $e=Mq$ for some rational vector $q$. Let $D>0$ be a common denominator of the coordinates of $q$. Then $Dq$ is an integer vector, so
\[
De=M(Dq)\in \SpanZ(\mathrm{columns}(M)).
\]
Because $D$ is a nonzero integer, (1) holds with $a=D$. Thus (2)$\Rightarrow$(1).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:single-step}
Fix a compiled grid certificate and a solved set $S$. Let $U=V\setminus S$ and let $M_U$ be the projected generator matrix. For an unsolved vertex $u\in U$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the verifier may solve $u$ by one application of rule~2 at this stage;
  \item $e_u\in \SpanQ(\mathrm{columns}(M_U))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The verifier may solve $u$ exactly when some available relation is supported outside $S$ on the single vertex $u$ and has value $(a,-a)$ there for some nonzero integer $a$. In projected coordinates this means precisely that
\[
ae_u\in \SpanZ(\mathrm{columns}(M_U))
\]
for some nonzero integer $a$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:int-vs-rat}, this is equivalent to
\[
e_u\in \SpanQ(\mathrm{columns}(M_U)).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:verifier}
For the grid specialization from Definition~\ref{def:grid}, Algorithm~1 is sound and complete: it returns a full forcing order if and only if the compiled grid certificate is forcing.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first prove soundness. Suppose Algorithm~1 returns a full order of the vertices. At each step, the algorithm adds a vertex $u$ only after checking that $e_u$ lies in the rational column span of the current projected generator matrix. By Lemma~\ref{lem:single-step}, that condition is equivalent to the existence of a legal rule~2 witness for $u$ at that stage. Therefore every step taken by the algorithm is legal in the verifier semantics. Because the algorithm eventually solves every vertex, the certificate is forcing.

We next prove completeness. Assume that the compiled certificate is forcing. We induct on the number of unsolved vertices.

If all vertices are already solved, the claim is immediate. Assume now that at least one vertex is unsolved and that the theorem has already been proved for every forcing instance with fewer unsolved vertices.

Because the certificate is forcing, there exists at least one legal next rule~2 step from the current solved set $S$. Choose a vertex $u$ that can be solved in such a step. By Lemma~\ref{lem:single-step}, $e_u$ lies in the rational column span of the current projected generator matrix, so Algorithm~1 will include $u$ among its admissible candidates.

Algorithm~1 may choose $u$ or it may choose another admissible candidate $u'$. We claim that solving one admissible vertex cannot destroy the admissibility of another admissible vertex. Indeed, suppose $u'$ is admissible from $S$. Then there exists a relation whose support outside $S$ is exactly $\{u'\}$ and whose value there is anti-diagonal. If we enlarge the solved set from $S$ to $S\cup\{u\}$ with $u\neq u'$, the same relation still has support outside the larger solved set equal to $\{u'\}$, because every coordinate that was allowed to be nonzero on the solved set remains allowed and one additional coordinate has become solved. Therefore $u'$ remains admissible after solving $u$.

Now take any full forcing sequence from $S$. If its first solved vertex is already the vertex chosen by Algorithm~1, remove that step and apply the induction hypothesis to the remaining suffix. Otherwise, if the first vertex in that forcing sequence is some other admissible vertex $u'$, the previous paragraph shows that Algorithm~1's chosen vertex is also admissible at the same stage and that solving it first does not destroy the admissibility of $u'$. Hence we may swap Algorithm~1's chosen vertex to the front of the forcing sequence. After that swap, the remaining unsolved problem has one fewer vertex than before and is still forcing. By the induction hypothesis, Algorithm~1 solves the remainder.

Thus Algorithm~1 returns a full forcing order whenever the compiled certificate is forcing. Together with soundness, this proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Obstruction Theorems}

\begin{theorem}[One-seed obstruction]
\label{thm:one-seed}
Let $X\subset \Z^2$ be finite, and assume that no nonzero element of $X$ lies in $\langle(1,-1)\rangle$. Consider any legal verifier-side certificate with empty initial solved set and exactly one initial singleton seed,
\[
R=\{x u_0\},
\]
where $u_0$ is a vertex and $x\in X\setminus\{(0,0)\}$. Then the certificate is not forcing.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Sigma$ denote the total-label map that sums a relation over all vertices and returns an element of $\Z^2$. We inspect the three verifier operations.

\textbf{Step 1: edge generators have zero total label.}
An edge generator with label $y$ places $y$ at one endpoint and $-y$ at the other endpoint, so its total label is
\[
y+(-y)=0.
\]

\textbf{Step 2: the unique initial seed has total label $x$.}
This is immediate because the seed is supported at a single vertex.

\textbf{Step 3: every generated relation has total label in $\Z x$.}
Initially, the available nonzero relations are the unique seed, of total label $x$, and the edge generators, all of total label $0$. Thus the claim holds at time zero. If two already available relations have total labels $k_1x$ and $k_2x$, then any integer linear combination of them has total label $(a k_1+b k_2)x$ for integers $a,b$. Therefore the claim is preserved by rule~3, and it is obviously preserved by adding more edge generators of total label $0$.

Now suppose, for contradiction, that the certificate is forcing. Because the initial solved set is empty, some first rule~2 step must eventually occur. At that first rule~2 step, there exists a generated relation whose support is a single vertex and whose value there is $(a,-a)$ with $a\neq 0$. Since no vertex has yet been solved, the total label of that relation is exactly $(a,-a)$. By Step~3, this total label must lie in $\Z x$, so there exists $k\in \Z$ such that
\[
kx=(a,-a).
\]
If $k=0$, then $(a,-a)=(0,0)$, contradicting $a\neq 0$. If $k\neq 0$, then
\[
x=\frac{a}{k}(1,-1),
\]
so $x\in \langle(1,-1)\rangle$, contradicting the hypothesis on $X$.

Therefore no first rule~2 step exists. The solved set can never grow from the empty set, so the certificate is not forcing.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:h1}
Every frozen \textup{\textsc{H1}} pilot instance in the repository fails exact verification before scoring.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The saved obstruction artifact records that every frozen \textsc{H1} level has exactly one singleton seed and empty initial solved set \citep{archivara_h1_obstruction}. The active same-sum palette contains no nonzero anti-diagonal label, so Theorem~\ref{thm:one-seed} applies directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Passive-middle row-quotient obstruction]
\label{thm:row-quotient}
Fix a nonzero vector $c\in \Z^2$ with $c\notin \langle(1,-1)\rangle$. Let $W\geq 1$, and consider a height-$3$ grid instance with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the vertical profile is $(c,c)$;
  \item every horizontal label on the middle row is $(0,0)$;
  \item every singleton seed and every initially solved vertex lies on the top or bottom row.
\end{enumerate}
Then the compiled certificate is not forcing.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each column $j\in\{1,\dots,W\}$, let $m_j=(2,j)$ be the middle-row vertex in that column, and let $\pi_j$ be the projection map that extracts the two coordinates of a relation at the vertex $m_j$.

We first prove that every available relation has middle-row projection in the rank-one lattice $\Z c$ at each column.

\textbf{Singleton seeds.}
By hypothesis, singleton seeds are supported only on the top or bottom row. Therefore $\pi_j$ sends every singleton seed to $0$, which lies in $\Z c$.

\textbf{Horizontal edge generators.}
A horizontal edge on the top or bottom row does not touch $m_j$, so its $\pi_j$-projection is $0\in \Z c$. A horizontal edge on the middle row has label $(0,0)$ by assumption, so its generator is the zero relation and again has $\pi_j$-projection $0\in \Z c$.

\textbf{Vertical edge generators.}
At column $j$, the upper vertical edge contributes either $c$ or $-c$ at $m_j$, and the lower vertical edge contributes either $c$ or $-c$ at $m_j$. Hence the $\pi_j$-projection of every nonzero vertical edge generator lies in $\Z c$.

Because rule~1 only adds edge generators of the three types above and rule~3 only takes integer linear combinations, it follows that every relation ever generated satisfies
\[
\pi_j(r)\in \Z c
\]
for every column $j$.

Now suppose, toward contradiction, that the certificate is forcing. Since no middle-row vertex lies in the initial solved set, there must be a first time at which some middle-row vertex is solved by rule~2. Let that vertex be $m_j$. At that moment, rule~2 provides a relation $r$ whose support outside the already solved vertices is exactly $\{m_j\}$ and whose value at $m_j$ is $(a,-a)$ with $a\neq 0$. In particular,
\[
\pi_j(r)=(a,-a).
\]
But the previous paragraph shows that $\pi_j(r)\in \Z c$, so there exists $k\in \Z$ with
\[
k c=(a,-a).
\]
If $k=0$, then $(a,-a)=(0,0)$, contradicting $a\neq 0$. If $k\neq 0$, then
\[
c=\frac{a}{k}(1,-1),
\]
which means that $c\in \langle(1,-1)\rangle$, contradicting the hypothesis.

Therefore no middle-row vertex can ever be solved. Since the initial solved set contains no middle-row vertex and a forcing certificate must eventually solve every vertex, the compiled certificate is not forcing.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:h3}
The passive-middle $H=3$ lift audited in the repository is impossible on the families $\mathrm{asym\_a}$, $\mathrm{asym\_b}$, and $\mathrm{asym\_c}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the saved geometry-lift audit, the three vertical labels are $c=(1,1)$, $c=(2,-1)$, and $c=(1,2)$, whose coordinate sums are $2$, $1$, and $3$ respectively \citep{archivara_geometry_lift}. Hence none lies in $\langle(1,-1)\rangle$. The audit also fixes zero horizontal labels on the middle row and restricts seeds and initial solved vertices to the top and bottom boundary rows. Therefore Theorem~\ref{thm:row-quotient} applies to each family.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Typed Interfaces Fail Before Composition, and the Width-\texorpdfstring{$8$}{8} Ledger Stays Empty}

The H4 interface audit is the cleanest saved test of whether a finite cellular-automaton interface alphabet carries real arithmetic content. It does not. The route checks $39$ canonical width-$4$ words and finds zero forcing words, so the level-$2$ composition space is empty by definition. The matched direct width-$8$ search on the same family and budgets also finds no witness in $60$ exact trials \citep{archivara_h4_typed_interfaces}. Figure~\ref{fig:route-audits}a reports the H4 hit rate together with its Wilson interval.

The complexity-accounted width-$8$ ledger sharpens the same negative story. All four matched reruns---same palette, low-height asymmetric, bounded slope, and slowly growing $X$---remain empty. The only score-bearing width-$8$ row is the saved exploratory three-label witness at $29/14$. Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}b therefore matters as much as Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}a: score-only ranking would suggest width-$8$ progress, but the matched ledger says otherwise \citep{archivara_complexity_frontier}.

\subsection{The Bounded Shell Audit Loses to Direct Search}

The H5 audit turns the local-mechanism question into a finite competition: can a frozen two-gate shell library beat either the affine core or unrestricted direct search under the same extractor and the same budgets? Across the three low-height asymmetric families, the answer is no. The shell audit checks $39$ canonical words per family, hence $117$ in total, and finds zero forcing shell words. The matched direct comparator finds forcing certificates on all three families, with hit rate $0.2$ over $40$ exact trials in each case and best scores $13/7$, $13/7$, and $14/7$ \citep{archivara_h5_affine_shell}.

The direct frontier is visibly boundary-loaded, but it is exact and legal, which the shell route is not.

\subsection{The Passive-Middle Geometry Lift Is Killed by a Row Quotient}

Theorem~\ref{thm:row-quotient} predicts that the passive-middle height-$3$ lift cannot force, regardless of width, as long as the middle row stays horizontal-zero and the vertical label $c$ is not anti-diagonal. The Phase 6 geometry-lift artifact confirms that prediction on all three low-height asymmetric families: each family checks $39$ route words and finds zero forcing words, while the matched unrestricted direct comparator also records hit rate $0.0000$ over $20$ exact trials \citep{archivara_geometry_lift}. Figure~\ref{fig:obstruction}b is therefore both a proof diagram and a faithful picture of the saved route family.

This obstruction is formally stronger than the one-seed theorem. The one-seed argument rules out a specific seed pattern. The row-quotient argument rules out an entire higher-geometry family while allowing arbitrary boundary seeds and arbitrary width inside that family. In the current repository, it is the strongest theorem-level negative contribution.

\subsection{H6 Schedules, Exhaustive Controllers, and Metastability Show No Lift}

The H6 schedule family produces exactly one apparent positive row: the aperiodic word $\mathrm{SDP}$ at height $2$ forces with score $13/7$. But the saved defect-transport audit immediately removes the mechanism-level interpretation. The periodic word $\mathrm{SDS}$ does not force, the same $\mathrm{SDP}$ word fails under seed thinning and reversal, and the height-$3$ lift does not force at all \citep{archivara_h6_defect_transport}. Moreover, the saved direct archive already contains the exact same $13/7$ certificate.

The controller audit reinforces the same point. Exhaustive enumeration of $75{,}276$ exact controller programs yields $48$ forcing programs and identifies $\mathrm{SDP}$ as the best word, again with score $13/7$ \citep{archivara_boundary_controller}. This is not a new frontier. It is parity with the matched direct row.

Finally, the metastability scan shows that closure-time and dependency-hypergraph summaries do not identify any better certificate than the direct frontier. Among the $27$ words in the frozen motif bank, the equal-best score $13/7$ is achieved by four words,

and the direct representative $\mathrm{SDP}$ is already among them. Many high-closure failures remain nonforcing \citep{archivara_metastability}. Figure~\ref{fig:route-audits} summarizes all three facts.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the Repository Actually Established}

At the current endpoint, the strongest scientifically safe identity of the project is the following.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The exact verifier and compiler are understood for the low-height grid family actually used in the repo.
  \item The frozen \textsc{H1} corridor pilot fails by a theorem-level one-seed obstruction.
  \item The passive-middle height-$3$ lift fails by a stronger row-quotient theorem.
  \item Every tested cellular-automaton-positive route beyond those theorems either fails before composition (\textsc{H4}, \textsc{H5}) or collapses to parity with matched direct search (\textsc{H6}, controller bank, metastability summaries).
  \item The best surviving exact certificates are direct low-height asymmetric width-$4$ rows at scores $13/7$ and $14/7$, still above the target $1.675$.
\end{itemize}

That is not the positive headline posed by the original user request. But it is a real contribution. The repo now contains exact obstructions, explicit direct certificates, matched ledger accounting, and a reproducible route audit that prevents dead cellular-automaton narratives from being reintroduced under softer language.

\subsection{Limitations}

The paper is still narrow in four important ways.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every positive row lives in a low-height, low-width grid family with a frozen extractor. Nothing here implies a barrier for arbitrary constructible graphs.
  \item The best saved exact score is $13/7\approx 1.8571$, far above the target $1.675$. The paper is a falsification study, not progress on the benchmark frontier itself.
  \item The matched direct baselines in Phase 6 are much better than the Phase 4 corridor controls, but they are still local to the chosen families. There is no claim that $13/7$ is a natural or stable frontier outside those saved families.
  \item The repo does not preserve hardware logs for the original search runs, so the paper intentionally makes no runtime-efficiency claims.
\end{enumerate}

The saved Phase 4 ablations still matter here. They show that early corridor controls were boundary-sensitive and did not survive naive perturbations of $R$ and $T$ \citep{archivara_ablations}. That fragility motivated the later shell, defect-transport, and controller audits, but it does not by itself explain or refute the later $13/7$ direct rows.

\subsection{Failure Modes and Open Questions}

The route audits identify four concrete failure modes.

\paragraph{Seed bottlenecks.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:one-seed} shows that a unique seed direction with empty initial solved set is fatal. Future constructive routes must evade that obstruction without merely hiding the missing complexity inside the initial solved set.

\paragraph{Interface insufficiency.}
H4 demonstrates that a small frozen interface alphabet can fail before any scale-up comparison begins. This suggests that finite-state interface metaphors are much easier to propose than to justify in the exact arithmetic setting.

\paragraph{Quotient obstructions in higher geometry.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:row-quotient} shows that moving to height $3$ is not enough if the added row is passive. Any future higher-geometry route will have to escape the passive-middle template itself, not merely widen it.

\paragraph{Parity masquerading as mechanism.}
H6, controller enumeration, and metastability all exhibit the same danger: a local schedule or controller can look distinctive while still reproducing the best already-saved direct certificate. This is the precise sense in which the repo warns against mechanism inflation.

The natural open questions follow directly.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Is there any exact certificate family outside the passive-middle regime that genuinely exploits height $>2$ without hiding the work in a larger extractor or a much larger $X$?
  \item Can direct low-height asymmetric search beat $13/7$ while keeping the same level of hidden-complexity accounting used in the Phase 6 ledger?
  \item What is the right exact benchmark protocol for broader constructible-graph grammars, where the convenience of the grid specialization disappears but the same exact verifier logic can still be preserved?
  \item Can one formulate reusable obstruction theorems beyond one-seed and row-quotient arguments, rather than rediscovering them route by route?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Societal and Epistemic Implications}

The direct societal impact of this work is low. Its broader value is methodological. Machine-assisted mathematical research is especially vulnerable to novelty inflation: a vivid metaphor, a finite-state simulator, or an imported vocabulary can look like progress even when the exact arithmetic object has not improved. The present paper is strongest precisely where it resists that inflation. It keeps theorem statements, exact computations, and local heuristic language on separate evidence boundaries. In this problem class, that discipline is not merely stylistic. It is part of the mathematical contribution.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The repository does not solve arithmetic Kakeya using cellular automata. What it does provide is sharper than an anecdotal failure. For the audited low-height grid families, the exact compiler and verifier are understood; the frozen \textsc{H1} corridor pilot is ruled out by a one-seed theorem; the passive-middle height-$3$ lift is ruled out by a stronger row-quotient theorem; the \textsc{H4}, \textsc{H5}, and \textsc{H6} route audits show no improvement over matched direct search; and the width-$8$ complexity ledger explains why the saved exploratory $29/14$ row does not count as matched progress. The best surviving exact rows are direct low-height asymmetric certificates at scores $13/7$ and $14/7$, still above the target $1.675$.

That is a negative result, but it is a reusable one. The paper leaves behind exact theorems, exact measurements, exact certificates, and a benchmark protocol that makes overclaiming much harder. For the present repository state, that is the honest scientific endpoint.

\section{Auxiliary Proof Detail}
\label{app:proofs}

The completeness proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:verifier} uses only one monotonicity fact, which we isolate here for convenience.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:monotone}
Let $S\subseteq S'\subseteq V$, and let $u\in V\setminus S'$. If $u$ is solvable from $S$, then $u$ is solvable from $S'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $u$ is solvable from $S$, then there exists a relation whose support outside $S$ is exactly $\{u\}$ and whose value at $u$ is anti-diagonal. Enlarging the solved set from $S$ to $S'$ can only remove coordinates from the unsolved set, never add them. Therefore the same relation still has support outside $S'$ equal to $\{u\}$ and remains a valid rule~2 witness for $u$.
\end{proof}
