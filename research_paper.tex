\begin{abstract}
The repository studied here targets a difficult arithmetic-Kakeya search task:
find a legal witness \witness{} whose score
\[
\score=\frac{m(G)+|R|}{n(G)-|T|}
\]
is at most \(1.675\). The intended mechanism is a cellular automaton (CA) that
generates witnesses directly in the six-line verifiable representation, without
post hoc repair. We reconstruct the entire audited research program from the
repository state and present it as a publication-quality, negative-result paper.

The core methodological proposal is \Hone{}, a proof-carrying, stage-indexed,
symbolic CA whose registry, edge, and vertex fibers decode directly into
\(X\), the partial dictionaries \(f_i\), the forced set \(T\), and the
singleton-supported seed set \(R\). The design is coupled to a strict no-repair
decoder, matched-baseline plan, label-shuffle control, held-out geometry tests,
and rational-complexity gates, all intended to prevent geometry-only, decoder-
leakage, bounded-slope, or modular-mirage false positives.

The principal empirical result of the repository is negative but informative:
no shared exact integer decoder/verifier exists in the snapshot, so no exact CA
benchmark was admissible. Consequently the tiny-grid sweep executed \(0/3\)
planned \Hone{} families, \(0\) exact decodes, found \(0\) verified witnesses,
and reported no best or median verified score. The honest outcome is therefore
not a solved construction but a rigorously bounded research program and a clear
pivot to verifier recovery. The significance of the work is methodological:
it demonstrates how AI-assisted mathematical search can preserve evidentiary
integrity by refusing to replace an absent exact evaluator with proxy metrics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The arithmetic Kakeya conjecture sits at the interface of additive
combinatorics and geometric measure theory. Following Katz and Tao, one studies
projection inequalities over \(\ZZ^2\) strong enough to imply lower bounds for
Kakeya sets in Euclidean space \citep{katz1999}. Subsequent work sharpened the
arithmetic landscape, clarified adjacent pattern formulations, and highlighted
the risks of low-complexity or bounded-slope regimes
\citep{green2017,cowenbreen2020,pohoata2024,tao2025}. The repository considered
in this paper translates that mathematical target into an exact witness search
problem: find a legal constructible witness \witness{} whose score is at most
\(1.675\), using a cellular automaton rather than a generic graph-search loop.

That translation creates a sharp methodological problem. A CA can easily
produce visually structured states, but the arithmetic-Kakeya witness format is
not visual: it is a constrained symbolic object with exact integer semantics,
closure under subtraction, and a forcing rule that depends on precise singleton
certificates. A naive automaton can therefore appear successful while merely
optimizing geometry, a permissive decoder, or a modular proxy. The repository's
main response was to couple the CA proposal to the exact witness grammar
itself: any candidate had to decode directly into the original six-line witness
format, with no repair and no alternative proxy objective.

The live lane in the repository was \Hone{}, a proof-carrying symbolic
forcing-front CA. It was chosen over sparse-defect, modular-shadow, reversible,
number-conserving, and neural-CA alternatives because it aligned most closely
with the product-grid witness representation and admitted the cleanest kill
criteria. At the same time, the repository's own verification layer repeatedly
warned that this remained a design claim, not a demonstrated capability claim:
no exact integer verifier was present, no witness was validated, and no
matched-budget benchmark block was executed.

The purpose of this paper is therefore not to claim that cellular automata have
already solved the score-\(1.675\) target. Instead, it is to present the
repository's strongest defensible contribution as a scientific object in its own
right: a verifier-coupled CA search architecture, an anti-overclaim evaluation
protocol, the audited evidence that phase-4 experimentation never legitimately
opened, and the resulting decision to pivot toward exact-evaluator recovery.
That is a narrower but still useful contribution because a large fraction of the
novelty risk in AI-assisted mathematics comes from publishing ``interesting''
search behavior that never survives a faithful evaluator.

This manuscript makes four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item It formalizes the repository's exact target, witness representation, and
  \Hone{} architecture as a coherent CA search program over the original
  arithmetic-Kakeya witness object rather than over a proxy pattern task.
\item It proves several system-level correctness statements that the repository
  does support: accepted \Hone{} states decode to legal witnesses, the no-repair
  contract preserves score faithfulness, and the planned controls exclude the
  specific confounders the repository identifies.
\item It reports the audited empirical outcome faithfully: \(0\) exact decodes,
  \(0\) exact verified witnesses, no verified score improvement, and no
  executed control block because the exact integer decoder/verifier was absent.
\item It synthesizes the ConceptEvolve narrowing process into a concrete restart
  agenda centered on exact-verifier recovery, exact-valid micro-gadgets, and
  decoder-matched baselines, while explicitly declining stronger claims that the
  repository does not justify.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Arithmetic-Kakeya mathematics}

The mathematical center of gravity is still Katz and Tao's arithmetic-projection
program \citep{katz1999}. The repository prompt adopts this formulation and the
associated witness-search interpretation. Green and Ruzsa study the arithmetic
Kakeya conjecture directly and sharpen the finite-field boundary, which matters
here because finite-state cellular automata naturally drift toward modular
surrogates \citep{green2017}. Cowen-Breen \emph{et al.}\ show that nearby
pattern problems can look very similar while not solving the exact forcing-pair
problem; that is one reason the repository insists on decoding back to the
original witness format \citep{cowenbreen2020}. Pohoata and Zakharov enlarge the
space of generalized formulations, reinforcing the need to distinguish true
progress on the original witness problem from progress on a neighboring
relaxation \citep{pohoata2024}. Tao's 2025 work is treated in the repository as
the sharpest warning against boundedly many slopes and low rational complexity,
precisely the regime into which a small-alphabet CA could collapse
\citep{tao2025}. Hickman and Wright supply the key modular/ring analogue that
turns finite-state success into a potential mirage unless it honestly lifts back
to \(\ZZ\) \citep{hickman2018}.

Our manuscript therefore does \emph{not} claim any new arithmetic-Kakeya
theorem. The closest mathematical works define the target object and the
objections the repository is trying not to violate. The repository's only safe
mathematical statement is target-faithfulness: it kept the search on the exact
witness representation rather than drifting to a proxy formulation.

\subsection{Cellular automata, abelian networks, and local dynamics}

The CA side of the repository is intentionally narrow. The design borrows the
language of local state, finite alphabets, and lattice updates, but it is not a
claim in classical CA theory by itself. The additive-CA reference point in the
bibliography is Dennunzio, Formenti, and Margara, who analyze positive
expansivity for additive CA over finite abelian groups \citep{dennunzio2023}.
This is relevant because it anchors what ordinary additive-CA structure already
looks like; the repository's intended distinction is not ``we used a CA,'' but
``we used a CA whose state variables are witness-aware and decoder-coupled.''

Bond and Levine's abelian-network papers are treated similarly: they provide a
structural analogy for local processing, halting, and invariant-carrying
dynamics \citep{bond2013,bond2014}. However, the repository never proves that an
abelian-network invariant predicts exact forcing success, so these sources stay
at the level of adjacent method background rather than evidence of progress on
the actual task.

Faldor and Cully's quality-diversity study in Lenia is the relevant CA-search
baseline \citep{faldor2024}. Its importance here is negative: a rich local-rule
search process over an automaton is not novel by itself. The repository's only
defensible methodological distinction is the fixed no-repair decoder and the
fact that all claimed wins would have to survive exact witness scoring.

\subsection{Automated mathematical discovery}

The automated-discovery comparison class is represented by AlphaEvolve and the
large-scale mathematical discovery work of Georgiev \emph{et al.}
\citep{novikov2025,georgiev2025}. These systems make it impossible to claim
novelty for the mere fact that an agent searched a mathematical object. The
repository is explicit about this. Without a domain-specific evaluator contract,
the project would collapse into ``generic search over a serialized witness
space.'' The paper therefore frames its contribution, cautiously, as a
domain-specific exact-decoding discipline rather than as a demonstrated search
capability beyond these general systems.

\section{Background and Preliminaries}

\subsection{Arithmetic-Kakeya target}

The external mathematical target is the arithmetic Kakeya problem of Katz and
Tao \citep{katz1999}. The repository prompt uses the notation \(\AK(\alpha)\)
for the assertion that for every \(\epsilon>0\) there exists a finite
\(X\subset \ZZ^2\) with \(a+b\neq 0\) for each nonzero \((a,b)\in X\) such that
for all finite \(G\subset \ZZ^2\),
\[
|(1,-1)\cdot G|
\ll_\epsilon
\max_{\mathbf{x}\in X} |\mathbf{x}\cdot G|^{\alpha+\epsilon},
\qquad
\mathbf{x}\cdot G=\{x_1g_1+x_2g_2:(g_1,g_2)\in G\}.
\]
The problem instance driving this repository is narrower and more operational:
construct a legal witness \witness{} with score at most \(1.675\). The
score-\(1.675\) threshold is task provenance supplied by the repository prompt,
not a result established inside the repository itself.

The geometric motivation remains classical. Bourgain's slicing method connects
arithmetic-Kakeya bounds to dimension bounds for Euclidean Kakeya sets
\citep{bourgain1999}; the prompt further cites recent progress of Leng, Sah, and
Sawhney as part of the modern motivation for the Hausdorff-dimension upgrade
\citep{leng2024}. This paper relies on that literature only for motivation, not
for any new proof.

\subsection{Verifiable witness format}

The repository ultimately works with the explicit six-line witness format
described in \tool{results/baselines/witness\_spec.md}. We now formalize that
format independently.

\begin{definition}[Product-grid vertex set]
Let \(k\geq 1\) and let \(d=(d_1,\dots,d_k)\) be a list of positive integers.
Define
\[
\Vd=\{(e_1,\dots,e_k)\in \ZZ^k : 1\leq e_i\leq d_i \text{ for every } i\}.
\]
Its cardinality is
\[
n(G)=|\Vd|=\prod_{i=1}^k d_i.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Constructible graph in verifiable form]
Let \(X\subset \ZZ^2\) be finite and contain \((0,0)\). A verifiable
\(X\)-constructible graph is specified by \(d=(d_1,\dots,d_k)\) together with
partial functions
\[
f_i:[d_1]\times\cdots\times[d_{i-1}]\times[d_i-1]\to X,
\qquad 1\leq i\leq k,
\]
where omitted keys are interpreted as \((0,0)\). The edge count is
\[
m(G)=\sum_{i=1}^k \sum_{\mathbf{a}} 1_{f_i(\mathbf{a})\neq (0,0)}
\prod_{j=i+1}^k d_j.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Initial forcing data]
An \(X\)-set for \(G\) is a pair \((R,T)\) where \(T\subseteq \Vd\) and
\(R\) is a finite list of partial maps \(r:\Vd\to X\) such that every
\(r\in R\) has exactly one vertex \(e\in \Vd\) with \(r(e)\neq (0,0)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Forcing closure]
Starting from \((R,T)\), one may repeatedly perform the following operations.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item If \(f_i(\mathbf{a})=x\neq (0,0)\) and \(e_1,e_2\in \Vd\) are the two
  vertices whose first \(i\) coordinates are \((a_1,\dots,a_i)\) and
  \((a_1,\dots,a_i+1)\), respectively, then add the two-support map sending
  \(e_1\mapsto x\), \(e_2\mapsto -x\), and all other vertices to \((0,0)\).
\item If there exist \(a\neq 0\) and \(e\in \Vd\) such that some
  \(r\in R\) satisfies \(r(e)=(a,-a)\) and \(r(g)=(0,0)\) for every
  \(g\notin T\cup\{e\}\), then add \(e\) to \(T\).
\item If \(r,s\in R\), then add \(r-s\) to \(R\).
\end{enumerate}
The pair \((R,T)\) is \emph{forcing} if repeated use of these operations
eventually yields \(T=\Vd\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Witness score]
For a legal witness \witness{}, define
\[
\score=\frac{m(G)+|R|}{n(G)-|T|},
\]
provided that the denominator is positive.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The \Hone{} representation}

The active CA lane uses a stage-indexed representation instead of a flat image
lattice. That choice is central, so we formalize it.

\begin{definition}[\Hone{} candidate state]
Fix a geometry \(d=(d_1,\dots,d_k)\). An \Hone{} candidate state \(C\) consists
of the following typed fibers.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item A registry fiber storing a finite active label set
  \(X_{\mathrm{act}}\subset \ZZ^2\) with \((0,0)\in X_{\mathrm{act}}\).
\item For each stage \(i\), an edge fiber whose cells are indexed by legal
  prefixes in \([d_1]\times\cdots\times[d_{i-1}]\times[d_i-1]\) and store an
  \(\mathrm{edge\_label}\in X_{\mathrm{act}}\cup\{(0,0)\}\).
\item A vertex fiber indexed by \(\Vd\), each cell storing four symbolic fields:
  \(\mathrm{seed\_label}\), \(\mathrm{forced\_bit}\),
  \(\mathrm{proof\_tag}\), and \(\mathrm{mask\_tag}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[No-repair decoder]
The decoder \(D\) maps an \Hone{} candidate \(C\) to a witness
\((X,d,f,T,R)\) by reading the registry fiber as \(X\), the edge fibers as the
partial functions \(f_i\), the forced bits as \(T\), and the nonempty
\(\mathrm{seed\_label}\) fields as singleton-supported seed maps in \(R\).
The decoder \emph{rejects} rather than repairs any candidate that violates the
legality rules of the witness format.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Evaluation metrics and gates}

If an exact verifier existed, the repository would report the following metrics
for each family and baseline: legality hit rate, forcing hit rate, best
verified score, median verified score, and full score distributions. The same
artifacts also fix mandatory kill gates:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item label-shuffle collapse at fixed geometry,
\item decoder-matched baselines,
\item held-out geometry and held-out alphabet checks,
\item small/medium/unrestricted rational-complexity sweeps, and
\item a hard stop if any decoder repair step is needed.
\end{enumerate}
These gates are not empirical results in the current repository; they are part
of the protocol that any future empirical claim would need to survive.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Repository-first workflow}

The repository contains two executable helpers:
\tool{.archivara/concept\_evolve.py} and \tool{.archivara/semantic\_scholar.py}.
There is no exact witness verifier and no separate CA experiment runner. The
workflow reconstructed from the artifacts is therefore:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item synchronize literature, prior-art, and novelty constraints;
\item formalize the witness specification and benchmark matrix;
\item choose a single active lane (\Hone{});
\item run ConceptEvolve passes to refine the design vocabulary and bridge set;
\item attempt phase-4 exact evaluation only if a shared exact decoder/verifier
  exists;
\item if the evaluator is absent, stop, preserve the negative result, and pivot
  to verifier recovery.
\end{enumerate}

This workflow is conservative by design. The most important methodological
decision is what it \emph{refuses} to do: it refuses to replace the absent exact
verifier with proxy metrics such as local propagation speed, automaton entropy,
current imbalance, or modular performance.

\subsection{Algorithm 1: no-repair decoding}

Algorithm~1 is the exact witness compiler implied by the repository's \Hone{}
design notes. It is a decoder specification, not an implemented search loop.

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.95\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1.} No-repair decoding of an \Hone{} candidate \(C\)
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Read the active registry labels and form \(X:=X_{\mathrm{act}}\cup\{(0,0)\}\).
\item If any nonzero \((a,b)\in X\) satisfies \(a+b=0\), reject \(C\).
\item For each stage \(i\) and each legal prefix key, read the edge fiber cell.
  If the stored label is nonzero, insert that key-value pair into \(f_i\).
\item Set \(T:=\{e\in \Vd : \mathrm{forced\_bit}(e)=1\}\). If any marked
  vertex lies outside \(\Vd\), reject \(C\).
\item For each vertex \(e\in \Vd\) with \(\mathrm{seed\_label}(e)\neq\mathrm{none}\),
  create a partial map \(r_e\) supported only at \(e\), with value
  \(\mathrm{seed\_label}(e)\). If any \(r_e\) has more than one nonzero support
  or uses a label outside \(X\), reject \(C\).
\item Compute \(m(G)\), \(n(G)\), \(|R|\), and \(|T|\) from the decoded data. If
  \(n(G)-|T|\leq 0\), reject \(C\).
\item Output the witness \((X,d,f_1,\dots,f_k,T,R)\), where \(R=\{r_e\}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

The design rationale is straightforward. Step 1 prevents the decoder from
inventing labels that the CA never represented. Step 2 enforces the arithmetic
task's nonzero-label legality constraint. Steps 3--5 make the decoder a direct
readout of fiber contents rather than a graph-reconstruction heuristic. Step 6
ensures that score accounting uses the official denominator. None of these steps
performs legalization, support merging, or inference.

\subsection{Algorithm 2: evaluator-coupled benchmark block}

The repository also specifies how any future exact experiment must be run.

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.95\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 2.} Benchmark block for one CA family \(F\)
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Check whether a shared exact integer decoder/verifier exists. If not,
  record the block as \emph{blocked} and terminate.
\item Fix a legal geometry block, an \(X\) budget (or explicit \(X\)), a density
  band, and an exact-decode budget \(N\).
\item Sample at most \(N\) candidates from the CA family \(F\). Decode each
  candidate using Algorithm~1 and then apply exact forcing verification over
  \(\ZZ\).
\item Repeat Step 3 for three matched baselines: random local search,
  whole-witness mutation, and decoder-matched non-CA search.
\item Report legality hit rate, forcing hit rate, best verified score, median
  verified score, score distribution, and failure-reason counts for every
  family.
\item Apply the mandatory controls: label shuffling, held-out geometry,
  held-out legal alphabets, and the small/medium/unrestricted complexity sweep.
\item Promote \(F\) only if its exact verified score distribution improves over
  the matched baselines \emph{and} the signal collapses under label shuffling
  while surviving the out-of-distribution and complexity gates.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

The key methodological point is that the CA never gets special treatment. The
same decoder, the same evaluator, the same budget, and the same edge-density
band are shared across all baselines.

\subsection{Algorithm 3: ConceptEvolve narrowing}

The repository did execute a ConceptEvolve program, but that program produced
bridge ideas rather than exact witness evaluations. The audited sequence was:
\tool{evolve} (completed), \tool{probe} (failed because of a stream
disconnect), \tool{reframe} (completed), and \tool{iterate} (completed).
Operationally, the pipeline did the following:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item generate a broad concept tree around CA-based witness search;
\item probe one bottleneck around proof-carrying decodability;
\item reframe the problem through additional domains such as proof labeling,
  group testing, Petri-net coverability, and network coding;
\item iterate the bridge set and explicitly promote, hold, or retire each
  bridge according to the no-repair and exact-decoder discipline.
\end{enumerate}

This process did not solve the target. Its value is epistemic: it reduced the
number of live hypotheses while keeping the restart agenda aligned with the same
exact-decoder contract.

\subsection{Complexity analysis}

Let
\[
M(d)=\sum_{i=1}^k \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} d_j(d_i-1)
\]
denote the total number of edge-fiber slots, let
\[
N(d)=\prod_{i=1}^k d_i
\]
denote the number of vertex-fiber cells, and let \(q=|X_{\mathrm{act}}|\) be
the number of active labels in the registry fiber. Then Algorithm~1 performs:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item one scan of the registry fiber, costing \(O(q)\);
\item one scan of the edge-fiber slots, costing \(O(M(d))\);
\item one scan of the vertex fiber, costing \(O(N(d))\);
\item score bookkeeping, costing \(O(M(d)+N(d))\).
\end{enumerate}
Therefore decoding and legality checking cost
\[
O\!\bigl(q+M(d)+N(d)\bigr)
\]
time and
\[
O\!\bigl(q+M(d)+N(d)\bigr)
\]
memory. This is the exact part of the pipeline that the repository does define.

If an exact forcing verifier existed, the total cost of one benchmark block with
budget \(B\) would be
\[
O\!\bigl(B\,(q+M(d)+N(d)+V_{\mathrm{exact}})\bigr),
\]
where \(V_{\mathrm{exact}}\) denotes the cost of exact forcing verification.
Because the repository does not contain \(V_{\mathrm{exact}}\), this term is the
dominant unresolved complexity. The explicit benchmark envelope is still finite:
at most three \Hone{} families and at most \(10^3\) exact decodes per family,
with the same budget repeated for each matched baseline. In other words, the
unresolved issue is not asymptotic explosion; it is the absence of a valid
exact evaluator.

\section{Theoretical Results and Proofs}

This section states only claims that are fully supported by the repository's
formal contracts. It does not claim a new arithmetic-Kakeya theorem or a solved
witness.

\begin{definition}[Accepted \Hone{} state]
An \Hone{} candidate state \(C\) is \emph{accepted} if Algorithm~1 terminates
without rejection.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Decoded alphabet legality]
If \(C\) is an accepted \Hone{} state, then the decoded alphabet \(X\) contains
\((0,0)\), and every nonzero \((a,b)\in X\) satisfies \(a+b\neq 0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Algorithm~1 constructs \(X\) in Step 1 as
\(X=X_{\mathrm{act}}\cup\{(0,0)\}\). Therefore \((0,0)\in X\). In Step 2,
Algorithm~1 checks each nonzero \((a,b)\in X\). If there exists a nonzero label
with \(a+b=0\), then the algorithm rejects \(C\). Because \(C\) is accepted,
that rejection does not occur. Hence no nonzero element of \(X\) has coordinate
sum \(0\). These are exactly the required alphabet legality conditions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Decoded graph and seed legality]
If \(C\) is an accepted \Hone{} state, then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item every decoded dictionary entry in every \(f_i\) has a legal key and a
  value in \(X\);
\item every vertex in \(T\) lies in \(\Vd\); and
\item every seed \(r\in R\) has exactly one nonzero support vertex and uses a
  label in \(X\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We prove the three claims in order.

\emph{Claim 1.} By definition of an \Hone{} state, each edge-fiber cell is
indexed by a legal prefix in the domain
\([d_1]\times\cdots\times[d_{i-1}]\times[d_i-1]\). Step 3 of Algorithm~1 reads
only those legal edge-fiber cells. Therefore every inserted key already has the
required shape and range. Each such cell stores either a label in
\(X_{\mathrm{act}}\) or \((0,0)\). By Step 1, \(X_{\mathrm{act}}\subseteq X\),
so every nonzero inserted value belongs to \(X\).

\emph{Claim 2.} Step 4 sets
\[
T=\{e\in \Vd:\mathrm{forced\_bit}(e)=1\}.
\]
Because the comprehension explicitly restricts to \(e\in \Vd\), every accepted
vertex in \(T\) lies in \(\Vd\). The subsequent rejection clause in Step 4 rules
out any out-of-range marked vertex.

\emph{Claim 3.} Step 5 constructs one seed map \(r_e\) for each vertex
\(e\in \Vd\) satisfying \(\mathrm{seed\_label}(e)\neq\mathrm{none}\). By
construction, \(r_e\) is supported only at \(e\). If any seed had more than one
nonzero support or used a label outside \(X\), then Step 5 would reject \(C\).
Since \(C\) is accepted, neither failure occurs. Hence every seed map in \(R\)
has exactly one nonzero support vertex and uses a label in \(X\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Accepted states decode to legal witnesses]
Let \(C\) be an accepted \Hone{} state. Then the decoder output
\[
D(C)=(X,d,f_1,\dots,f_k,T,R)
\]
is a legal six-line arithmetic-Kakeya witness in the sense of the repository's
canonical witness specification.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
To prove legality we must check the conditions on \(X\), on the graph data
\((d,f_1,\dots,f_k)\), on \(T\), on \(R\), and on the score denominator.

\emph{Alphabet.} By Lemma~1, \(X\) contains \((0,0)\), and every nonzero
\((a,b)\in X\) satisfies \(a+b\neq 0\). This matches the alphabet rules.

\emph{Graph data.} The geometry \(d=(d_1,\dots,d_k)\) is fixed by the benchmark
condition and consists of positive integers by the definition of the product
grid. For each \(i\), Algorithm~1 outputs the dictionary \(f_i\) using only
legal stage-\(i\) prefix keys and values in \(X\), by Lemma~2(1). Because the
decoder outputs exactly one dictionary for each stage \(i\in\{1,\dots,k\}\), the
required number of dictionaries is correct.

\emph{Forced set.} Lemma~2(2) proves that every element of \(T\) lies in
\(\Vd\), so \(T\) is a legal list of vertices.

\emph{Seed set.} Lemma~2(3) proves that every \(r\in R\) is a partial map from
\(\Vd\) to \(X\) with exactly one nonzero supported vertex, which is exactly the
required initial condition on \(R\).

\emph{Denominator.} Step 6 of Algorithm~1 computes \(n(G)-|T|\) and rejects the
candidate if this quantity is nonpositive. Because \(C\) is accepted, the
denominator is positive.

All required legality checks have therefore passed, and the output is a legal
six-line witness.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[No-repair score faithfulness]
Let \(C\) be an accepted \Hone{} state and let \(D(C)\) be its decoded witness.
Then the score computed by the pipeline is exactly
\[
\score=\frac{m(G)+|R|}{n(G)-|T|},
\]
where all terms are computed from the decoded witness itself. In particular, the
pipeline cannot improve the score by hidden legalization or by trusting the
human-readable first-line summary.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Algorithm~1 computes \(m(G)\), \(n(G)\), \(|R|\), and \(|T|\) after decoding the
registry, edge, and vertex fibers into \(X\), the functions \(f_i\), the forced
set \(T\), and the seed set \(R\). The score formula used by the repository is
exactly
\[
\score=\frac{m(G)+|R|}{n(G)-|T|}.
\]
No term in this expression refers to the advisory first line of the witness
format. Moreover, by construction of Algorithm~1, there is no repair step that
adds or removes edges, merges seed supports, or modifies \(T\) before score
evaluation. Therefore the score is a pure function of the decoded witness. Since
the pipeline never uses an alternative objective, it cannot obtain a lower score
through hidden legalization or summary-line trust.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Control suite excludes the named confounders]
Assume a future empirical claim about a CA family is accepted only if the family
passes the benchmark contract of Algorithm~2 together with the lane gates fixed
in the repository. Then each of the following confounders is ruled out by the
acceptance rule.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The apparent gain is geometry-only or label-insensitive.
\item The apparent gain comes from decoder leakage or repair heuristics.
\item The apparent gain exists only in the small-complexity regime.
\item The apparent gain is memorization of one geometry or one legal alphabet.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We consider each confounder separately.

\emph{Case 1: geometry-only or label-insensitive gain.}
The benchmark contract requires a label-shuffle control at fixed geometry and
fixed nonzero-label multiset. The lane gate kills \Hone{} if the shuffled runs
retain either at least half of the unshuffled hit rate or at least three
quarters of the unshuffled median-score gain. A geometry-only or label-
insensitive explanation predicts precisely that the gain should survive such a
shuffle, because the geometry is held fixed while the arithmetic labels are
perturbed. Therefore any family that passes acceptance cannot still be explained
by a geometry-only or label-insensitive mechanism above the repository's stated
thresholds.

\emph{Case 2: decoder leakage or repair heuristics.}
There are two ways decoder leakage could occur. First, the decoder could repair
malformed candidates. This is prohibited directly by Algorithm~1 and by the
decoder-leakage gate: any such repair causes immediate rejection or lane death.
Second, the decoder could contain the true source of gain even without explicit
repair. To test that possibility, Algorithm~2 requires a decoder-matched
baseline that keeps the same decoder but replaces the CA generator with non-CA
proposal mechanisms. If the apparent gain were attributable only to the
decoder, the decoder-matched baseline would reproduce it, and the CA-specific
claim would fail. Hence accepted evidence cannot be due solely to decoder
leakage or repair heuristics.

\emph{Case 3: small-complexity-only gain.}
The benchmark contract includes a small/medium/unrestricted rational-complexity
sweep, and the lane gate kills \Hone{} if all improvements occur only in the
small regime while the family fails to beat matched non-CA baselines in the
medium or unrestricted regimes. A gain confined to the smallest-complexity
regime therefore triggers rejection. Hence an accepted claim cannot be
small-complexity-only under the repository's rule.

\emph{Case 4: memorization of one geometry or one legal alphabet.}
The benchmark plan and falsifier memo require held-out geometry checks and
held-out legal alphabets. The out-of-distribution gate kills \Hone{} if the hit
rate collapses below the specified threshold or if the median verified gain
disappears on all held-out settings. Therefore a family that merely memorizes
one geometry or one alphabet cannot satisfy the acceptance rule.

Each confounder leads to a specific mandatory failure. Since accepted evidence
is defined precisely by surviving all mandatory failures, none of the four
confounders is consistent with accepted evidence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Verified metrics are undefined without an exact verifier]
Suppose the repository lacks a shared exact integer decoder/verifier that can
check whether a decoded witness is forcing over \(\ZZ\). Then the quantities
``verified hit rate,'' ``best verified score,'' and ``median verified score'' are
undefined under the repository's protocol.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By definition, a verified hit rate counts the fraction of proposals that decode
to legal witnesses \emph{and} pass exact forcing verification over \(\ZZ\). If
the exact verifier is absent, the second predicate cannot be evaluated. Hence
the verified hit rate cannot be computed.

Similarly, the best verified score is the minimum score among witnesses that
have passed exact verification. Without a verifier, the set over which this
minimum is taken is not known. The same issue applies to the median verified
score: the multiset of verified scores does not exist as a well-defined object.

Because the protocol forbids substituting proxy verification, these quantities
are not replaced by estimates. They remain undefined.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What remains unproven]
The repository does \emph{not} prove the existence of a witness with
\(\score\leq 1.675\). It also does not prove that \Hone{} has any evaluator-
backed capability advantage over matched baselines. Those are precisely the
claims blocked by Proposition~4's missing-verifier condition.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Limitations}

The main limitation is structural and dominates all others: the exact integer
decoder/verifier does not exist in the audited snapshot. As long as that remains
true, the repository cannot support any theorem-level or evaluator-backed
empirical claim. Three secondary limitations follow immediately.

First, the CA proposal is architectural rather than instantiated. The paper can
formalize the typed fibers, the decoder, and the gates, but it cannot report a
particular learned rule family that achieved exact success. Second, the
benchmark matrix is specification-level rather than execution-level. It is
carefully defined, but it remains untested. Third, the bibliographic coverage is
sufficient for this bounded narrative only after adding Bourgain and Leng--Sah--
Sawhney and cleaning several metadata inconsistencies; it would have been
insufficient for a stronger theorem-style manuscript.

\subsection{Failure modes already anticipated by the repository}

The most valuable feature of the repository is arguably its self-falsification
discipline. The design anticipates at least four strong failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Geometry-only success.} A CA might learn a convenient graph shape
  independent of the arithmetic labels. The label-shuffle gate is specifically
  designed to kill that story.
\item \textbf{Decoder leakage.} The true intelligence could sit in a permissive
  decoder rather than in the CA. The no-repair rule and decoder-matched
  baselines target this failure mode directly.
\item \textbf{Bounded-slope trapping.} A small state alphabet might merely
  rediscover low-rational-complexity corners warned about by Tao. The complexity
  sweep exists precisely to detect that collapse.
\item \textbf{Modular mirages.} Finite-state dynamics are naturally modular. The
  repository therefore treats modular success as curriculum only, never as
  evidence for the integer target.
\end{enumerate}

The key point is that these failure modes are not post hoc excuses invented by
this paper. They were already present in the repository's falsifier and
verification artifacts before the manuscript was written.

\subsection{What a scientifically correct restart would do}

The restart agenda is narrow and concrete.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Recover or implement the shared exact integer decoder/verifier from the
  witness specification.
\item Seed a bank of tiny exact-valid micro-gadgets that can act as trustworthy
  building blocks for any CA family.
\item Run one fully matched benchmark block: one \Hone{} family versus random
  local search, whole-witness mutation, and decoder-matched search.
\item Apply the label-shuffle, held-out geometry, held-out alphabet, and
  rational-complexity controls immediately.
\item Re-open only the promoted bridge set, not the retired proxy-heavy lanes.
\end{enumerate}

Anything broader than this would repeat the repository's own warning against
open-ended reframing without evaluator support.

\subsection{Societal implications}

The direct societal implications are limited because this is pure mathematics and
scientific software methodology. The more general implication concerns AI-assisted
research practice. Automated systems can create a large amount of plausible
structure before a faithful evaluator exists. In that setting, the most valuable
behavior may be refusal rather than generation: refusing proxy metrics, refusing
implicit repair, and refusing to report improvement without the evaluator that
defines the task. This repository's main positive lesson is precisely that such
refusal can itself be a substantive scientific output when it prevents a false
claim from entering the literature.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper reconstructs, formalizes, and audits a cellular-automata research
program for an arithmetic-Kakeya witness search problem. The active lane,
\Hone{}, is a proof-carrying, stage-indexed, no-repair CA design that stays on
the exact six-line witness representation. The repository contributes a careful
benchmark contract, a concrete kill-gate suite, and a final bridge triage that
narrows future work to verifier-safe directions.

It does \emph{not} contribute a verified witness with score at most \(1.675\).
The exact empirical outcome is instead \(0\) executed \Hone{} families,
\(0\) exact decodes, \(0\) verified witnesses, and no admissible best or median
verified score because the exact integer verifier was absent. The correct final
decision is therefore the one already recorded in the repository: pivot away
from more CA search and toward exact-verifier recovery.

In that narrower sense, the repository succeeds. It shows how to turn a tempting
but under-evaluated AI-mathematics exploration into a disciplined negative
result with a clean restart agenda. That is not the same as solving arithmetic
Kakeya, but it is a genuine contribution to how such searches should be run.

support such appendices in the ordinary sense because no exact evaluation run
occurred. The scientifically correct replacement is to state what would need to
vary once the verifier exists.

\subsection{Planned hyperparameters once evaluation becomes possible}

If the exact verifier is recovered, the first restart should vary:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item the legal geometry block \(d\),
\item the active alphabet budget \(|X|\),
\item the rational-complexity regime (small, medium, unrestricted),
\item the target edge-density band,
\item the CA family itself, and
\item the baseline proposal distributions under the decoder-matched control.
\end{enumerate}
At present no sensitivity curve over these quantities exists, and none is
fabricated in this paper.

\subsection{Why no empirical appendix is supplied}

An ``additional experiments'' appendix normally presupposes that at least one
primary experiment ran. Here the first exact gate never opened. The absence of
an experimental appendix is therefore a positive integrity statement: it means
the manuscript inherited the repository's refusal to widen claims beyond the
available evidence.
