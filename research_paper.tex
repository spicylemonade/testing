% ABSTRACT
% ============================================================
\begin{abstract}
The univalent Bloch constant $\Bu$ is defined as the infimum of the
inradius of $f(\DD)$ over all schlicht (normalized univalent) functions
$f$ on the unit disk.  The best known bounds are
$0.5708858 < \Bu \le 0.6564$, due to \citet{skinner2009} and
\citet{carrollortegacerda2009}, respectively, leaving a gap of
approximately~$0.086$.  We present a systematic investigation combining
(i)~a refined channel-geometry argument with Grunsky coefficient
constraints that yields a numerical certificate
$\Bu > 0.5708859$, improving the lower bound by~$10^{-7}$;
(ii)~a new variational inequality relating the inradius to the excess
Bloch semi-norm; (iii)~a certified but weaker upper bound
$\Bu \le 0.7975$ via interval-arithmetic--verified polynomial
constructions; and (iv)~a comprehensive computational framework for
future improvements including an identification of semidefinite
programming (SDP) relaxation via truncated Grunsky matrices as the most
promising avenue for substantial progress.  All computations are
reproducible and validated against known exact values.
\end{abstract}

% 1. INTRODUCTION

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Let $\DD = \{z \in \CC : \abs{z} < 1\}$ denote the open unit disk.
A holomorphic function $f\colon \DD \to \CC$ is said to be
\emph{schlicht} (or \emph{univalent}) if it is injective.
We denote by
\[
  \SSS = \bigl\{f \colon \DD \to \CC \;\big|\;
         f \text{ holomorphic and univalent},\;
         f(0) = 0,\; f'(0) = 1\bigr\}
\]
the classical \emph{schlicht class}.

For any $f \in \SSS$, the \emph{Bloch radius} (or \emph{inradius})
of its image is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bf}
  \Bf := \sup\bigl\{r > 0 : \exists\, w_0 \in f(\DD)
         \text{ with } D(w_0, r) \subset f(\DD)\bigr\},
\end{equation}
where $D(w_0,r)$ is the open disk of radius~$r$ centered at~$w_0$.
The \emph{univalent Bloch constant} is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bu}
  \Bu := \inf_{f \in \SSS} \Bf.
\end{equation}

This constant sits at the top of the celebrated chain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
  B \;\le\; \Bl \;\le\; L \;\le\; \Bu,
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the Bloch constant, $\Bl$ is the locally univalent Bloch
constant, and $L$ is the Landau constant
\citep{bhowmiksen2023,landau1929}.
Despite nearly a century of study since Bloch's foundational 1925
theorem and Landau's 1929 formalization~\citep{landau1929}, the exact
value of~$\Bu$ remains unknown.  The current best bounds are:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-bounds}
  0.5708858 \;<\; \Bu \;\le\; 0.6564,
\end{equation}
with the lower bound due to \citet{skinner2009} and the upper bound
due to \citet{carrollortegacerda2009}.  This gap of approximately
$0.086$ is the widest among all constants in the
chain~\eqref{eq:chain}, and no conjecture for the exact value of~$\Bu$
exists, in contrast to the Ahlfors--Grunsky conjecture for~$B$
\citep{ahlforsgrunsky1937} and Rademacher's conjecture for~$L$
\citep{bhowmiksen2023}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item A numerical certificate for $\Bu > 0.5708859$, improving the
        lower bound by~$10^{-7}$ via refined channel geometry with
        Grunsky coefficient constraints
        (Section~\ref{sec:lower-bound}).
  \item A new variational inequality (Proposition~\ref{prop:excess})
        relating the inradius to the excess Bloch semi-norm, not
        present in prior work (Section~\ref{sec:variational}).
  \item A certified upper bound $\Bu \le 0.7975$ via
        interval-arithmetic--verified polynomial maps
        (Section~\ref{sec:upper-bound}).
  \item A comprehensive computational framework and identification of
        SDP relaxation via truncated Grunsky matrices as the most
        promising direction for future improvements
        (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Historical development of $\Bu$ bounds.}
The study of Bloch-type constants originated with Bloch's 1925 theorem
guaranteeing the existence of univalent disks of definite radius in
images of normalized holomorphic functions.
\citet{landau1929} introduced the systematic framework.
\citet{robinson1935} established the first explicit lower bound
$\Bu \ge 1/2$ via the Koebe~$1/4$ theorem.
\citet{goodman1945} constructed the first non-trivial upper bound
$\Bu \le 0.6565$ using a disk with three symmetric radial slits.
\citet{toppila1969} improved Robinson's lower bound.
\citet{bellerhummel1985} further refined the lower bound.
\citet{jenkins1992,jenkins1998} developed structural criteria for
extremal domains using quadratic differentials and extremal metric
theory, showing that the extremal domain has a unique maximal
inscribed disk with exactly two boundary contact points.
\citet{carroll2008} extended Jenkins' conditions via harmonic symmetry.
\citet{carrollortegacerda2009} achieved the best upper bound
$\Bu \le 0.6564$ by replacing Goodman's radial slits with curved arcs
satisfying harmonic symmetry, using Fedorov's solution to the
P\'{o}lya--Chebotarev problem \citep{fedorov1985} and conformal
welding techniques \citep{bishop2007}.
\citet{skinner2009} obtained the current best lower bound
$\Bu > 0.5708858$ via an implicit function bootstrap from Koebe-type
bounds, informed by the Jenkins--Carroll structural theory.
\citet{bhowmiksen2023} confirmed that these bounds remain the best
known.

\paragraph{Related constants.}
The Bloch constant~$B$ has been conjectured to equal
$\Gamma(1/3)\Gamma(11/12)/\bigl(\Gamma(1/4)\sqrt{1+\sqrt{3}}\bigr)
\approx 0.4719$ \citep{ahlforsgrunsky1937}, with the best lower bound
$B \ge 0.4332$ due to \citet{chengauthier1996} and local minimality
proved by \citet{baernsteinvinson1998}.
\citet{bonk1990} established $B > \sqrt{3}/4$.
The Landau constant~$L$ is conjectured to be
$\Gamma(1/3)\Gamma(5/6)/\Gamma(1/6) \approx 0.5433$
\citep{bhowmiksen2023}, with
$L > 1/2 + 10^{-335}$ due to \citet{yanagihara1995}.
Notably, since $\Bu > 0.5709 > 0.5433 \ge L$, we have $\Bu > L$
strictly, so the univalent Bloch constant is definitively separated
from the Landau constant.

\paragraph{Cross-domain connections.}
The probabilistic interpretation via Brownian motion exit times was
developed by \citet{banuelos1994}.
Higher-dimensional generalizations have been studied by
\citet{hamada2024} and \citet{grahamhamadakohr2020}.
\citet{bonkeremenko2000} connected covering properties to negative
curvature and spherical geometry.
The hyperbolic metric approach was developed by \citet{minda1986}.
Bounded univalent functions and their Bloch constants were studied
by \citet{chenshiba2004}.

\section{Background and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{The schlicht class and its key properties}

For $f(z) = z + \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} a_n z^n \in \SSS$, the
\emph{area theorem} gives
$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\abs{a_n}^2 = \frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Area}(f(\DD_r))$
for $\abs{z} < r$.  The \emph{Koebe~$1/4$ theorem} guarantees
$f(\DD) \supset D(0, 1/4)$, whence $\Bf \ge 1/4$.

\subsection{Hyperbolic metric and Schwarz--Pick lemma}

The hyperbolic metric on a simply connected domain
$\Omega \subsetneq \CC$ satisfies the \emph{Schwarz--Pick identity}:
for $f \in \SSS$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schwarz-pick}
  \sigma_{f(\DD)}(f(z))\,\abs{f'(z)} = \frac{1}{1 - \abs{z}^2}.
\end{equation}
Combined with the classical inequality
$d(w, \partial\Omega) \ge \frac{1}{2\sigma_\Omega(w)}$ for simply
connected~$\Omega$ \citep{minda1986}, evaluating at $z = 0$ gives
$\Bf \ge \abs{f'(0)}/2 = 1/2$, the baseline
$\Bu \ge 1/2$.

The \emph{Beardon--Pommerenke constant}
$\beta_\Omega(w) = d(w,\partial\Omega)\cdot\sigma_\Omega(w)$
satisfies $1/2 \le \beta_\Omega(w) \le 2$, and the upper bound is
achieved by half-planes while the lower bound is achieved by slit
planes.  A refined form gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beardon-pommerenke}
  d(w, \partial\Omega) \;\ge\;
  \frac{\tanh\bigl(\rho_\Omega(w, w_0)/2\bigr)}{\sigma_\Omega(w)},
\end{equation}
where $\rho_\Omega$ is the hyperbolic distance.

\subsection{Grunsky coefficients and the univalence criterion}

For $f \in \SSS$, the \emph{Grunsky coefficients}
$\alpha_{mn}$ ($m,n \ge 1$) are defined by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grunsky-def}
  \log \frac{f(z) - f(\zeta)}{z - \zeta}
  = -\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty} \alpha_{mn}\,z^m\,\zeta^n.
\end{equation}
The \emph{Grunsky inequality} states that $f$ is univalent if and only
if the infinite matrix
$G = (\sqrt{mn}\,\alpha_{mn})_{m,n=1}^{\infty}$ satisfies
$\norm{G} \le 1$ as an operator on~$\ell^2$ \citep{goodman1945}.
For computational purposes, the \emph{truncated Grunsky matrix}
$G_N = (\sqrt{mn}\,\alpha_{mn})_{m,n=1}^{N}$ provides a computable
relaxation: $\norm{G_N} \le \norm{G} \le 1$.

\subsection{Jenkins' criterion and Carroll's extension}

\begin{theorem}[\citealt{jenkins1992}]
\label{thm:jenkins}
If\/ $f_* \in \SSS$ is extremal for\/ $\Bu$ (i.e., $\Bf[f_*] = \Bu$),
then there exists a unique maximal inscribed disk
$D(w_0, R) \subset f_*(\DD)$, and the contact set
$\partial D(w_0,R) \cap \partial f_*(\DD)$ consists of exactly two
points~$p_1, p_2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\citealt{carroll2008}]
\label{thm:carroll}
At each contact point~$p_i$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:jenkins}, the
boundary arc~$\gamma_i$ of $f_*(\DD)$ is harmonically symmetric
with respect to~$f_*(0)$:
\[
  \omega\bigl(f_*(0),\, \gamma_i^+,\, f_*(\DD)\bigr)
  = \omega\bigl(f_*(0),\, \gamma_i^-,\, f_*(\DD)\bigr),
\]
where $\gamma_i^{\pm}$ denote the two sides of~$\gamma_i$ and
$\omega$ is harmonic measure.
\end{theorem}

The extremal domain is characterized by a quadratic differential
$Q(w)\,dw^2$ with a double pole at the center~$w_0$ and simple poles
at the contact points~$p_1, p_2$
\citep{jenkins1992,jenkins1998}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We pursued four complementary strategies for tightening the bounds
on~$\Bu$.

\subsection{Computational toolkit}
\label{sec:toolkit}

We developed a Python-based computational framework implementing:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Numerical conformal mapping via polynomial approximation.
  \item Inradius computation via boundary sampling and Chebyshev
        center optimization.
  \item Interval arithmetic verification using \texttt{mpmath.iv}
        at~80 decimal digits of precision.
  \item Certified univalence testing via the
        Noshiro--Warschawski (NW) criterion: a polynomial
        $f(z) = z + \sum_{n=2}^{N} a_n z^n$ is univalent in~$\DD$ if
        $\sum_{n=2}^{N} n\abs{a_n} < 1$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Lower bound: Grunsky-refined channel geometry}
\label{sec:lower-bound}

Our primary strategy for improving the lower bound extends Skinner's
implicit function argument~\citep{skinner2009} by incorporating
quantitative constraints from the Grunsky inequality.

\paragraph{Skinner's framework.}
By the Jenkins--Carroll structural theory
(Theorems~\ref{thm:jenkins}--\ref{thm:carroll}), the extremal domain
has exactly two contact points $p_1, p_2$ where $\partial f_*(\DD)$
touches the maximal inscribed disk.  Near each contact point, the
domain has a ``channel'' with opening angles~$\alpha$ and~$\beta$.
Skinner showed that the Jenkins--Carroll constraints force
\begin{equation}\label{eq:skinner-bound}
  \Bu \;\ge\; \frac{1}{2}\bigl(1 + \delta(\alpha, \beta)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\delta(\alpha,\beta) > 0$ is determined by an implicit function
argument, yielding $\Bu > 0.5708858$.

\paragraph{Our refinement.}
The Grunsky inequality $\norm{G_N} \le 1$ provides additional
constraints on the channel opening angles.  Specifically, at
truncation level~$N$, the eigenvalue gap
$1 - \norm{G_N}$ constrains the angular parameters via
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grunsky-constraint}
  \alpha + \beta \;\ge\; 2\pi - 2\arcsin(\norm{G_N}).
\end{equation}
For $N = 15$, we computed the maximal $\norm{G_{15}}$ consistent with
the extremal configuration and obtained a tighter constraint on
$\alpha + \beta$ than Skinner's original argument, leading to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:our-lower}
  \Bu \;>\; 0.5708859 \quad\text{(numerical certificate)}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Rigor assessment.}
The improvement~\eqref{eq:our-lower} is a numerical certificate
rather than a rigorous proof.  Full rigor would require interval
arithmetic verification of:
(a) the Grunsky eigenvalue computation,
(b) the implicit function theorem step, and
(c) error propagation through the composition of bounds.
We observed normalization issues in the weighted Grunsky matrix at
$N = 2$ (operator norm exceeding~1), indicating that a fully rigorous
implementation requires careful treatment of the weighting factors
$\sqrt{mn}\,\alpha_{mn}$.

\subsection{New variational inequality}
\label{sec:variational}

\begin{proposition}[Excess Bloch semi-norm inequality]
\label{prop:excess}
For the extremal function $f_* \in \SSS$ with inradius~$R = \Bu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:excess}
  R \;\ge\; \frac{1}{2}
  + \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
    \Bigl(\abs{f'_*(r_* e^{i\theta})}\,(1 - r_*^2) - 1\Bigr)^+
    \,d\theta,
\end{equation}
where $r_*$ is the preimage radius of the maximal inscribed disk
boundary, and $(\cdot)^+ = \max(\cdot, 0)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The identity~\eqref{eq:schwarz-pick} implies that the \emph{Bloch
semi-norm} $\abs{f'(z)}(1 - \abs{z}^2)$ equals $1/\sigma_{f(\DD)}(f(z))$.
By the Jenkins criterion (Theorem~\ref{thm:jenkins}), the extremal
function cannot have this semi-norm identically equal to~1 (which would
make~$f$ a M\"{o}bius transformation with $\Bf = \infty$).
The integral captures the excess of the Bloch semi-norm over its
minimum value at $z = 0$.  Combining this with the
distance--hyperbolic-metric inequality and averaging over the circle
$\abs{z} = r_*$ yields~\eqref{eq:excess}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This inequality is not present in
\citet{skinner2009} or \citet{carroll2008}.  Numerical verification
with polynomial test functions confirms that the integral term is
strictly positive (see Section~\ref{sec:results-variational}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Upper bound: certified polynomial constructions}
\label{sec:upper-bound}

For the upper bound, we searched for univalent functions with small
inradius among three families:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item \textbf{Goodman-type radial slit domains}: $\DD$ minus $n$
        equally spaced radial slits of length~$\ell$, with parameters
        $n \in \{2,3,\ldots,8\}$ and $\ell \in (0,1)$.
  \item \textbf{Carroll--Ortega-Cerd\`{a} arc domains}: $\DD$ minus
        $n$ circular arcs satisfying harmonic symmetry, parameterized
        by arc position, radius, and opening angle.
  \item \textbf{Logarithmic spiral slit domains} (novel): $\DD$ minus
        $n$ logarithmic spiral arcs, parameterized by spiral rate and
        arc length.
\end{enumerate}

Additionally, we performed a systematic search over polynomial
families $f(z) = z + a_2 z^2 + a_3 z^3$ with univalence certified by
the NW criterion ($\sum n\abs{a_n} < 1$), scanning over $1000$+
parameter combinations and applying targeted optimization (Nelder--Mead
and gradient-based methods via \textsc{SciPy}).

\subsection{Interval arithmetic framework}
\label{sec:interval}

We implemented a rigorous interval arithmetic framework using
\texttt{mpmath.iv} to certify bounds.  The framework:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Verifies univalence via the NW criterion with interval
        coefficient arithmetic.
  \item Computes certified inradius lower bounds via boundary sampling
        with explicit discretization error bounds.
  \item Handles branch cuts and singularities in conformal maps.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cross-domain exploration}
\label{sec:cross-domain}

Using the ConceptEvolve system, we explored $12$ cross-domain concepts
connecting the~$\Bu$ problem to optimization theory, spectral geometry,
probability, and computational algebra.  The three most productive
concept paths were:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Channel geometry $+$ Grunsky constraints (produced our
        numerical improvement).
  \item Hyperbolic metric / Beardon--Pommerenke refinement (theoretical
        insight, new inequality).
  \item SDP / conformal bootstrap analogy (identified as the most
        promising future direction).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

All computations were performed in Python~3.10 with the following
libraries: \texttt{mpmath}~1.4.0 (arbitrary-precision and interval
arithmetic), \texttt{numpy}~2.2.6, \texttt{scipy}~1.15.3,
\texttt{matplotlib}~3.10.8, \texttt{seaborn}~0.13.2, and
\texttt{cvxpy}~1.7.5.
All random computations used seed~42 for reproducibility.

\paragraph{Lower bound computation.}
The channel geometry analysis was performed at Grunsky truncation
levels $N \in \{2, 5, 10, 15\}$ using 80-digit multiprecision
arithmetic.  The implicit function iteration was run until convergence
at each level.

\paragraph{Upper bound search.}
Three domain families were scanned:
Goodman (20 configurations),
Carroll-type (27 configurations),
and spiral slits (27 configurations).
The polynomial search evaluated $5000$ random polynomials of degree
$\le 15$ and $1000$+ targeted configurations in the quadratic--cubic
family, with optimization via Nelder--Mead (tolerance $10^{-8}$).

\paragraph{Interval verification.}
Certified bounds were computed with \texttt{mpmath.iv} at 80-digit
precision.  Boundary sampling used $N_{\text{bdy}} = 2000$ points with
explicit discretization error bounds.

\paragraph{Sensitivity analysis.}
Each parameter was perturbed by $\pm 1\%$ around its baseline value,
and the resulting change in the lower bound was recorded.

\subsection{New variational inequality}
\label{sec:results-variational}

The excess Bloch semi-norm inequality
(Proposition~\ref{prop:excess}) was verified numerically on polynomial
test functions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item For $f(z) = z + 0.3z^3$ (3-fold symmetric):
        $\Bf \approx 0.79$, integral term $\approx 0.29$.
  \item For $f(z) = z - 0.49z^2$ (maximal quadratic deformation):
        $\Bf \approx 0.94$, integral term $\approx 0.44$.
\end{itemize}
In all tested cases, the integral term is strictly positive,
confirming the theoretical prediction.

\subsection{Certified upper bound}
\label{sec:results-upper}

\begin{proposition}[Certified upper bound]
\label{prop:upper}
$\Bu \le 0.7975$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The polynomial $f(z) = z - 0.171z^2 - 0.190z^3$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Univalence}: $2\abs{a_2} + 3\abs{a_3} =
        2(0.171) + 3(0.190) = 0.912 < 1$, so~$f$ is univalent
        by the NW criterion.
  \item \emph{Inradius}: Boundary sampling with $N_{\text{bdy}} = 2000$
        points and interval arithmetic gives a certified
        inradius $\ge 0.7975$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

This bound does \emph{not} improve upon the Carroll--Ortega-Cerd\`{a}
upper bound of $0.6564$~\citep{carrollortegacerda2009}, which
employs much more sophisticated domain constructions involving harmonic
symmetric arcs and Fedorov's P\'{o}lya--Chebotarev
solution~\citep{fedorov1985}.  The NW criterion restricts to
near-convex functions, which necessarily have large inradii.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Assessment of results}

Our investigation achieved a very modest improvement ($10^{-7}$) to
the lower bound on~$\Bu$.  While the mathematical framework is sound,
the magnitude of the improvement is comparable to the uncertainty from
non-rigorous computational steps, and we classify it as a numerical
certificate rather than a proof.

The certified upper bound $\Bu \le 0.7975$ is rigorous but weak
compared to the Carroll--Ortega-Cerd\`{a} bound of~$0.6564$.  The
gap between our polynomial-based approach and the state of the art
reflects the fundamental limitation of the NW univalence criterion,
which is restricted to near-convex functions.

\subsection{Why the problem is hard}

The univalent Bloch constant problem resists significant improvement
for several structural reasons:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The extremal function is unknown.}  Unlike the Bloch
    constant~$B$, where the Ahlfors--Grunsky conjecture gives a
    specific candidate, there is no conjectured extremal for~$\Bu$.

  \item \textbf{Competing constraints.}  Minimizing the inradius
    requires the image to be ``thin'' in all directions, but
    univalence prevents the boundary from folding back, creating
    tension between thinness and injectivity.

  \item \textbf{Infinite-dimensional optimization.}  The space of
    schlicht functions is infinite-dimensional, and finite
    truncations (polynomials) are poor approximations to the
    extremal function, which likely has boundary singularities at
    the contact points.

  \item \textbf{Computational barriers.}  Rigorous conformal mapping
    of general simply connected domains requires either
    Schwarz--Christoffel computation (polygonal domains) or
    integral equation methods, both requiring substantial
    implementation effort for certified results.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What worked and what did not}

\paragraph{Successful approaches.}
The conformal toolkit correctly reproduces all known exact values
(Table~\ref{tab:verification}).
The interval arithmetic framework provides certified bounds for
polynomial maps.
The variational analysis (Proposition~\ref{prop:excess}) yields a new
inequality with a concrete proof sketch.
The concept-tree exploration identified the SDP/Grunsky direction as
high-potential.

\paragraph{Unsuccessful approaches.}
The Grunsky matrix SDP relaxation at low truncation ($N = 2$) suffered
from normalization issues.
Random polynomial search with approximate univalence testing produced
false positives.
The NW-based upper bound cannot compete with specialized domain
constructions.
We did not implement the full conformal welding + Fedorov
P\'{o}lya--Chebotarev computation needed to reproduce or improve the
Carroll--Ortega-Cerd\`{a} bound.

\subsection{Most promising future directions}
\label{sec:future}

Based on our comprehensive exploration, we identify the following
ranked directions for future work:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{SDP relaxation via truncated Grunsky matrices
    (highest potential).}
    The truncated Grunsky matrix $G_N$ provides a convex relaxation
    of the univalence constraint.  Minimizing the inradius subject to
    $\norm{G_N} \le 1$ is a semidefinite program.  Using exact
    arithmetic solvers (SDPA-GMP or SDPB) at $N = 50$--$100$ could
    yield rigorous lower bounds significantly above $0.571$.
    The convergence rate as $N \to \infty$ would give numerical
    evidence for the exact value of~$\Bu$.

  \item \textbf{Numerical conformal welding for upper bounds.}
    Implementing Carroll--Ortega-Cerd\`{a}'s conformal welding
    approach with $n = 4, 5, 6$ symmetric arcs could improve
    the upper bound below $0.6564$.

  \item \textbf{Extremal length refinement.}
    The modulus of the channel path family at the contact points
    provides a more refined constraint than the opening angle alone,
    potentially improving the lower bound by a larger margin.

  \item \textbf{Symmetry-constrained optimization.}
    Restricting to the class $\SSS_3$ of schlicht functions with
    3-fold symmetry $f(e^{2\pi i/3}z) = e^{2\pi i/3}f(z)$ reduces
    the problem to a lower-dimensional space and is consistent with
    the expected symmetry of the extremal domain.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have conducted a comprehensive investigation of the univalent Bloch
constant~$\Bu$, developing computational tools, analyzing the
mathematical structure, and attempting to tighten the known bounds.

Our main results are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A numerical certificate for $\Bu > 0.5708859$, improving the
    lower bound by~$10^{-7}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:lower}).
  \item A new variational inequality
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:excess}) relating the inradius to the
    excess Bloch semi-norm.
  \item A certified upper bound $\Bu \le 0.7975$
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}).
  \item Identification of SDP relaxation via Grunsky matrices as
    the most promising direction for future progress.
\end{itemize}

The gap between the best lower bound ($0.5709$) and upper bound
($0.6564$) remains at approximately~$0.086$, essentially unchanged
from the published state of the art.  The univalent Bloch constant
remains one of the most challenging open problems in geometric
function theory, and we hope the computational framework and
mathematical insights developed here will serve as a foundation for
future work.

A key observation for the community is that, to our knowledge, no
prior work has applied semidefinite programming methods to the
univalent Bloch constant.  Given the maturity of exact-arithmetic
SDP solvers and the natural formulation via Grunsky matrices, this
represents a significant unexplored opportunity.
