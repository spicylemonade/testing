\begin{abstract}
Cold start from simultaneous weak energy sources is usually attacked by adding more elaborate source-aware arbitration ahead of the main power-management loop. This paper tests that assumption directly. We built five helper-free \texttt{ngspice}-39 topologies sharing one source model, one handoff threshold pair, and one repaired pre-handoff energy contract: a fixed single-branch startup path, a nonaware joined-input baseline, an RC-ranked packet gate, a source-blind packet gate, and a lower-overhead time-constant-ranked arbiter. Across a 24-case primary startup matrix, all five designs are tied at $17/24$ startup successes with zero measured pre-handoff back-drive. Across an expanded 10-case falsifier suite containing collapse, mixed polarity, unequal open-circuit voltage, leak-path, and real UVLO-chatter stress, the blind packet gate achieves $10/10$ startups, outperforming the original RC-ranked design at $8/10$, the fixed baseline at $7/10$, and the nonaware baseline at $5/10$. A time-constant-ranked arbiter retains $9/10$ falsifier startups while reducing the median successful-case pre-handoff control energy from $2.37717\times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{J}$ to $1.11852\times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{J}$, a $52.9\%$ reduction relative to the RC-ranked design. We prove that pre-handoff control energy is governed exactly by effective control conductance in the implemented netlists and that only the nonaware baseline contains an explicit pre-handoff branch-joining path. Robustness runs with 24 randomized samples per design-case confirm that the fixed baseline remains $0/24$ on the source-collapse case \texttt{fa\_001}, the nonaware baseline remains $0/24$ on the mixed-conflict case \texttt{fa\_005}, and the packet-gated family remains $24/24$ on both. The main result is negative but important: in this helper-free weak-source model, packet-gated isolation matters, explicit pre-handoff source ranking does not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Batteryless sensor nodes increasingly aggregate multiple weak transducers because no single harvester is reliable enough across all environmental states. That design choice shifts the burden from steady-state extraction to startup correctness: when several weak sources ramp slowly, collapse asynchronously, or arrive with opposite polarity, the startup path must accumulate the first useful packet of charge without a hidden helper rail and without letting one source damage the other \citep{goppert2016startup70mv,das2017selfstarter,gogolou2025multisourcereview}. The standard response is to add more pre-handoff intelligence: adaptive dual-source startup, polarity detection, branch ranking, or autonomous multi-input routing \citep{tang2018dualsource,cao2019bipolarinput,kuai2022dualpolarityjssc,li2022multiinputplatform}.

That intuition is plausible, but the literature rarely closes the same-family ablation loop. It is common to compare a new startup selector against an open-loop baseline or a weakly isolated merge path. It is much less common to execute the nearby ``do less'' alternative that keeps the same isolation topology but removes the explicit selector. For simultaneous weak-source cold start, that omission matters: if packet-gated isolation is the real load-bearing feature, then complex pre-handoff ranking may add area, tuning burden, and energy without increasing the reachable startup envelope.

This paper began as a conventional champion-design study around an RC-ranked packet gate. The executed evidence overturned that story. A source-blind packet gate that retains the same isolated topology but fixes the branch weights at $0.5/0.5$ matches the RC-ranked design on every primary startup-matrix case and outperforms it on the expanded falsifier suite. The interesting result is therefore not ``the new selector wins.'' The interesting result is that explicit pre-handoff source ranking is unnecessary in the tested helper-free model, while a simpler packet-gated isolator is enough to preserve the adversarial startup boundary.

The paper makes four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item We define and execute a unified helper-free weak-source benchmark with corrected pre-handoff accounting, a 24-case startup matrix, a 10-case falsifier suite, and a 24-sample robustness study on four load-bearing cases.
\item We show experimentally that the primary startup matrix is a null result: all five designs start in $17/24$ cases and all five exhibit zero measured pre-handoff back-drive on that matrix.
\item We falsify the original mechanism hypothesis: a source-blind packet gate is $10/10$ on the falsifier suite, compared with $8/10$ for the original RC-ranked design, while a time-constant-ranked arbiter preserves $9/10$ falsifier successes at roughly half the pre-handoff control energy.
\item We prove two structural facts about the implemented netlists: pre-handoff control energy is exactly determined by effective control conductance and the nonaware baseline is the only design with an explicit pre-handoff branch-joining path. The first theorem predicts the measured energy ordering, while the second predicts the failure localization seen in the leak-path and mixed-conflict falsifiers.
\end{enumerate}

The rest of the paper proceeds from prior art to model definition, method, proofs, experiments, and limitations. The key theme is deliberate restraint: the final claim is not that a new source-aware architecture wins broadly, but that a minimal packet-gated isolator is sufficient and that explicit pre-handoff ranking should be treated as a falsifiable hypothesis rather than a default assumption.

\section{Related Work}
Integrated low-voltage startup for energy harvesters is mature enough that broad ``weak-source cold start'' language no longer provides a novelty moat. Fully integrated startup below \SI{100}{\milli\volt} has been demonstrated repeatedly for thermoelectric harvesters and self-starters \citep{goppert2016startup70mv,das2017selfstarter,quintero2019cmosstartup,coustans2019coldstart60mv}. Those papers establish that low-voltage startup is difficult, but they are mostly single-input or effectively single-input at startup.

The closest architectural overlap on dual-source awareness is the adaptive dual-source startup line typified by Tang et al. \citep{tang2018dualsource}. Once the claim is phrased as ``adaptive'' or ``source-aware'' startup, the overlap becomes immediate. Likewise, polarity handling itself is not fresh. Bipolar-input and dual-polarity interfaces already integrate polarity detection or polarity-tolerant front ends with cold start \citep{cao2019bipolarinput,kuai2022dualpolarityjssc,alhawari2017polaritydetection}. Any new paper must therefore do something more specific than ``handle mixed polarity.''

The broader multi-input and autonomous platform literature further narrows the space. Batteryless multi-source piezoelectric PMUs, autonomous multi-input cold-start platforms, and recent self-powered multi-input interface circuits already cover many combinations of routing, self-powering, and isolation \citep{alghisi2017batteryless,li2022multiinputplatform,wang2023serialstack,chen2024collaborative,weng2024osece}. Review articles now treat multi-source integration, intermittency, and interface complexity as an active but crowded design family rather than an empty frontier \citep{gogolou2025multisourcereview}.

What is not well represented in that literature is an explicit falsification study of pre-handoff ranking inside one tightly controlled helper-free family. The paper does not claim a better platform than those prior interfaces. It claims something narrower: when all designs share the same source model, the same handoff contract, and the same packet-gated isolation topology, explicit RC-style source ranking is not the ingredient that preserves startup under the executed adversarial stresses.

\section{Background and Preliminaries}
\subsection{Source Model and Symbols}
We study two floating Thevenin sources, indexed by $i\in\{A,B\}$, that feed a common cold-start front end. Source $i$ has open-circuit ceiling $V_i>0$, source resistance $R_i>0$, polarity $p_i\in\{+1,-1\}$, and common ramp slope $S>0$. The source-pair include implements
\begin{equation}
  v_i^{\mathrm{oc}}(t)=p_i\,\min\{V_i,St\}\,c_i(t),
\end{equation}
where $c_i(t)$ is a collapse-and-recovery window equal to one during nominal operation and reduced to a user-set scale factor during falsifier events. The collapse windows are generated by smooth hyperbolic-tangent transitions so that the resulting netlists remain differentiable in transient analysis.

The routed differential at the local branch bus is denoted by $u_i(t)=V(b_{i,p}(t),b_{i,n}(t))$. The shared forward router \texttt{fw\_router} flips a negative input when necessary so that $u_i(t)$ is the oriented branch voltage presented to downstream startup logic. The startup dead-zone is \(\vdead=\SI{5}{\milli\volt}\) unless overridden by a falsifier or robustness sample.

The store capacitor voltage is \(\vstore(t)=V(n_{\mathrm{store}}(t))\). The main handoff threshold pair is \((V_{\mathrm{handoff}},V_{\mathrm{handoff,fall}})=(\SI{1.2}{\volt},\SI{1.0}{\volt})\) in the primary matrix and is tightened to \((\SI{0.8}{\volt},\SI{0.7}{\volt})\) in the chatter falsifier. Startup is declared successful if \(\vstore(t)\) reaches \(V_{\mathrm{handoff}}\) before the case-specific stop time.

For packet-gated topologies, define the available branch drive
\begin{equation}
  x_i(t)=\max\{u_i(t)-\vdead,0\}.
\end{equation}
The shared pump abstraction then injects storage current proportional to a weighted combination of \(x_A\) and \(x_B\).

\subsection{Topology Family}
All five studied designs reuse the same source model, handoff latch, and measurement hooks, but they differ in how the two routed branches are connected before handoff.

The fixed baseline uses only one routed branch and therefore represents the strongest ``do almost nothing'' startup path. The nonaware baseline routes both inputs and then hard-joins them onto a common startup bus through four \SI{1}{\milli\ohm} resistors. The three packet-gated designs never hard-join the two routed branches; instead they feed branch-local current sources that inject charge into the common store node.

For the packet-gated family, the generic storage current has the form
\begin{equation}
  i_{\mathrm{store}}(t)=\etap\Gp\bigl(x_A(t)w_A(t)+x_B(t)w_B(t)\bigr),
  \label{eq:storecurrent}
\end{equation}
where \(w_A,w_B\in[0,1]\) and \(w_A+w_B=1\). The three packet-gated implementations differ only in the weight law.

For \RCRanked, scout states \(q_A\) and \(q_B\) satisfy matched first-order probe dynamics with time constant \(\tau_{\mathrm{scout}}=R_{\mathrm{scout}}C_{\mathrm{scout}}\), and the weight is
\begin{equation}
  w_A(t)=\frac{1+\tanh\bigl(k_{\mathrm{sel}}(q_A(t)-q_B(t))\bigr)}{2},\qquad w_B(t)=1-w_A(t).
\end{equation}
For \Blind, the weights are fixed at \(w_A=w_B=\tfrac12\). For \TCRanked, each branch is filtered by a fast and a slow window; the selector applies a hyperbolic tangent to the difference of fast-slow contrasts plus a small fast-window tie term.

\subsection{Measurement Contract}
The measurement include exports four load-bearing quantities:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Startup success.} \(\mathrm{startup\_ok}=1\) if \(\vstore\) reaches \(V_{\mathrm{handoff}}\) before \(T_{\mathrm{stop}}\).
\item \textbf{First handoff time.} \(\thandoff\) is the first rising threshold crossing of \(\vstore\) at \(V_{\mathrm{handoff}}\).
\item \textbf{Pre-handoff back-drive energy.} \(\Eback\) is the sum of the negative port-power integrals seen at the two source measurement elements before the handoff latch closes.
\item \textbf{Pre-handoff control energy.} \(\Ectrl\) is the energy drawn through the dedicated control monitor source before the same handoff latch closes.
\end{enumerate}
The repaired measurement hook also exports full-window diagnostics \(\Ectrl^{\mathrm{full}}\) and \(\Eback^{\mathrm{full}}\), but those are treated as diagnostics rather than headline metrics. Figure~\ref{fig:accounting} shows why that distinction matters.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Simulation Pipeline}
Every deterministic case is rendered from one of five template netlists, simulated in batch mode with \texttt{ngspice-39}, and parsed through a shared log extractor. The design list is
\[
\mathcal{D}=\{\Fixed,\Nonaware,\RCRanked,\Blind,\TCRanked\}.
\]
The primary startup manifest contains 24 cases chosen to cover each voltage, ramp, impedance-ratio, and polarity level at least once. The resulting matrix is intentionally budgeted rather than factorial; it is a coverage matrix, not a causal design of experiments. The falsifier manifest contains 10 adversarial cases spanning source collapse, mixed polarity, unequal open-circuit voltage, finite off-isolation leak, and one deliberately repaired UVLO-chatter case.

The case stop time follows the contract
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{stop}}=\max\left\{1.25\,\frac{V_{\mathrm{level}}}{S},\; 8\min\{R_A,R_B\}C_{\mathrm{store}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
This rule guarantees enough dwell time for very slow ramps without wasting runtime on obviously dead cases.

\subsection{Expanded Falsifier Suite}
The expanded suite differs from the original narrow package in three ways. First, it adds unequal-\(V_{\mathrm{OC}}\) cases with \((V_A,V_B)=(\SI{20}{\milli\volt},\SI{50}{\milli\volt})\) and \((\SI{100}{\milli\volt},\SI{300}{\milli\volt})\). Second, it adds finite off-isolation leak-path cases by forcing the router leak resistance down to \SI{1}{\kilo\ohm}. Third, it repairs the chatter case so that the handoff threshold and the collapse windows actually create a first handoff, a fall event, and a second rise in the vulnerable designs. Those changes matter because a falsifier that never induces the advertised failure mode cannot support a mechanism claim.

\subsection{Robustness Protocol}
The robustness study evaluates the four load-bearing cases \texttt{fa\_001}, \texttt{fa\_004}, \texttt{fa\_005}, and \texttt{sm\_015}. For each case-design pair, the script draws 24 independent samples with seed
\begin{equation}
  s = 20260312 + 10000\,o_c + 1000\,o_d + n,
\end{equation}
where \(o_c\) is the case offset, \(o_d\) the design offset, and \(n\in\{0,\dots,23\}\) the sample index. The randomized parameters are:
\begin{itemize}
\item source resistances: multiplicative Gaussian variation with standard deviation $8\%$ and floor factor $0.6$;
\item store capacitance: multiplicative Gaussian variation with standard deviation $10\%$ and floor factor $0.5$;
\item startup dead-zone: multiplicative Gaussian variation with standard deviation $12\%$, clipped to \SIrange{1}{15}{\milli\volt};
\item RC-ranked and blind packet controls: scout $R$, scout $C$, and control conductance with standard deviations $12\%$, $12\%$, and $10\%$;
\item time-constant-ranked control: fast/slow $R$ and $C$ values with standard deviation $12\%$, plus $10\%$ control-conductance variation;
\item leak resistance: $15\%$ multiplicative Gaussian variation in the leak-path cases;
\item collapse timing: additive Gaussian jitter with standard deviation \SI{0.12}{\second};
\item collapse scales: additive Gaussian jitter with standard deviation $0.04$, clipped to $[0,1]$.
\end{itemize}
Wilson \(95\%\) intervals are reported for startup probability. Since the study is simulation-only, those intervals quantify only the scripted parameter uncertainty, not fabrication variation or model inadequacy.

\subsection{Software and Compute Environment}
The experiments were executed on a Linux x86-64 workstation with 20 logical CPUs, Python~3.10.17, and \texttt{ngspice-39}. The artifact bundle, netlists, figures, and manifests are stored in the repository snapshot cited as \citep{archivara2026repo}. All deterministic and robustness scripts are included in the same workspace.

\section{Theoretical Results and Proofs}
This section proves three statements directly from the implemented netlists. They are intentionally modest. The paper does not attempt a transistor-level proof of startup probability. It proves the structural facts that are actually available from the model and then tests the remaining claims experimentally.

\begin{proposition}[Symmetry reduction of \RCRanked to \Blind]
\label{prop:symmetry}
Assume the two routed branch voltages satisfy
\begin{equation}
  u_A(t)=u_B(t)\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,T],
\end{equation}
and that the scout networks in the RC-ranked gate use identical component values and zero initial charge. Then, on $[0,T]$, the RC-ranked packet gate is dynamically identical to the blind packet gate: its branch weights satisfy \(w_A(t)=w_B(t)=\tfrac12\), and its pump and storage current equations reduce exactly to the blind-packet equations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(q_A(t)\) and \(q_B(t)\) denote the scout capacitor voltages \texttt{a\_probe} and \texttt{b\_probe}. Because the two probe branches use the same \texttt{SCOUT\_R} and \texttt{SCOUT\_C} values, each obeys the same first-order linear differential equation with the corresponding routed source voltage as input:
\begin{align}
  R_{\mathrm{scout}}C_{\mathrm{scout}}\,\dot q_A(t) + q_A(t) &= u_A(t), \\
  R_{\mathrm{scout}}C_{\mathrm{scout}}\,\dot q_B(t) + q_B(t) &= u_B(t).
\end{align}
Subtract the second equation from the first and define \(z(t)=q_A(t)-q_B(t)\). Then
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{scout}}C_{\mathrm{scout}}\,\dot z(t) + z(t) = u_A(t)-u_B(t).
\end{equation}
By assumption, the right-hand side is identically zero on $[0,T]$. The scout capacitors are initialized equally at zero, so \(z(0)=0\). The homogeneous linear differential equation with zero initial condition has the unique solution \(z(t)=0\) for all \(t\in[0,T]\). Therefore \(q_A(t)=q_B(t)\) for all \(t\in[0,T]\).

The RC-ranked selector law is
\begin{equation}
  w_A(t)=\frac{1+\tanh\bigl(k_{\mathrm{sel}}(q_A(t)-q_B(t))\bigr)}{2},
  \qquad
  w_B(t)=1-w_A(t).
\end{equation}
Since \(q_A(t)-q_B(t)=0\), the argument of the hyperbolic tangent is zero. Because \(\tanh(0)=0\), we obtain
\begin{equation}
  w_A(t)=\frac{1+0}{2}=\frac12,
  \qquad
  w_B(t)=1-\frac12=\frac12.
\end{equation}
Substituting these weights into the RC-ranked branch current expressions gives
\begin{align}
  i_{\mathrm{store}}(t)
   &= \etap\Gp\left(x_A(t)\frac12+x_B(t)\frac12\right), \\
  i_{A,\mathrm{in}}(t)
   &= \Gp\,x_A(t)\frac12,
  \qquad
  i_{B,\mathrm{in}}(t)
   = \Gp\,x_B(t)\frac12,
\end{align}
which are exactly the blind-packet equations. The control branch and the handoff latch are already shared between the two designs, so the full netlist dynamics coincide on $[0,T]$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:symmetry} does not imply that the two packet-gated designs are always identical, because many cases intentionally violate branch symmetry. It does show, however, that explicit ranking cannot create a difference in any operating regime where the routed branch histories are already matched.

\begin{theorem}[Exact pre-handoff control-energy law]
\label{thm:ctrl}
For each implemented design $d$, the repaired pre-handoff control energy is exactly
\begin{equation}
  \Ectrl^{(d)} = G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(d)}\int_0^{\tau_d} \vstore(t)^2\,dt,
\end{equation}
where \(\tau_d\) is the first handoff-latch time (or \(T_{\mathrm{stop}}\) if no handoff occurs) and the effective control conductance is
\begin{align}
  G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Fixed)} &= \frac{1}{40\,\mathrm{M\Omega}}+\frac{1}{80\,\mathrm{M\Omega}} = \SI{37.5}{\nano\siemens}, \\
  G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Nonaware)} &= \frac{1}{40\,\mathrm{M\Omega}}+\frac{1}{80\,\mathrm{M\Omega}}+\frac{1}{80\,\mathrm{M\Omega}} = \SI{50}{\nano\siemens}, \\
  G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\RCRanked)} &= G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Blind)} = \SI{40}{\nano\siemens}, \\
  G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\TCRanked)} &= \SI{18}{\nano\siemens}.
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We proceed design by design. In every design, the control-monitor element \texttt{VCTRL\_MON} is an ideal zero-volt source from \texttt{n\_store} to \texttt{n\_ctrl}. Therefore the source enforces
\begin{equation}
  V(n_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)) = V(n_{\mathrm{store}}(t)) = \vstore(t)
  \label{eq:vctrl-equals-vstore}
\end{equation}
for all $t$ in the transient analysis.

The measurement hook defines
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t) = \max\{\vstore(t)\,I(\texttt{VCTRL\_MON})(t),0\}
\end{equation}
and then multiplies it by the pre-handoff factor \(1-\ell_d(t)\), where \(\ell_d(t)\in[0,1]\) is the nondecreasing handoff latch. Since every control branch draws current from \texttt{n\_ctrl} to ground and \(\vstore(t)\ge 0\) throughout the startup interval, the current through \texttt{VCTRL\_MON} is nonnegative whenever the control network is active. The outer maximum is therefore inactive, so
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)=\vstore(t)\,I(\texttt{VCTRL\_MON})(t).
  \label{eq:pctrl-no-pos}
\end{equation}

For the fixed design, the control network consists of two resistors from \texttt{n\_ctrl} to ground: \texttt{RCTRL\_OSC} = \SI{40}{\mega\ohm} and \texttt{RCTRL\_PATH} = \SI{80}{\mega\ohm}. By Ohm's law and \eqref{eq:vctrl-equals-vstore}, the current through the monitor source is
\begin{equation}
  I(\texttt{VCTRL\_MON})(t)=\frac{\vstore(t)}{40\,\mathrm{M\Omega}}+\frac{\vstore(t)}{80\,\mathrm{M\Omega}}=G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Fixed)}\,\vstore(t).
\end{equation}
Substitute this into \eqref{eq:pctrl-no-pos} to obtain
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Fixed)}(t)=G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Fixed)}\,\vstore(t)^2.
\end{equation}
Integrating only while the handoff latch is open yields
\begin{equation}
  \Ectrl^{(\Fixed)}=\int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}} p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Fixed)}(t)(1-\ell_{\mathrm{fix}}(t))\,dt = G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Fixed)}\int_0^{\tau_{\mathrm{fix}}} \vstore(t)^2\,dt.
\end{equation}

The nonaware design adds one extra \SI{80}{\mega\ohm} branch, so the monitor current becomes
\begin{equation}
  I(\texttt{VCTRL\_MON})(t)=\left(\frac{1}{40\,\mathrm{M\Omega}}+\frac{1}{80\,\mathrm{M\Omega}}+\frac{1}{80\,\mathrm{M\Omega}}\right)\vstore(t)=G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Nonaware)}\vstore(t),
\end{equation}
and the same integration step gives the claimed expression for \(\Ectrl^{(\Nonaware)}\).

The RC-ranked and blind packet-gated designs use a single behavioral control sink of the form
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)=G_{\mathrm{scout}}\,V(n_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t))=G_{\mathrm{scout}}\,\vstore(t)
\end{equation}
with \(G_{\mathrm{scout}}=\SI{40}{\nano\siemens}\). Therefore
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\RCRanked)}(t)=p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Blind)}(t)=G_{\mathrm{scout}}\,\vstore(t)^2,
\end{equation}
and integrating to the respective handoff latch times yields the stated formulas.

The time-constant-ranked design has the same structure, but its control sink is \texttt{BCTRL\_TC} with conductance \(G_{\mathrm{TC}}=\SI{18}{\nano\siemens}\). Hence
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\TCRanked)}(t)=G_{\mathrm{TC}}\,\vstore(t)^2,
\end{equation}
which integrates to the claimed result.

All cases therefore share the same generic law
\begin{equation}
  \Ectrl^{(d)} = G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(d)} \int_0^{\tau_d}\vstore(t)^2\,dt.
\end{equation}
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Matched-trajectory energy ratio]
\label{cor:matched}
If two designs $d_1$ and $d_2$ satisfy \(\vstore^{(d_1)}(t)=\vstore^{(d_2)}(t)\) for all \(t\in[0,\tau]\) and hand off at the same time \(\tau\), then
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Ectrl^{(d_1)}}{\Ectrl^{(d_2)}} = \frac{G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(d_1)}}{G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(d_2)}}.
\end{equation}
In particular, a matched \TCRanked trajectory should consume only \(18/40=0.45\) of the \RCRanked pre-handoff control energy.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Under the stated assumptions, the two integrals \(\int_0^{\tau}\vstore(t)^2dt\) are identical. Dividing the expressions from Theorem~\ref{thm:ctrl} immediately yields the conductance ratio. Substituting \(G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\TCRanked)}=\SI{18}{\nano\siemens}\) and \(G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\RCRanked)}=\SI{40}{\nano\siemens}\) gives $18/40=0.45$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Only \Nonaware contains an explicit pre-handoff branch-joining path]
\label{prop:isolation}
Before handoff, the nonaware netlist contains finite-resistance paths from the routed branch-A bus to the routed branch-B bus, while the fixed and all three packet-gated netlists do not.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We inspect each implementation directly.

\textbf{Nonaware.} The nonaware netlist includes
\begin{align}
  &\texttt{RJOIN\_A\_P } n_{A,p} \leftrightarrow n_{\mathrm{start},p} \quad (\SI{1}{\milli\ohm}), \\
  &\texttt{RJOIN\_B\_P } n_{B,p} \leftrightarrow n_{\mathrm{start},p} \quad (\SI{1}{\milli\ohm}), \\
  &\texttt{RJOIN\_A\_N } n_{A,n} \leftrightarrow n_{\mathrm{start},n} \quad (\SI{1}{\milli\ohm}), \\
  &\texttt{RJOIN\_B\_N } n_{B,n} \leftrightarrow n_{\mathrm{start},n} \quad (\SI{1}{\milli\ohm}).
\end{align}
Therefore the positive buses are connected by the explicit resistor chain
\begin{equation}
  n_{A,p} \longleftrightarrow n_{\mathrm{start},p} \longleftrightarrow n_{B,p}
\end{equation}
with total resistance \SI{2}{\milli\ohm}, and the negative buses are connected by the analogous \SI{2}{\milli\ohm} chain. These are finite, direct, pre-handoff branch-joining paths.

\textbf{Fixed.} The fixed design routes only source A into the startup pump. Source B is present in the source model and in the measurement hooks, but there is no resistor, switch, or pump input connecting the branch-B routed bus to the startup path. Hence there is no explicit pre-handoff branch-A to branch-B conductive path.

\textbf{Packet-gated family.} In the RC-ranked, blind, and time-constant-ranked netlists, the routed buses of A and B appear only as input arguments to separate behavioral current sources. Each branch injects current into the common store node through its own current source expression, but there is no resistor, switch, or explicit bus-join element connecting branch A to branch B. A current source from branch A to the store node and a second current source from branch B to the same store node do not create a finite-resistance branch-to-branch path, because the constitutive law of each source depends on its local terminal voltage and not on a direct conductive link to the other branch bus.

Consequently, before handoff, only \Nonaware contains an explicit finite branch-joining path between the routed inputs. The fixed and packet-gated designs do not.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:isolation} does not prove that \Nonaware must fail every adversarial case. It proves the structural asymmetry that makes \Nonaware uniquely vulnerable to branch-loading and leak-path stress. The experimental sections test whether that structural difference is large enough to matter in the chosen weak-source regime.

\section{Discussion}
The paper's most important message is methodological. The same-family ablation should have been the first experiment, not the last. Once the blind packet gate is executed, the architectural story changes immediately. The correct conclusion is no longer ``a smarter selector improves startup.'' The correct conclusion is ``the selector was never the real ingredient; the isolation topology was.'' That is a stronger engineering insight precisely because it is negative.

The evidence also suggests a practical design rule. If the design goal is adversarial startup robustness inside this helper-free model, then a blind packet gate is sufficient and often better. If the design goal includes tighter control-energy budget without giving up most adversarial robustness, then a time-constant-ranked arbiter is the only source-aware variant that remains worth considering. In contrast, the original RC-ranked selector now appears to combine the cost of explicit ranking with none of the falsifier advantage that motivated it.

Several limitations matter.
\begin{itemize}
\item The primary startup matrix is aliased. It is useful for coverage but not for causal factor attribution.
\item The study is simulation-only and uses behavioral startup pumps rather than a full transistor-level switched-converter realization.
\item The literature comparison is structural, not measured. We did not implement a literature-faithful autonomous multi-input silicon comparator.
\item The robustness study covers four load-bearing cases rather than the full deterministic manifest.
\end{itemize}
These limitations are serious enough to block a broad architecture paper, but they do not invalidate the narrower falsification result.

The societal implication is modest but real. Weak-source energy-harvesting interfaces are often discussed only through positive-result narratives: a new circuit, a new minimum startup voltage, a new efficiency peak. This paper argues for a more disciplined alternative. Publishing negative design-space results can reduce wasted complexity in batteryless sensing, especially when startup robustness matters more than nominal efficiency. The result is not that intelligence is useless. The result is that intelligence should earn its keep against the simplest nearby alternative, not against only the weakest baseline.

\section{Conclusion}
A helper-free RC-ranked packet gate does not win the weak multi-source cold-start problem in the tested model. The primary startup matrix is a five-way tie at $17/24$ successes. The executed same-family ablation then overturns the original hypothesis: a blind packet gate reaches $10/10$ startups on the expanded falsifier suite, while the RC-ranked design reaches only $8/10$. A lower-overhead time-constant-ranked arbiter remains worthwhile as a secondary point, achieving $9/10$ falsifier startups at $52.9\%$ lower successful-case pre-handoff control energy than the RC-ranked design. The engineering conclusion is clear. Packet-gated isolation matters. Explicit pre-handoff source ranking does not.

\section{Extended Proof Details}
\subsection{Why the scout-state difference remains zero under symmetry}
The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:symmetry} relied on uniqueness of a first-order linear differential equation. For completeness, write the homogeneous solution explicitly:
\begin{equation}
  z(t)=z(0)\exp\left(-\frac{t}{R_{\mathrm{scout}}C_{\mathrm{scout}}}\right).
\end{equation}
Since \(z(0)=0\), the solution is identically zero. No hidden branch state can break that equality because the two scout capacitors are the only dynamic elements in the selector law.

\subsection{Detailed conductance derivation}
The conductance values in Table~\ref{tab:designs} follow directly from parallel combination.
\begin{align}
  G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Fixed)} &= \frac{1}{40\,\mathrm{M\Omega}} + \frac{1}{80\,\mathrm{M\Omega}} = 25\,\mathrm{nS}+12.5\,\mathrm{nS}=37.5\,\mathrm{nS}, \\
  G_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Nonaware)} &= 25\,\mathrm{nS}+12.5\,\mathrm{nS}+12.5\,\mathrm{nS}=50\,\mathrm{nS}.
\end{align}
The packet-gated conductances are literal parameter values from the behavioral sinks. Thus the observed successful-case median ratio
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Ectrl^{(\TCRanked)}}{\Ectrl^{(\RCRanked)}} = \frac{1.11852\times 10^{-13}}{2.37717\times 10^{-13}} \approx 0.4705
\end{equation}
is close to the ideal conductance ratio \(18/40=0.45\), while the observed ratio
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Ectrl^{(\Nonaware)}}{\Ectrl^{(\Fixed)}} \approx 1.364
\end{equation}
is close to the ideal ratio \(50/37.5 = 1.333\). Those near-matches support the interpretation that pre-handoff control energy is being measured coherently after the metric repair.
