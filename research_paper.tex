\begin{abstract}
Cold start from multiple weak harvesters is often framed as a pre-handoff source-ranking problem: before the main power-management loop wakes up, the front end is expected to infer the best source and favor it aggressively. We test that assumption inside one helper-free two-source packet-gated startup family implemented in \texttt{ngspice-39}, repair the measurement contract where needed, and then design the smallest new mechanism that survives the corrected evidence pack. The study has four layers: (i) a 24-case primary startup matrix over voltage, ramp, polarity, and impedance asymmetry; (ii) a 10-case adversarial falsifier suite; (iii) a 24-sample robustness rerun on four context cases; and (iv) a bounded DEEPEN near-tie matrix with 12 core cases plus 4 same-scaffold no-packet controls. The repaired context pack rejects both universal stories. All five legacy designs are tied at $17/24$ startups on the primary matrix, while the falsifier suite yields a frontier rather than a champion: \TCRanked reaches $9/10$, \RCRanked reaches $8/10$, \Fixed reaches $7/10$, and both \Blind and \Nonaware reach $5/10$. We then introduce a \Confidence controller that stays near the blind packet posture until a normalized separability integrator clears threshold. On static near ties, the new controller and the blind baseline are both $6/6$, the controller commits $0/6$ times, and the median handoff difference is only $-0.000545\,\mathrm{s}$. On late-arrival near ties, both are again $6/6$, but the controller commits $6/6$ times with median $\tcommit = 1.02799\,\mathrm{s}$ and improves median handoff time by $0.30882\,\mathrm{s}$. A same-scaffold \Merge control is faster still at $4.67638\,\mathrm{s}$, but it reopens $1.54753\times 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{J}$ of wrong-way energy, whereas \Confidence maintains zero measured backdrive. The sampled robustness rerun preserves the corrected separators: \Fixed remains $0/24$ on the source-collapse separator \texttt{fa\_001}, while \Blind remains $0/24$ on the mixed-conflict separator \texttt{fa\_005}. We prove explicit abstention, commitment-latency, and monitor-path control-energy bounds directly from the implemented netlists. The resulting contribution is narrow but useful: in this family, startup should stay blind on static ambiguity and commit only when temporal separability appears.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Batteryless sensor nodes increasingly aggregate several harvesters because no single transducer is reliable across every environmental state. The hard problem is therefore often not steady-state extraction but pre-regulation startup: the interface must accumulate enough energy to wake the main power path while sources are weak, ramps are slow, impedances are mismatched, and one source may arrive or collapse before another \citep{goppert2016startup70mv,das2017selfstarter,gogolou2025multisourcereview}. This startup interval is the least forgiving part of the design. The regulator is not yet alive, the energy budget is smallest, and any unnecessary pre-handoff control burden is paid exactly when the system can least afford it.

The usual response is to add more source awareness before handoff. Dual-source tracking, polarity detection, and autonomous multi-input routing all embody the same intuition: the startup front end should identify the stronger source early and then favor it aggressively \citep{tang2018dualsource,liu2018,cao2019bipolarinput,kuai2022dualpolarityjssc,li2022multiinputplatform}. That intuition is plausible, but it has a methodological weakness. Many papers compare a new source-aware controller against a weak open-loop baseline, a different topology, or a fully integrated platform with several changes at once. It is rarer to execute the same-family ``do less'' alternative that keeps the isolation scaffold but removes the ranking logic.

This project began with exactly that more conventional story. The first champion was an \RCRanked packet-gated startup path. The repaired context evidence overturned any one-winner narrative. Across the 24-case primary matrix, five designs are tied at $17/24$ startups. Across the repaired 10-case falsifier suite, \TCRanked is strongest at $9/10$, \RCRanked reaches $8/10$, \Fixed reaches $7/10$, and both \Blind and \Nonaware reach $5/10$. The sampled 24-run robustness pack preserves the same split on its decisive separators: \Fixed remains $0/24$ on \texttt{fa\_001}, whereas \Blind and \Nonaware remain $0/24$ on \texttt{fa\_005}. The correct conclusion is therefore neither ``always rank early'' nor ``stay blind always.'' Startup behavior in this family is regime-dependent.

That corrected frontier sharpened the next question. If the front end should neither rank universally nor remain blind universally, when \emph{should} it move away from the blind packet posture? The DEEPEN lane answered that question with one bounded new mechanism: a \Confidence controller that integrates a normalized separability metric, stays near the blind packet posture while the evidence is ambiguous, and commits only after the margin persists long enough to clear threshold. The key discovery is not a new general power-management platform. It is a more specific design law: static ambiguity should trigger abstention, while temporal asymmetry can justify commitment.

The paper makes five contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item We reconstruct a causally cleaner startup evidence pack with repaired explicit-handoff timing, equal pre-handoff accounting, and an executed same-scaffold no-packet control, all distributed in \laneroot.
\item We show experimentally that the broad ranking story and the broad blind story both fail: the primary startup matrix is a five-way tie at $17/24$, the repaired falsifier suite produces no perfect row, and the 24-sample robustness reruns preserve that regime split on the decisive separators.
\item We design and execute a bounded 12-case near-tie matrix plus 4 decisive controls for a single new controller, \Confidence, and demonstrate a regime split that was absent from the earlier paper story: no benefit on static near ties, but a $0.30882\,\mathrm{s}$ median handoff gain over \Blind on late-arrival near ties with zero measured backdrive.
\item We prove, directly from the implemented netlists, three structural properties of the \Confidence deck: an abstention bound under sub-threshold separability, a commitment-latency bound under sustained asymmetry, and a dynamic monitor-path control-energy bound relative to the blind baseline.
\item We place the result honestly within the literature boundary recovered during the overlap audit: the contribution is not a new adaptive tracking architecture, not a new general multi-input PMU, and not a measured superiority claim against prior silicon. It is a bounded operating-regime result for helper-free startup.
\end{enumerate}

The rest of the paper proceeds from related work and model definition to method, proofs, experiments, and limitations. The central claim is intentionally narrow: in this executed family, startup should stay blind on unresolved near ties and commit only when temporal separability appears.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Weak-Source Startup and Polarity Handling}
Weak-source startup is already a mature design domain. Integrated thermoelectric startup below \SI{100}{\milli\volt} has been demonstrated repeatedly with careful charge-pump or boost-converter front ends \citep{goppert2016startup70mv,das2017selfstarter,quintero2019cmosstartup,coustans2019coldstart60mv}. Those papers establish the difficulty of cold start, but they do not create a novelty moat for any new paper that merely says weak sources are hard.

Likewise, mixed-polarity handling is not a new claim by itself. Bipolar-input and dual-polarity thermoelectric interfaces already integrate polarity detection or polarity-tolerant startup structures \citep{alhawari2017polaritydetection,cao2019bipolarinput,kuai2022dualpolarityjssc}. In the present paper, mixed polarity is therefore used as a stress regime, not as the novelty basis.

\subsection{Adaptive Source Tracking}
The closest direct overlap for any source-aware startup story is the adaptive dual-source line. Tang et al.\ demonstrate a dual-source thermoelectric/RF startup circuit with adaptive source handling \citep{tang2018dualsource}. Liu et al.\ go further and present cycle-by-cycle source tracking with adaptive peak-inductor-current control \citep{liu2018}. Those papers already occupy the conceptual space ``startup should infer the stronger source and act on that inference.'' Any paper that claimed another generally better pre-handoff ranking controller would therefore enter a crowded and already competent literature family.

This is exactly why the surviving claim here is different. The executed controller does not try to optimize source ranking continuously. It does something more conditional: refuse to rank until the margin is persistent enough to justify a commitment. The contribution survives only as that abstain-to-commit rule.

\subsection{Autonomous Multi-Input Platforms}
The overlap becomes even sharper at the PMU level. Battery-less multi-source interfaces, self-powered piezoelectric front ends, and autonomous multi-input platforms already cover routing, energy sharing, cold startup, and distributed power management at system scale \citep{alghisi2017batteryless,wang2023serialstack,chen2024collaborative,weng2024osece,li2022multiinputplatform,liu2024,liu2024distributedpmu}. Review articles now treat multi-source integration as an active and crowded design family rather than an open architectural vacuum \citep{gogolou2025multisourcereview}. Helper-assisted ultra-low-voltage startup also exists, which blocks any simplistic claim that helper-free operation or low voltage alone is the novelty \citep{lu2024tegassist}.

The present work is therefore intentionally narrower than a new PMU paper. It is a two-source same-scaffold startup study with one behavioral controller added to an existing packet-gated family. The paper makes no claim about steady-state extraction efficiency, multi-rail management, or all-rail autonomy.

\section{Background and Preliminaries}
\subsection{Source Model and Symbols}
We study two floating Thevenin sources indexed by $i\in\{A,B\}$ feeding a common startup front end. Source $i$ has open-circuit ceiling $V_i>0$, series resistance $R_i>0$, polarity $p_i\in\{+1,-1\}$, and a linear open-circuit ramp with slope $S>0$. The implemented source model is
\begin{equation}
  v_i^{\mathrm{oc}}(t)=p_i \min\{V_i,St\}\gamma_i(t),
  \label{eq:source-model}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_i(t)\in[0,1]$ is a case-specific window that represents collapse, recovery, or delayed arrival.

The forward router flips a negative input when necessary and presents the oriented routed branch voltage
\begin{equation}
  u_i(t)=V(b_{i,p}(t),b_{i,n}(t)).
\end{equation}
The startup dead zone is
\begin{equation}
  \vdead=\SI{5}{\milli\volt},
\end{equation}
unless a falsifier overrides it. The branch drive available to the startup pump is
\begin{equation}
  x_i(t)=\max\{u_i(t)-\vdead,0\}.
  \label{eq:xi}
\end{equation}
The store node voltage is
\begin{equation}
  \vstore(t)=V(n_{\mathrm{store}}(t)).
\end{equation}

Most deterministic cases use a handoff threshold pair
\begin{equation}
  (\vhand,\vhandfall)=(\SI{1.2}{\volt},\SI{1.0}{\volt}),
\end{equation}
while the repaired chatter falsifier uses $(\SI{0.8}{\volt},\SI{0.7}{\volt})$ to induce a real rise-fall-rise sequence. The repaired measurement hooks declare
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{startup\_ok} = \mathbf{1}\{\vstore(T_{\mathrm{stop}})\ge \vhand\}\mathbf{1}\{\texttt{handoff\_seen\_final}\ge 0.5\}.
  \label{eq:startupok}
\end{equation}
The second factor is important. Startup is not counted solely from the store-voltage proxy; the explicit handoff latch must also be observed.

\subsection{Design Family}
Two design sets appear in the paper. The context pack compares five earlier designs
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ctx}}=\{\RCRanked,\Fixed,\Nonaware,\Blind,\TCRanked\},
\end{equation}
and the DEEPEN pack compares three core designs plus one same-scaffold control
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{deep}}=\{\Confidence,\Blind,\TCRanked,\Merge\}.
\end{equation}

All packet-gated designs share one pump abstraction:
\begin{equation}
  i_{\mathrm{store}}(t)=\eta_{\mathrm{pump}}G_{\mathrm{pump}}\left(x_A(t)w_A(t)+x_B(t)w_B(t)\right),
  \label{eq:storecurrent}
\end{equation}
with $w_A,w_B\in[0,1]$ and $w_A+w_B=1$. What changes between designs is the pre-handoff coupling and the weight law.

The \Confidence controller is defined by three nested state variables. First, each branch passes through a scout filter:
\begin{equation}
  \taus \dot q_A(t)+q_A(t)=u_A(t),\qquad
  \taus \dot q_B(t)+q_B(t)=u_B(t),
  \label{eq:scout}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  \taus = R_{\mathrm{scout}}C_{\mathrm{scout}} = \SI{150}{\kilo\ohm}\cdot\SI{5}{\nano\farad}=\SI{0.75}{\milli\second}.
\end{equation}
Second, the raw separability metric is
\begin{equation}
  r(t)=\mathbf{1}\{|q_A(t)|+|q_B(t)|>V_{\mathrm{floor}}\}
       \min\!\left\{1,\frac{|q_A(t)-q_B(t)|}{\epsilon + |q_A(t)|+|q_B(t)|}\right\},
  \label{eq:rawconfidence}
\end{equation}
with $V_{\mathrm{floor}}=\SI{10}{\milli\volt}$ and $\epsilon=\SI{1}{\milli\volt}$. Third, the confidence accumulator satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \tauc \dot c(t)+c(t)=r(t),
  \qquad
  \tauc=R_{\mathrm{conf}}C_{\mathrm{conf}}=\SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}\cdot\SI{500}{\nano\farad}=\SI{50}{\milli\second},
  \label{eq:confidenceode}
\end{equation}
and the commit state is
\begin{equation}
  m(t)=\frac{1+\tanh\!\bigl(k_{\mathrm{c}}(c(t)-\Theta)\bigr)}{2},
  \label{eq:commit}
\end{equation}
with $k_{\mathrm{c}}=30$ and $\Theta=0.08$.

The winner preference is still computed from the signed scout margin,
\begin{equation}
  a(t)=\frac{1+\tanh\!\bigl(k_{\mathrm{s}}(q_A(t)-q_B(t))\bigr)}{2},
  \qquad
  k_{\mathrm{s}}=20,
\end{equation}
but the actual packet weight is blended between blind allocation and winner-take-more allocation:
\begin{equation}
  w_A(t)=\frac{1-m(t)}{2}+m(t)a(t),
  \qquad
  w_B(t)=1-w_A(t).
  \label{eq:confidenceweights}
\end{equation}
When $m(t)$ is small, the controller stays near the blind packet posture. When $m(t)$ approaches one, the controller approaches the winner preference $a(t)$.

\subsection{Measurement Contract}
The repaired measurement include exports four quantities that carry the paper's actual claims.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Startup success:} Eq.~\eqref{eq:startupok}.
\item \textbf{Explicit handoff timing:} $\thandoff$, plus fall and second-rise diagnostics when a chatter case induces them.
\item \textbf{Pre-handoff backdrive energy:} $\Eback$, the sum of the negative source-port power integrals before the handoff latch closes.
\item \textbf{Monitor-path pre-handoff control energy:} $\Ectrl$, the energy drawn through \texttt{VCTRL\_MON} while the handoff latch is open.
\end{enumerate}

The measurement file also records full-window versions of the energy integrals, but those are diagnostics rather than headline metrics. They deliberately continue to count post-handoff activity and therefore cannot be used to support claims about pre-handoff controller burden. Because \texttt{VCTRL\_MON} sees only the explicit control sink, $\Ectrl$ is exact for the monitored control branch but is not claimed as the total selector overhead of every auxiliary behavioral element.

\subsection{Lane Narrowing and Design Rationale}
The paper is intentionally not an open-ended architecture search. The repository's verified plan constrained the work to the existing H1 packet-gated scaffold, permitted only one new confidence mechanism, and parked restart-safe, reverse-port, and bridge hypotheses instead of reopening broad PMU or MPPT searches. That design discipline matters because it changes the research question. The goal was not ``find any topology that looks new.'' The goal was ``identify what the existing evidence actually leaves open.''

The executed context evidence left exactly one live question. The repaired falsifier suite did not crown a global champion; instead it exposed a frontier in which always-ranking controllers survive some heavy-load separators better, blind packet isolation survives others, and merged or fixed structures fail on different edges. The \Confidence deck is the answer the paper tests. It adds one normalized separability integrator, keeps the blind packet weights as the default posture, and pays its extra monitor-path control cost through the same \texttt{VCTRL\_MON} path used for every other design.

\subsection{Why a Bounded Positive Result Is Legitimate}
Three conditions were imposed before the DEEPEN lane could be written up as a real contribution.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The same-scaffold no-packet control had to be executed, so that any ``packet isolation matters'' statement was not resting on unmatched topologies.
\item Explicit handoff timing had to be repaired, so that pre-handoff metrics were tied to an actual latch event rather than to a store-voltage proxy.
\item The confidence-gated controller had to beat \Blind on the pre-registered late-arrival near-tie family without broadening the claim to a new best-overall selector.
\end{enumerate}
All three conditions were met, but only narrowly. That is why the paper's central result is a design law about temporal separability rather than a new universal winner.

\section{Theoretical Results and Proofs}
The theoretical section proves only the statements that the implemented behavioral netlists actually support. It does \emph{not} attempt a theorem for startup probability across the full nonlinear transient system. The resulting proofs are narrower but exact.

\begin{lemma}[Confidence-state dynamics]
\label{lem:dynamics}
Let $q_A(t)$ and $q_B(t)$ denote the scout capacitor voltages and let $c(t)$ denote the confidence-accumulator voltage in the implemented \Confidence deck. Then
\begin{align}
  \taus \dot q_A(t)+q_A(t) &= u_A(t), \\
  \taus \dot q_B(t)+q_B(t) &= u_B(t), \\
  \tauc \dot c(t)+c(t) &= r(t),
\end{align}
with $\taus=\SI{0.75}{\milli\second}$, $\tauc=\SI{50}{\milli\second}$, and $0\le r(t)\le 1$ for all $t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In the shared netlist, scout node $q_A$ is implemented by the resistor-capacitor pair \texttt{RSCOUT\_A} and \texttt{CSCOUT\_A}. The ideal observation source \texttt{EOBS\_A} enforces $V(a_{\mathrm{obs}})=u_A(t)$. The resistor current from the observation node into the scout capacitor is therefore
\begin{equation}
  i_{R_A}(t)=\frac{u_A(t)-q_A(t)}{R_{\mathrm{scout}}}.
\end{equation}
The scout capacitor current is
\begin{equation}
  i_{C_A}(t)=C_{\mathrm{scout}}\dot q_A(t).
\end{equation}
Kirchhoff's current law at the scout node gives $i_{R_A}(t)=i_{C_A}(t)$, hence
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{scout}}\dot q_A(t)=\frac{u_A(t)-q_A(t)}{R_{\mathrm{scout}}}.
\end{equation}
Multiplying by $R_{\mathrm{scout}}$ yields
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{scout}}C_{\mathrm{scout}}\dot q_A(t)+q_A(t)=u_A(t).
\end{equation}
Since $\taus=R_{\mathrm{scout}}C_{\mathrm{scout}}$, the first equation follows. The equation for $q_B$ is identical by symmetry.

For the confidence state, the source \texttt{BCONF\_RAW} fixes node \texttt{conf\_raw} at the voltage $r(t)$ defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rawconfidence}. The resistor \texttt{RCONF} connects \texttt{conf\_raw} to the accumulator node \texttt{conf\_acc}, whose voltage is $c(t)$, and \texttt{CCONF} connects \texttt{conf\_acc} to ground. The resistor current into the accumulator is therefore
\begin{equation}
  i_{R_c}(t)=\frac{r(t)-c(t)}{R_{\mathrm{conf}}},
\end{equation}
and the capacitor current is
\begin{equation}
  i_{C_c}(t)=C_{\mathrm{conf}}\dot c(t).
\end{equation}
Applying Kirchhoff's current law at the accumulator node yields
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{conf}}\dot c(t)=\frac{r(t)-c(t)}{R_{\mathrm{conf}}},
\end{equation}
or equivalently
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{conf}}C_{\mathrm{conf}}\dot c(t)+c(t)=r(t).
\end{equation}
Since $\tauc=R_{\mathrm{conf}}C_{\mathrm{conf}}$, the third state equation follows.

It remains to show $0\le r(t)\le 1$. The factor
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{1}\{|q_A(t)|+|q_B(t)|>V_{\mathrm{floor}}\}
\end{equation}
is either $0$ or $1$. The ratio
\begin{equation}
  \frac{|q_A(t)-q_B(t)|}{\epsilon + |q_A(t)|+|q_B(t)|}
\end{equation}
is nonnegative because numerator and denominator are nonnegative and the denominator is strictly positive since $\epsilon>0$. The outer minimum with $1$ therefore returns a value in $[0,1]$. Multiplying by the indicator preserves the interval $[0,1]$. Hence $0\le r(t)\le 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Static-abstention bound]
\label{thm:abstention}
Fix a time horizon $T>0$ and suppose the raw confidence obeys $r(t)\le \rho$ for every $t\in[0,T]$, where $0\le \rho<\Theta$ and $\Theta=0.08$ is the implemented commit threshold. Assume $c(0)=0$. Then, for every $t\in[0,T]$,
\begin{equation}
  c(t)\le \rho\left(1-e^{-t/\tauc}\right)<\Theta.
  \label{eq:cupper}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the commit state
\begin{equation}
  m(t)=\frac{1+\tanh\!\bigl(k_{\mathrm{c}}(c(t)-\Theta)\bigr)}{2}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
  m(t)\le m_{\rho}\coloneqq \frac{1+\tanh\!\bigl(k_{\mathrm{c}}(\rho-\Theta)\bigr)}{2}<\frac12,
\end{equation}
and the selector remains within
\begin{equation}
  \left|w_A(t)-\frac12\right| \le \frac{m_{\rho}}{2},
  \qquad
  \left|w_B(t)-\frac12\right| \le \frac{m_{\rho}}{2}
  \label{eq:blinddeviation}
\end{equation}
of the blind packet weights.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:dynamics} gives the linear state equation
\begin{equation}
  \tauc \dot c(t)+c(t)=r(t),\qquad c(0)=0.
\end{equation}
The variation-of-constants formula for this first-order linear differential equation is
\begin{equation}
  c(t)=e^{-t/\tauc}c(0)+\frac{1}{\tauc}\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)/\tauc}r(s)\,ds.
  \label{eq:variation}
\end{equation}
Substituting $c(0)=0$ yields
\begin{equation}
  c(t)=\frac{1}{\tauc}\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)/\tauc}r(s)\,ds.
\end{equation}
Since $r(s)\le \rho$ for every $s\in[0,t]$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
  c(t)\le \frac{\rho}{\tauc}\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)/\tauc}\,ds.
\end{equation}
The integral can be evaluated exactly:
\begin{align}
  \frac{1}{\tauc}\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)/\tauc}\,ds
   &= \frac{1}{\tauc}\int_0^t e^{-u/\tauc}\,du \qquad (u=t-s) \\
   &= \frac{1}{\tauc}\left[-\tauc e^{-u/\tauc}\right]_{u=0}^{u=t} \\
   &= 1-e^{-t/\tauc}.
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
  c(t)\le \rho\left(1-e^{-t/\tauc}\right).
\end{equation}
Because $0\le 1-e^{-t/\tauc}<1$ and $\rho<\Theta$, we obtain the strict bound $c(t)<\Theta$, which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:cupper}.

The hyperbolic tangent is strictly increasing, so $c(t)\le \rho$ implies
\begin{equation}
  \tanh\!\bigl(k_{\mathrm{c}}(c(t)-\Theta)\bigr)\le \tanh\!\bigl(k_{\mathrm{c}}(\rho-\Theta)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Adding $1$ to both sides and dividing by $2$ preserves the inequality, hence
\begin{equation}
  m(t)\le \frac{1+\tanh\!\bigl(k_{\mathrm{c}}(\rho-\Theta)\bigr)}{2}=m_{\rho}.
\end{equation}
Because $\rho-\Theta<0$ and $\tanh(x)<0$ for every negative $x$, we have $m_{\rho}<1/2$.

It remains to bound the deviation from the blind packet weights. Define
\begin{equation}
  a(t)=\frac{1+\tanh\!\bigl(k_{\mathrm{s}}(q_A(t)-q_B(t))\bigr)}{2}.
\end{equation}
Since $\tanh$ maps $\mathbb{R}$ into $(-1,1)$, it follows that $a(t)\in(0,1)$ for every $t$. The implemented weight law is
\begin{equation}
  w_A(t)=\frac{1-m(t)}{2}+m(t)a(t).
\end{equation}
Subtracting $1/2$ gives
\begin{equation}
  w_A(t)-\frac12 = m(t)\left(a(t)-\frac12\right).
\end{equation}
Taking absolute values yields
\begin{equation}
  \left|w_A(t)-\frac12\right| = m(t)\left|a(t)-\frac12\right|.
\end{equation}
Since $a(t)\in(0,1)$, we have $|a(t)-1/2|\le 1/2$, so
\begin{equation}
  \left|w_A(t)-\frac12\right| \le \frac{m(t)}{2}\le \frac{m_{\rho}}{2}.
\end{equation}
Finally, $w_B(t)=1-w_A(t)$ implies
\begin{equation}
  \left|w_B(t)-\frac12\right| = \left|\frac12-w_A(t)\right| = \left|w_A(t)-\frac12\right| \le \frac{m_{\rho}}{2}.
\end{equation}
This proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:blinddeviation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Commitment-latency bound under sustained separability]
\label{thm:latency}
Let $t_0\ge 0$ and assume that $r(t)\ge \rho$ for every $t\in[t_0,T]$, where $\rho>\Theta$. Let $c_0=c(t_0)$. Then, for every $t\in[t_0,T]$,
\begin{equation}
  c(t)\ge \rho - (\rho-c_0)e^{-(t-t_0)/\tauc}.
  \label{eq:clower}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $c_0<\Theta$, then every time
\begin{equation}
  t \ge t_0 + \tauc \ln\!\left(\frac{\rho-c_0}{\rho-\Theta}\right)
  \label{eq:commitbound}
\end{equation}
satisfies $c(t)\ge \Theta$, and therefore $m(t)\ge 1/2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Starting from Eq.~\eqref{eq:variation}, but now using $t_0$ as the initial time, we obtain
\begin{equation}
  c(t)=e^{-(t-t_0)/\tauc}c_0+\frac{1}{\tauc}\int_{t_0}^t e^{-(t-s)/\tauc}r(s)\,ds
  \qquad \text{for } t\ge t_0.
\end{equation}
Since $r(s)\ge \rho$ on $[t_0,t]$,
\begin{equation}
  c(t)\ge e^{-(t-t_0)/\tauc}c_0+\frac{\rho}{\tauc}\int_{t_0}^t e^{-(t-s)/\tauc}\,ds.
\end{equation}
The integral evaluates exactly as before:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{\tauc}\int_{t_0}^t e^{-(t-s)/\tauc}\,ds = 1-e^{-(t-t_0)/\tauc}.
\end{equation}
Substituting this identity yields
\begin{align}
  c(t)
   &\ge e^{-(t-t_0)/\tauc}c_0+\rho\left(1-e^{-(t-t_0)/\tauc}\right) \\
   &= \rho - (\rho-c_0)e^{-(t-t_0)/\tauc},
\end{align}
which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:clower}.

Now assume $c_0<\Theta<\rho$. Because $\rho-\Theta>0$ and $\rho-c_0>0$, the logarithm in Eq.~\eqref{eq:commitbound} is well defined. If
\begin{equation}
  t \ge t_0 + \tauc \ln\!\left(\frac{\rho-c_0}{\rho-\Theta}\right),
\end{equation}
then dividing by $\tauc$ and exponentiating the negated inequality gives
\begin{equation}
  e^{-(t-t_0)/\tauc} \le \frac{\rho-\Theta}{\rho-c_0}.
\end{equation}
Multiplying both sides by the positive factor $\rho-c_0$ yields
\begin{equation}
  (\rho-c_0)e^{-(t-t_0)/\tauc} \le \rho-\Theta.
\end{equation}
Subtracting both sides from $\rho$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \rho-(\rho-c_0)e^{-(t-t_0)/\tauc} \ge \Theta.
\end{equation}
Combining this inequality with Eq.~\eqref{eq:clower} yields $c(t)\ge \Theta$. The commit signal is
\begin{equation}
  m(t)=\frac{1+\tanh\!\bigl(k_{\mathrm{c}}(c(t)-\Theta)\bigr)}{2}.
\end{equation}
Since $c(t)-\Theta\ge 0$ and $\tanh(x)\ge 0$ for every $x\ge 0$, it follows that $m(t)\ge 1/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dynamic monitor-path control-energy law]
\label{thm:ctrl}
Let $\ell(t)$ denote the exported handoff latch on $[0,T_{\mathrm{stop}}]$, and define the repaired monitor-path control energy by
\begin{equation}
  \Ectrl =
  \int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}}
  \operatorname{pos}\!\bigl(\vstore(t)I(\texttt{VCTRL\_MON})(t)\bigr)\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt.
  \label{eq:ctrlmetric}
\end{equation}
Then the blind packet deck satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \Ectrl^{(\Blind)} = \Gscout \int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}} \vstore(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt,
  \qquad
  \Gscout = \SI{40}{\nano\siemens}.
  \label{eq:blindenergy}
\end{equation}
For the \Confidence deck,
\begin{equation}
  \Ectrl^{(\Confidence)} =
  \int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}}
  \left[\Gscout + \Gconf\left(0.25 + 0.75m(t)\right)\right]\vstore(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt,
  \qquad
  \Gconf = \SI{8}{\nano\siemens}.
  \label{eq:confenergy}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
  42\,\mathrm{nS}\int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}}\vstore(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt
  \le
  \Ectrl^{(\Confidence)}
  \le
  48\,\mathrm{nS}\int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}}\vstore(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt.
  \label{eq:energyband}
\end{equation}
If a run closes once and never reopens, then $\ell(t)=0$ before the closure time and $\ell(t)=1$ afterward, so Eqs.~\eqref{eq:blindenergy}--\eqref{eq:energyband} reduce to the simpler pre-first-handoff integrals over $[0,\tau]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:ctrlmetric} is the repaired measurement contract. The ideal zero-volt source \texttt{VCTRL\_MON} forces
\begin{equation}
  V(n_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)) = V(n_{\mathrm{store}}(t)) = \vstore(t).
  \label{eq:ctrlstoreequal}
\end{equation}
We evaluate the current through \texttt{VCTRL\_MON} by inspecting each deck's control branch.

For the blind packet gate, the only control sink is
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{blind}}(t)=\Gscout V(n_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)).
\end{equation}
Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:ctrlstoreequal} gives
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{blind}}(t)=\Gscout \vstore(t).
\end{equation}
This current is nonnegative because $\Gscout>0$ and $\vstore(t)\ge 0$ throughout the simulated startup interval. Therefore the outer $\operatorname{pos}$ operator is inactive and
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Blind)}(t)=\Gscout \vstore(t)^2(1-\ell(t)).
\end{equation}
Integrating from $0$ to $T_{\mathrm{stop}}$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:blindenergy}.

For the \Confidence deck, the blind packet sink is still present, and the implementation adds
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{conf,extra}}(t)=\Gconf V(n_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t))\left(0.25+0.75m(t)\right).
\end{equation}
Again substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:ctrlstoreequal} gives
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{conf,extra}}(t)=\Gconf \vstore(t)\left(0.25+0.75m(t)\right).
\end{equation}
The total control current is therefore
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{conf,total}}(t)=
  \left[\Gscout + \Gconf\left(0.25+0.75m(t)\right)\right]\vstore(t).
\end{equation}
Since $\Gscout>0$, $\Gconf>0$, and $m(t)\in[0,1]$, the bracketed coefficient is nonnegative. Hence the $\operatorname{pos}$ operator is again inactive and
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{(\Confidence)}(t)=
  \left[\Gscout + \Gconf\left(0.25+0.75m(t)\right)\right]\vstore(t)^2(1-\ell(t)).
\end{equation}
Integrating from $0$ to $T_{\mathrm{stop}}$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:confenergy}.

Finally, because $m(t)\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
  0.25 \le 0.25+0.75m(t)\le 1.
\end{equation}
Multiplying by $\Gconf=\SI{8}{\nano\siemens}$ and adding $\Gscout=\SI{40}{\nano\siemens}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \SI{42}{\nano\siemens}\le \Gscout+\Gconf\left(0.25+0.75m(t)\right)\le \SI{48}{\nano\siemens}.
\end{equation}
Multiplying the inequality by the nonnegative quantity $\vstore(t)^2(1-\ell(t))$ and integrating from $0$ to $T_{\mathrm{stop}}$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:energyband}. If $\ell(t)$ makes exactly one transition from $0$ to $1$, then the factor $(1-\ell(t))$ is the indicator of $[0,\tau)$, which gives the stated single-close reduction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Matched-trajectory control-energy ratio band]
\label{cor:ratio}
Suppose a \Confidence run and a \Blind run share the same store-voltage trajectory $v(t)$ and the same latch trace $\ell(t)$ on $[0,T_{\mathrm{stop}}]$, and suppose
\begin{equation}
  \int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}} v(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt > 0.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  1.05 \le \frac{\Ectrl^{(\Confidence)}}{\Ectrl^{(\Blind)}} \le 1.20.
  \label{eq:ratioband}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Under the matched-trajectory assumption, Eq.~\eqref{eq:blindenergy} gives
\begin{equation}
  \Ectrl^{(\Blind)} = 40\,\mathrm{nS}\int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}} v(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt,
\end{equation}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:energyband} gives
\begin{equation}
  42\,\mathrm{nS}\int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}} v(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt
  \le
  \Ectrl^{(\Confidence)}
  \le
  48\,\mathrm{nS}\int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}} v(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt.
\end{equation}
Dividing all three terms by the positive denominator $\Ectrl^{(\Blind)}=40\,\mathrm{nS}\int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}} v(t)^2(1-\ell(t))\,dt$ yields
\begin{equation}
  \frac{42}{40}\le \frac{\Ectrl^{(\Confidence)}}{\Ectrl^{(\Blind)}} \le \frac{48}{40},
\end{equation}
which is exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:ratioband}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Only merge-style decks create an explicit pre-handoff branch join]
\label{prop:merge}
Among the DEEPEN decks $\{\Confidence,\Blind,\TCRanked,\Merge\}$, only \Merge contains a finite-resistance pre-handoff path from the routed branch-A bus to the routed branch-B bus.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the \Merge implementation, the positive buses are connected by two explicit resistors:
\begin{equation}
  a_p \xleftrightarrow{R_{\mathrm{merge}}=\SI{5}{\ohm}} \mathrm{merge}_p
  \xleftrightarrow{R_{\mathrm{merge}}=\SI{5}{\ohm}} b_p.
\end{equation}
Therefore the positive routed buses are connected by a finite-resistance chain of total resistance \SI{10}{\ohm}. The negative buses are connected by the analogous \SI{10}{\ohm} chain. Hence \Merge has an explicit pre-handoff branch join.

For \Blind, \Confidence, and \TCRanked, the routed branch nodes appear only in three places: observer sources that copy local branch voltage, local current-source pump terms that depend on the same branch voltage, and the shared store node. No resistor, switch, or other finite-conductance element directly connects branch A to branch B before handoff. A controlled current source that injects current based on $V(a_p,a_n)$ does not create a finite-resistance path to the branch-B bus, because its constitutive law depends only on the local branch voltage and the store node, not on a direct branch-to-branch conduction channel. Therefore none of the packet-isolated DEEPEN decks contains a finite-resistance branch join.
\end{proof}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Context Suites}
The context evidence that motivated DEEPEN uses two deterministic suites and one robustness suite.

\textbf{Primary startup matrix.} The 24-case matrix spans nominal open-circuit voltages $\{20,50,100,300\}\,\mathrm{mV}$, ramp rates $\{0.1,1,10,100\}\,\mathrm{mV/s}$, impedance ratios $\{1,5,20\}$, and same or mixed polarity. It is a budgeted coverage matrix, not a full factorial. The active designs are $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ctx}}$.

\textbf{Falsifier suite.} The 10-case deterministic falsifier pack targets source collapse, mixed polarity, unequal open-circuit voltage, finite off-isolation leak, and repaired UVLO chatter. The cases are \texttt{fa\_001} through \texttt{fa\_010}, and Appendix~\ref{app:cases} gives the detailed manifest.

\textbf{Robustness suite.} Four context cases are rerun under randomized perturbations: \texttt{fa\_001}, \texttt{fa\_004}, \texttt{fa\_005}, and \texttt{sm\_015}. Each design-case pair uses 24 independent samples. The executed design set is $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ plus \Merge and \Confidence, so that the corrected context frontier and the new controller appear under the same sampled separators. The randomized parameters include source resistances, store capacitance, startup dead zone, controller $RC$ values, leak resistance in leak-path cases, collapse timing, and collapse scales. The seed formula is
\begin{equation}
  s = 20260312 + 10000\,o_c + 1000\,o_d + n,
\end{equation}
where $o_c$ is the case offset, $o_d$ is the design offset, and $n\in\{0,\dots,23\}$ is the sample index.

\subsection{DEEPEN Near-Tie Matrix}
The DEEPEN matrix is intentionally small. Its purpose is not to redraw the full startup envelope, but to answer one sharply bounded question: when does a confidence-gated controller help relative to the blind packet baseline?

The 12 core cases are the Cartesian product of
\begin{enumerate}
\item family: static or late arrival,
\item voltage pair: $(100,101)\,\mathrm{mV}$, $(100,103)\,\mathrm{mV}$, or $(100,105)\,\mathrm{mV}$,
\item impedance ratio: $1:1$ or $1:3$,
\item same polarity and ramp rate \SI{10}{\milli\volt\per\second}.
\end{enumerate}
In the late family, source $B$ is suppressed until \SI{4}{\second}. The core compared designs are $\{\Confidence,\Blind,\TCRanked\}$. Four additional decisive controls add \Merge on the two $V_B-V_A=\SI{3}{\milli\volt}$ cases at both impedance ratios and both temporal families.

\subsection{Near-Tie Matrix Reveals a Temporal-Separability Law}
The DEEPEN near-tie matrix answers that question directly. Table~\ref{tab:deepen-core} and Figure~\ref{fig:deepen} show a clear regime split.

On \emph{static} near ties, the new controller behaves almost exactly like the blind baseline. Both \Confidence and \Blind start all $6/6$ cases. The median handoff-time difference is only $-0.000545\,\mathrm{s}$, and the controller commits in $0/6$ cases. Theorem~\ref{thm:abstention} is the right interpretation here: when the normalized separability signal stays sub-threshold, the selector is provably confined near the blind packet posture.

On \emph{late-arrival} near ties, the story changes. Again both designs start all $6/6$ cases, but \Confidence now commits in $6/6$ cases with median $\tcommit=1.02799\,\mathrm{s}$ and improves median handoff time by $0.30882\,\mathrm{s}$ relative to \Blind. Theorem~\ref{thm:latency} explains why that behavior is expected: the delayed source creates a persistent separability window while source $A$ is active alone, so the integrator has time to cross threshold before the second source arrives.

\subsection{Theory and Measurement Agree on What the Controller Is Paying For}
Theoretical predictions from Section~5 line up with the measured data in a way that is too structured to be accidental.

First, Theorem~\ref{thm:ctrl} and Corollary~\ref{cor:ratio} predict that, for matched trajectories, \Confidence must pay between $1.05\times$ and $1.20\times$ the monitor-path pre-handoff control energy of \Blind. The static-family median ratio is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{5.28058\times10^{-8}}{5.01102\times10^{-8}} = 1.0538,
\end{equation}
which is essentially the lower edge of the matched-trajectory band. This is exactly what one would expect from Theorem~\ref{thm:abstention}: static near ties rarely activate the extra confidence burden beyond its minimum. The late-family median ratio is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{5.14936\times10^{-8}}{4.35393\times10^{-8}} = 1.1827,
\end{equation}
which remains inside the $[1.05,1.20]$ band and moves toward the upper edge, consistent with sustained commitment activity.

Second, Theorem~\ref{thm:latency} predicts that commitment occurs only after the raw separability signal stays above threshold long enough to charge the confidence node. The measured late-family median $\tcommit=1.02799\,\mathrm{s}$ is qualitatively consistent with that mechanism: commitment occurs early enough to help handoff, but not instantaneously. The controller is integrating evidence, not acting on a single transient excursion.

The agreement is not perfect and should not be oversold. The theorems are structural netlist theorems, whereas the reported ratios are family medians over deterministic cases with slightly different store trajectories. The claim is therefore modest: the measured numbers occupy the regions that the implemented equations say they should occupy.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The paper's main message is not that another source-aware controller wins. The main message is that startup-control research benefits from a tighter causal ladder. First execute the same-family ``do less'' ablation. Then execute the same-scaffold no-packet control. Then rerun the fragile rows after implementation repairs and sampled perturbations. Only after that should a new mechanism be granted a live niche. In this repository, that ladder changed the conclusion completely. Neither the original \RCRanked story nor the earlier blind-only story survived. A narrower \Confidence result did.

That surviving result is scientifically useful because it is not just a null and not just an unconstrained frontier plot. It isolates a design law that an analog designer can actually act on:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If weak sources remain statically ambiguous, stay close to the blind packet posture. The controller should not spend startup energy pretending to know what the evidence does not support.
\item If one source is present early and the other arrives later, commit during that separability window. The controller can recover part of the blind baseline's delay without reopening the backdrive of a merged startup path.
\item If the dominant stress is heavy-load mixed conflict or leak, blind packet isolation alone is not enough; the repaired context pack favors ranked packet-isolated controllers such as \TCRanked or \RCRanked on those separators.
\item If absolute speed matters more than isolation, a merged path is still faster, but the wrong-way energy is no longer zero and must be accounted for explicitly.
\item If a ranked controller is desired independent of abstention, the lower-overhead \TCRanked deck remains faster than \Confidence in the executed matrix, so the paper does not support promoting \Confidence to a universal default.
\end{enumerate}

Several limitations are important and should not be hidden.
\begin{itemize}
\item The study is simulation-only and uses behavioral current-source abstractions for the startup pump. The paper therefore offers a circuit-family mechanism result, not a silicon demonstration.
\item The DEEPEN matrix is intentionally small: 12 core cases plus 4 control cases. That is enough to support a bounded operating-regime claim, but not a field-wide envelope map.
\item No literature-faithful external comparator was executed under the repaired hooks. The paper therefore makes no measured superiority claim against prior silicon.
\item The confidence node remains a behavioral macro, not a transistor-level implementation. Silicon relevance requires a future hardware-plausible analog realization.
\item The robustness study was not rerun for every DEEPEN separator case. The context boundary is robustness-qualified; the confidence-gated late-arrival result is not yet.
\end{itemize}

These limitations matter because they bound what the paper is allowed to claim. The work is not a new PMU platform. It is not a first-of-its-kind proof that the literature lacks abstention. It is not a best-overall selector paper. The accurate statement is narrower: the recovered overlap set did not reveal a close same-family abstention-like pre-handoff controller, and the executed evidence shows that abstention is useful only when ambiguity resolves over time.

The broader implication is methodological. Circuit papers often reward only positive stories: lower startup voltage, higher efficiency, more autonomy. This project shows the value of publishing a bounded negative-to-positive refinement instead. The negative part removes unsupported complexity. The positive part tells the designer when a little extra complexity is actually justified. For intermittent and batteryless systems, that kind of disciplined simplification can be more valuable than one more loosely benchmarked architecture claim.

\section{Conclusion}
The executed helper-free packet-gated startup family does not support a broad ``smarter pre-handoff ranking wins'' story, but it also does not support a universal blind story. The context pack is a five-way tie at $17/24$ startups on the primary matrix, the repaired falsifier suite yields a frontier with \TCRanked at $9/10$ and \Blind at $5/10$, and the sampled robustness pack preserves the same decisive separators. The surviving positive result is narrower and more useful. A confidence-gated abstention controller should stay near the blind packet posture on static near ties, but commit when temporal separability appears. In the DEEPEN matrix, that rule produces no benefit on static ambiguity, a $0.30882\,\mathrm{s}$ median handoff gain over \Blind on late-arrival near ties, and zero measured backdrive on the decisive same-scaffold controls. The resulting contribution is not a new PMU architecture. It is a design law for when helper-free startup should stay blind and when it should act.

\label{app:sensitivity}

\subsection{Commit-Latency Sensitivity}
The bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:latency} can be written as
\begin{equation}
  t_{\mathrm{commit}}^{\max}(\rho,\Theta,\tauc,c_0)
  =
  t_0 + \tauc \ln\!\left(\frac{\rho-c_0}{\rho-\Theta}\right).
  \label{eq:tmax}
\end{equation}
Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:tmax} with respect to its parameters yields exact monotonicity statements.

\begin{proposition}[Sensitivity of the commitment bound]
\label{prop:commit-sensitivity}
Assume $c_0<\Theta<\rho$. Then
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial t_{\mathrm{commit}}^{\max}}{\partial \tauc}
    &= \ln\!\left(\frac{\rho-c_0}{\rho-\Theta}\right) > 0, \\
  \frac{\partial t_{\mathrm{commit}}^{\max}}{\partial \Theta}
    &= \frac{\tauc}{\rho-\Theta} > 0, \\
  \frac{\partial t_{\mathrm{commit}}^{\max}}{\partial \rho}
    &= \tauc\left(\frac{1}{\rho-c_0}-\frac{1}{\rho-\Theta}\right)
     = \tauc \frac{c_0-\Theta}{(\rho-c_0)(\rho-\Theta)} < 0.
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $c_0<\Theta<\rho$, the fraction $(\rho-c_0)/(\rho-\Theta)$ is greater than one, so its logarithm is positive. This proves the first derivative statement. Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:tmax} with respect to $\Theta$ while holding the other parameters fixed gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial t_{\mathrm{commit}}^{\max}}{\partial \Theta}
  = \tauc \frac{\partial}{\partial \Theta}
    \left[-\ln(\rho-\Theta)\right]
  = \tauc \frac{1}{\rho-\Theta},
\end{equation}
which is positive because $\rho>\Theta$.

For the derivative with respect to $\rho$,
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial t_{\mathrm{commit}}^{\max}}{\partial \rho}
   &= \tauc \frac{\partial}{\partial \rho}
      \left[\ln(\rho-c_0)-\ln(\rho-\Theta)\right] \\
   &= \tauc\left(\frac{1}{\rho-c_0}-\frac{1}{\rho-\Theta}\right) \\
   &= \tauc \frac{(\rho-\Theta)-(\rho-c_0)}{(\rho-c_0)(\rho-\Theta)} \\
   &= \tauc \frac{c_0-\Theta}{(\rho-c_0)(\rho-\Theta)}.
\end{align}
The denominator is positive because $\rho>c_0$ and $\rho>\Theta$. The numerator is negative because $c_0<\Theta$. Hence the derivative is negative.
\end{proof}

The proposition formalizes the intuitive design tradeoffs. Increasing the integrator time constant $\tauc$ or the commit threshold $\Theta$ delays commitment and makes the controller more conservative. Increasing the sustained separability floor $\rho$ accelerates commitment.

\subsection{Control-Energy Sensitivity}
Theorem~\ref{thm:ctrl} shows that the incremental monitor-path control-energy cost of \Confidence is linear in $\Gconf$ once the store trajectory, latch trace, and commit history are fixed.

\begin{proposition}[Sensitivity of control energy to the extra confidence sink]
\label{prop:conf-sensitivity}
For a fixed startup trajectory $\vstore(t)$, fixed commit trace $m(t)$, and fixed latch trace $\ell(t)$ over $[0,T_{\mathrm{stop}}]$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \Ectrl^{(\Confidence)}}{\partial \Gconf}
  =
  \int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}}\left(0.25+0.75m(t)\right)\vstore(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt > 0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:confenergy} is affine in $\Gconf$:
\begin{equation}
  \Ectrl^{(\Confidence)}
  =
  \Gscout \int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}} \vstore(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt
  +
  \Gconf
  \int_0^{T_{\mathrm{stop}}} \left(0.25+0.75m(t)\right)\vstore(t)^2\bigl(1-\ell(t)\bigr)\,dt.
\end{equation}
Differentiating with respect to $\Gconf$ immediately yields the stated integral. The integrand is nonnegative because $m(t)\in[0,1]$, $\vstore(t)^2\ge 0$, and $1-\ell(t)\ge 0$. It is strictly positive on every successful startup interval because $\vstore(t)$ is positive over a set of nonzero measure while the latch is still open, and the factor $(0.25+0.75m(t))$ is bounded below by $0.25$.
\end{proof}

\section{Novelty-Boundary Audit Notes}
The repository that launched this run began from a deliberately broad natural-language task. These items were retained only as audit artifacts showing why the seed query had to be cleaned. They are not part of the technical prior-art boundary and were not used to justify the paper's claim.

The actual novelty boundary is therefore the one stated in Table~\ref{tab:priorwork} and in Section~\ref{sec:related}: strong overlap exists with adaptive dual-source startup and autonomous multi-input PMUs, but the recovered overlap set did not reveal a close same-family abstention-like pre-handoff controller. That sentence is intentionally scoped. It is a retrieval result for the recovered overlap set, not a field-wide absence proof.
