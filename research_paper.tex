\begin{abstract}
We study a repository-level attempt to solve the Hadamard order-$668$ problem using the phrase ``cellar automata'' as a design prompt, subject to a strict evidence rule: every substantive claim must be justified either by a formal proof or by a reproducible measurement on checked-in artifacts. The repository first interpreted the prompt as ordinary cellular automata and ran two matched-control branches on the exact length-$167$, weight-$80$ cyclic obstruction of \citet{constantine2025cyclic} and the published mod-$64$ order-$668$ seed of \citet{eliahou2025mod64}. Those branches do not survive review: on the exact-$167/80$ target, the matched non-CA baseline \DG{} beats \CA{} on all $24/24$ paired starts, and on the decisive real mod-$64$ seed repair test the two methods tie exactly on the best achieved modulus, $\ell_1$ defect, defect count, and maximum defect magnitude.

We then execute the literal cellar interpretation instead of leaving it as a slogan. The implemented Phase~6 branch fixes a canonical tail-panel encoding for binary supports, scans the free suffix left-to-right, and maintains a dyadic pushdown-style stack of block summaries together with a matched non-stack residual state, the \emph{boundary-debt summary}. On this encoding we prove an exact decomposition theorem for the periodic half-autocorrelation, show that every prefix coordinate is exposed to some unread-bit interaction at every cut, and prove that the boundary-debt state determines the exact completion set of any prefix. Consequently the exact extendability problem for the implemented panels collapses to finite residual state memory, and visibly pushdown memory is unnecessary for exact correctness on this encoding.

The empirical packet is also negative. On solved same-template control panels of free-tail lengths $10$, $12$, and $14$, the exact extendable frontier has sizes $11$, $13$, and $15$, and the matched static comparator attains exactly the same frontier in every case with zero false negatives. Moreover the boundary-debt signature is already prefix-identifying on those panels, with group counts equal to all weight-feasible prefixes: $494$, $2001$, and $4367$. On four exact-$167/80$ tail panels selected from the best recorded $\mathrm{H1}$ states and on four degraded order-$668$ seed projections, the analysis scans between $1000$ and $3431$ weight-feasible prefixes per panel and finds zero exact completions, zero boundary-debt frontier, and zero cellar-stack frontier. The strongest justified conclusion is therefore narrow but clean: under the exact tail-panel encoding implemented here, literal cellar memory yields no surviving advantage over the matched static residual state, so the branch should be retired rather than promoted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Recent work gives the unresolved Hadamard-$668$ search problem two unusually sharp exact anchors \citep{eliahou2025mod64}. On the cyclic side, \citet{constantine2025cyclic} isolate an unresolved length-$167$, weight-$80$ support with a fully specified periodic autocorrelation target. On the modular side, \citet{eliahou2025mod64} construct a $64$-modular Hadamard matrix of order $668$, providing a concrete structured near-solution but not an exact Hadamard certificate. Those anchors are ideal for controlled method evaluation because they freeze both the state representation and the verifier before search begins.

The repository studied here used those anchors in two stages. The first stage implemented two flat cellular-automata style local-search branches. The first branch, $\mathrm{H1}$, worked directly in the support space of the exact $167/80$ cyclic obstruction. The second branch, $\mathrm{H2}$, worked in the structured $(q,s)$ representation induced by the published mod-$64$ seed. Both branches were paired with a matched same-representation non-CA baseline, \DG{}, and both failed under the repository's pre-registered kill rules. That left one materially different reopen candidate: interpret ``cellar automata'' literally as a pushdown-style prefix automaton over exact tail panels instead of as another local-update schedule on the same neighborhoods.\repoevidence{\path{results/research_context.json}; \path{results/concept_evolve/recurrent_state.json}; \path{results/concept_evolve/bridge_candidates.json}.}

This paper is about that literal reopen. The repository's Phase~6 work did not merely rename the branch. It fixed a canonical dihedral representative of each support, exposed only a contiguous free suffix, scanned that suffix left-to-right, and maintained two matched residual representations: a dyadic cellar stack and a static boundary-debt summary.\repoevidence{\path{results/swarm/director_brief.md}; \path{results/swarm/hypotheses.json}; \path{results/analysis/cellar_no_go.md}.} The key scientific question is not whether the stack is evocative, but whether it carries exact completion information that the matched static summary does not. The paper answers that question both formally and empirically for the encoding that was actually implemented.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item We formalize the executed literal cellar branch as an exact tail-panel automaton with canonical tokenization, dyadic stack updates, a matched static boundary-debt comparator, and a shared exact tail-completion oracle.
  \item We prove complete decomposition and sufficiency results for the implemented encoding: every assigned coordinate is exposed at every cut, the periodic half-autocorrelation splits into assigned, crossing, wrap, and suffix-internal terms, and the boundary-debt state determines the exact completion set of any prefix.
  \item We preserve the earlier $\mathrm{H1}/\mathrm{H2}$ packet as context and show why the literal cellar reopen was warranted at all: the support-space CA loses decisively to its matched baseline, while the structured seed-repair CA ties on the only real order-$668$ seed test.
  \item We report an exact no-go for the implemented cellar encoding. On solved $4\times79$ control panels of free-tail lengths $10$, $12$, and $14$, the static frontier is already exact and prefix-identifying; on four exact-$167/80$ tail panels and four degraded order-$668$ seed projections, no exact completion or stack-only frontier survives.
  \item We isolate what remains open. The paper does \emph{not} prove that all weaker static summaries are equivalent, that pushdown memory can never help under another tokenization, or that transfer- or proof-reuse-based reopenings are impossible. It proves only that the specific exact tail-panel encoding implemented in this repository has no surviving cellar advantage.
\end{enumerate}

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} positions the work against exact Hadamard search, CA-based design generation, automata theory, and direct Hadamard heuristics. Section~\ref{sec:background} defines the exact cyclic, modular, and tail-panel objects. Section~\ref{sec:method} gives the executed algorithms, control panels, and complexity analysis. Section~\ref{sec:theory} proves the exact residual-state results for the implemented cellar encoding. Sections~\ref{sec:setup} and \ref{sec:results} describe the experiments and their outcomes. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} states the limitations, open questions, and implications.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Exact Hadamard anchors and structural search}
The paper does not propose a new Hadamard construction. Its exact objects are imported from the primary order-$668$ anchors. The unresolved length-$167$, weight-$80$ cyclic target comes from \citet{constantine2025cyclic}; the structured mod-$64$ seed comes from \citet{eliahou2025mod64}. We use them only as fixed problem instances and verifiers, not as claims of new reduction or new modular construction.

The novelty boundary is therefore set by exact and structured Hadamard search, not by the word ``automata'' in isolation. The closest structured-search comparators are the SAT+CAS enumeration line of \citet{bright2018satcas}, Williamson-type circulant construction work such as \citet{fitzpatrick2023williamson}, and Goethals--Seidel difference-family search such as \citet{djokovic2018goethalsseidel}. Methodologically, these are the lines against which a symbolic or prefix-based Hadamard search method risks collapsing into exact pruning or family-parameter search. Broader modular-Hadamard background is summarized by \citet{eliahoukervaire2005survey} and \citet{horadam2007applications}.

\subsection{Cellular automata and automata-theoretic context}
Cellular automata are already established in adjacent design-generation tasks, so this paper does not claim that ``automata meet combinatorial design'' is new. The survey of \citet{manzoni2025survey} documents that broader context. Orthogonal Latin-square construction from cellular-automaton dynamics appears in \citet{mariot2016ols}, and CA-guided search for bent and semi-bent Boolean functions appears in \citet{gadouleau2020bent,mariot2021semibent}. Those papers do not solve the order-$668$ problem, but they are enough to block any broad novelty claim at the level of CA-based discrete search itself.

The literal Phase~6 branch is closer in spirit to automata theory than to flat local CA dynamics. The repository explicitly implements a visibly pushdown-style scan over suffix symbols, so the natural external vocabulary is that of \citet{alur2004vpl}. The substantive question, however, is more specific than the general theory of visibly pushdown languages: for the exact tail-panel encoding used here, does the pushdown stack carry any exact completion information beyond the matched static residual state? Our main theorems show that it does not.

\subsection{Direct heuristic competitors for Hadamard search}
The order-$668$ search problem already has a heuristic literature outside cellular automata. Simulated annealing, simulated quantum annealing, quantum annealing, and QAOA-style formulations have all been proposed for Hadamard search \citep{suksmono2016sa,suksmono2018sqa,suksmono2019,suksmono2022quantum,suksmono2024qaoa}. That prior art matters methodologically even though the repository does not reproduce those algorithms here. For this paper's novelty test, it means that merely wrapping a familiar local neighborhood in new language is not a publishable advance. The present paper survives only because it compares executed branches against matched same-representation controls and then narrows its conclusion to a precise no-go statement.

\subsection{How this paper differs}
Relative to the exact Hadamard literature, this paper contributes neither a new family nor a new exact solver. Relative to the CA-design literature, it contributes neither a general automata paradigm nor a new positive application. Relative to the direct heuristic literature, it contributes neither a better optimizer nor a broader benchmark. What it does contribute is narrower and, for that reason, more defensible: a formal and empirical demonstration that the specific literal cellar encoding implemented in this repository collapses to a matched static residual state on the exact tail panels that were actually executed.

\section{Background and Preliminaries}\label{sec:background}
\subsection{Hadamard and modular-Hadamard context}
\begin{definition}[Hadamard matrix]
A Hadamard matrix of order $N$ is a matrix $H \in \pmone^{N \times N}$ such that $H H^\top = N I_N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Modular Hadamard surrogate]
For a positive integer $m$, an $m$-modular Hadamard matrix of order $N$ is a sign matrix whose Gram matrix is congruent to $N I_N$ modulo $m$ \citep{eliahoukervaire2005survey,horadam2007applications}. In this repository the modular anchor is the published $64$-modular order-$668$ seed of \citet{eliahou2025mod64}.
\end{definition}

The natural block length is $n=167$, so $668=4n$ and $n=2h+1$ with $h=83$. The cyclic obstruction and the tail-panel automaton both work over binary supports on $\Z/n\Z$. The structured modular seed works over sign sequences $(q,s)\in\pmone^n\times\pmone^n$.

\subsection{Exact cyclic obstruction and precursor branches}
Let $x\in\bits^n$. For $1\le k\le h$, define the periodic binary half-autocorrelation
\begin{equation}
  c_x(k)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} x_i x_{i+k\bmod n}.
\end{equation}
The exact $\mathrm{H1}$ target fixes three binary vectors from \citet{constantine2025cyclic} and asks for a fourth support of weight $80$ whose half-autocorrelation matches a prescribed target vector $\tau=(\tau_1,\dots,\tau_h)$. The branch objective is the $\ell_1$ debt
\begin{equation}
  D_1(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{h} |c_x(k)-\tau_k|.
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:problem-objects}a plots the exact target vector.

The $\mathrm{H2}$ precursor uses the structured mod-$64$ seed of \citet{eliahou2025mod64}. The repository derives four sequences $A,B,C,D\in\pmone^n$ from $(q,s)$ and ranks states by exact certificate first, then by best two-adic modulus, then by smaller $\ell_1$ defect and supporting defect statistics.\repoevidence{\path{results/artifacts/seed_668_mod64.json}; \path{results/analysis/paper_metrics.json}; \path{hadamard668/experiments.py}.} Figure~\ref{fig:problem-objects}b plots the nonzero combined defects of the published seed before degradation.

The earlier flat CA packet matters here only because it closes the obvious local-update channels before the literal cellar reopen begins. On $\mathrm{H1}$, the support-space CA loses to the matched baseline on the solved $4\times79$ control and on the exact $167/80$ target. On $\mathrm{H2}$, the structured defect-transport CA ties the matched baseline exactly on the decisive real degraded order-$668$ seed. Sections~\ref{sec:setup} and \ref{sec:results} give the numerical details.

\subsection{Tail panels, extendable prefixes, and completion sets}
The literal cellar branch does not search the full support space directly. Instead it fixes a canonical representative and leaves only a contiguous suffix free.

\begin{definition}[Tail panel]
Fix odd length $n=2h+1$, weight $w$, target half-autocorrelation vector $\tau\in\Z^h$, and a fixed binary prefix $p\in\bits^B$ with $B>h$. Let $m=n-B$. The associated tail panel is the exact completion problem on words of the form
\begin{equation}
  z = p y, \qquad y\in\bits^m,
\end{equation}
subject to $\sum_i z_i = w$ and $c_z(k)=\tau_k$ for all $1\le k\le h$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Extendable prefix and completion set]
Let $x\in\bits^r$ with $0\le r\le m$, and let $u=m-r$. We say that $x$ is \emph{extendable} on panel $P$ if there exists $y\in\bits^u$ such that $p x y$ has weight $w$ and satisfies the exact target. The completion set of $x$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Comp_P(x)=\left\{y\in\bits^u : pxy \text{ has weight } w \text{ and } c_{pxy}=\tau\right\}.
\end{equation}
We also write $\Ext_P(x)$ for the predicate $\Comp_P(x)\neq\varnothing$.
\end{definition}

The Phase~6 control family uses solved $4\times79$ panels with free-tail lengths $10$, $12$, and $14$. The stress packet uses one exact control tail-$12$ panel, four tail-$12$ panels cut from the best recorded $\mathrm{H1}$ states, and four tail-$12$ panels obtained by projecting degraded order-$668$ seeds back into the exact $167/80$ support channel.\repoevidence{\path{results/experiments/cellar_phase6.json}.}

\subsection{Boundary-debt state and cellar stack}
The executed literal cellar branch maintains two matched residual descriptions.

\begin{definition}[Boundary-debt state]
Let $P$ be a tail panel and let $x\in\bits^r$. Write $t=B+r$ for the assigned length and $u=m-r$ for the unread suffix length. Let $a=px$ be the assigned prefix and let $a0^u$ denote the full-length word obtained by padding the unread suffix with zeros. The boundary-debt state of $x$ is
\begin{equation}
  \BD_P(x)=\bigl(r,t,|a|,w-|a|,\alpha_x,\lambda,\rho_x\bigr),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
  \alpha_x(k) &= c_{a0^u}(k), \qquad 1\le k\le h, \\
  \lambda &= (p_0,p_1,\dots,p_{h-1}), \\
  \rho_x &= (a_{t-h},a_{t-h+1},\dots,a_{t-1}).
\end{align}
Thus $\BD_P(x)$ stores the zero-padded assigned pair vector, the fixed left boundary bits, the current right boundary bits, and the exact remaining weight.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cellar stack state]
The cellar state augments $\BD_P(x)$ with a dyadic stack of block summaries. Reading the free suffix left-to-right, each incoming bit starts as a length-$1$ block; whenever the top two blocks have equal length they are merged into a parent block that stores its length, weight, bit string, boundary bit patterns, and internal linear pair vector. The resulting stack is the repository's literal ``cellar'' memory.
\end{definition}

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{From flat CA no-go to literal cellar reopen}
The repository's experimental chronology matters because it explains why the literal cellar branch received budget at all. The initial $\mathrm{H1}$ and $\mathrm{H2}$ branches were same-space, same-budget comparisons against \DG{}. The literal cellar reopen was authorized only after those branches had already failed or tied under matched controls.\repoevidence{\path{results/swarm/director_brief.md}; \path{results/swarm/hypotheses.json}; \path{results/analysis/cellar_no_go.md}.} The Phase~6 reopen therefore had a narrower burden than the earlier flat CA packet: it had to justify its \emph{different information channel}, not merely another move-selection rule on the same neighborhood.

\subsection{Exact tail-panel analysis pipeline}
For a fixed tail panel $P$, the executed analysis computes exact completions, enumerates all weight-feasible prefixes, groups prefixes by residual signature, and measures how large the resulting frontier remains when only signatures with at least one exact completion are retained. The same pipeline is applied twice, once with the static signature $\BD_P$ and once with the cellar signature $\Cellar_P$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Exact Tail-Panel Analysis for a Fixed Panel $P$}
\label{alg:panel-analysis}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Compute all exact completions $\Comp_P(\epsilon)$ by enumerating all tails of the required weight.
\State Enumerate every weight-feasible prefix $x$ of the free suffix.
\For{each prefix $x$}
  \State Compute the exact completion index set $I_P(x)$.
  \State Mark $x$ extendable iff $I_P(x)\neq\varnothing$.
  \State Compute the static signature $\BD_P(x)$.
  \State Compute the cellar signature $\Cellar_P(x)$.
\EndFor
\For{each signature family $\sigma\in\{\BD,\Cellar\}$}
  \State Group prefixes by the key $\sigma_P(x)$.
  \State Mark a group positive iff it contains at least one extendable prefix.
  \State Report frontier size as the total number of prefixes in positive groups.
  \State Report purity by checking whether any group mixes extendable and nonextendable prefixes or distinct completion sets.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The exact control family additionally evaluates held-out free-tail lengths $10$, $12$, and $14$ because the failed proof-carrying feedback layer required a $25\%$ frontier reduction at unchanged witness retention.\repoevidence{\path{research_rubric.json}; \path{results/analysis/cellar_no_go.md}.} Once the matched static frontier is already exact on that control family, the feedback objective becomes impossible by construction.

\subsection{Cellar update law}
The dynamic part of the literal cellar branch is the streaming construction of the dyadic stack. The acceptance test itself remains exact and external: a prefix is accepted only when the matched exact completion oracle says it remains extendable.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Streaming Update of the Executed Cellar State}
\label{alg:cellar-update}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Current prefix $x$, current stack $S$, incoming bit $b$.
\State Append $b$ to the free-prefix word to obtain $xb$.
\State Push a length-$1$ block summary for $b$ onto $S$.
\While{the top two stack blocks have equal length}
  \State Pop those two blocks.
  \State Merge them into their parent block and push the merged summary.
\EndWhile
\State Update the matched boundary-debt state $\BD_P(xb)$.
\State Return the pair $\bigl(\BD_P(xb),S\bigr)$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The key fairness property is that the cellar stack never receives a stronger exact oracle than the static baseline. Both views use the same canonical tokenization, the same exact completion test, the same control panels, and the same accounting of canonical prefixes. The only difference is memory discipline.

\subsection{Complexity analysis}
Let $m$ be the free-tail length and let $R$ be the required number of ones in that tail. The exact completion oracle in Algorithm~\ref{alg:panel-analysis} enumerates $\binom{m}{R}$ tails and evaluates each full word by a length-$n$ half-autocorrelation computation, so the dominant exact-oracle cost is
\begin{equation}
  O\!\left(\binom{m}{R} n h\right)=O\!\left(\binom{m}{R} n^2\right)
\end{equation}
for the naive implementation used here. The number of weight-feasible prefixes is at most $2^m$ and equals $494$, $2001$, and $4367$ on the executed control family with $m\in\{10,12,14\}$. Computing $\BD_P(x)$ for one prefix costs $O(nh)$ because the code recomputes the zero-padded half-autocorrelation directly. Building the dyadic stack from scratch for one length-$r$ prefix costs $O(hr\log r)$ in the implemented block-summary construction because each level of merges processes total block length $O(r)$ across $O(\log r)$ levels. Since the executed panels keep $m\le 14$, the exhaustive oracle rather than the streaming update dominates runtime in practice.

\section{Theoretical Results and Proofs}\label{sec:theory}
The theoretical purpose of this section is not to prove a general theorem about all possible cellar-like representations. It is to prove, completely and explicitly, what the implemented boundary-debt state does and does not forget.

\subsection{Exposure and decomposition}
\begin{theorem}[Every assigned coordinate is exposed at every cut]\label{thm:exposure}
Let $n=2h+1$ be odd, and let $t$ satisfy $1\le t<n$. For each shift $k\in\{1,\dots,h\}$ define the left and right exposure windows
\begin{equation}
  L_k=[0,k-1], \qquad R_k=[t-k,t-1],
\end{equation}
where $R_k$ is intersected with $[0,t-1]$ when $t-k<0$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \bigcup_{k=1}^{h} (L_k \cup R_k) = [0,t-1].
\end{equation}
Equivalently, every assigned prefix index belongs to some left or right boundary window for a half-correlation shift no larger than $h$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We compute the two unions separately.

First,
\begin{equation}
  \bigcup_{k=1}^{h} L_k = [0,h-1],
\end{equation}
because the intervals are nested and the largest one is $L_h=[0,h-1]$.

Second, if $t-k<0$ then $R_k\cap[0,t-1]=[0,t-1]$, so the claim is immediate. It therefore suffices to consider the case $t-k\ge 0$. In that case the right windows are also nested as $k$ grows, and their union is
\begin{equation}
  \bigcup_{k=1}^{h} R_k = [\max(0,t-h),t-1].
\end{equation}

We now distinguish two cases.

\textbf{Case 1: $t\le h$.} Then $[0,h-1]\supseteq[0,t-1]$, so the left-window union alone already covers the full assigned prefix.

\textbf{Case 2: $h<t<2h+1$.} Since $t\le 2h$, we have $t-h\le h$. Therefore the two covering intervals
\begin{equation}
  [0,h-1] \quad\text{and}\quad [t-h,t-1]
\end{equation}
overlap or touch: the left interval ends at $h-1$ and the right interval begins at $t-h\le h$. Hence their union is the full interval $[0,t-1]$.

In both cases the union over all left and right boundary windows equals $[0,t-1]$. This proves that every assigned coordinate is exposed by at least one half-correlation shift. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact decomposition of the half-autocorrelation at a tail cut]\label{lem:decomposition}
Fix a tail panel $P$ of odd length $n=2h+1$ with fixed prefix $p\in\bits^B$, free-tail length $m=n-B$, and target vector $\tau$. Let $x\in\bits^r$ be a free-prefix assignment, let $u=m-r$, and let $y\in\bits^u$ be a completion candidate. Write $a=px$ for the assigned prefix of length $t=B+r$, and define
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_x(k)=c_{a0^u}(k), \qquad 1\le k\le h.
\end{equation}
For each shift $k\in\{1,\dots,h\}$,
\begin{align}
  c_{axy}(k) = \alpha_x(k)
  &+ \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} a_{t-k+j} y_j \label{eq:cross-term} \\
  &+ \sum_{j=0}^{u-k-1} y_j y_{j+k} \label{eq:internal-term} \\
  &+ \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} y_{u-k+j} p_j. \label{eq:wrap-term}
\end{align}
When $u<k$, the middle sum is empty and contributes zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $k\in\{1,\dots,h\}$. By definition,
\begin{equation}
  c_{axy}(k)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} z_i z_{i+k\bmod n},
\end{equation}
where $z=axy$ is the full completed word.

We partition the index set $\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}$ according to the location of the pair $\bigl(i,i+k\bmod n\bigr)$ relative to the cut at position $t$.

\textbf{1. Fully assigned terms.} If both endpoints lie in the assigned region $[0,t-1]$, then the corresponding contribution is already present in the zero-padded word $a0^u$. Summing all such terms yields exactly $\alpha_x(k)$.

\textbf{2. Crossing terms from assigned to unread suffix.} These are the indices $i$ for which $i\in[0,t-1]$ and $i+k\in[t,n-1]$. The second condition is equivalent to $i\in[t-k,t-1]$. Because $k\le h<B\le t$, the interval $[t-k,t-1]$ lies entirely inside the assigned region. Writing $i=t-k+j$ with $0\le j\le k-1$, the contribution becomes
\begin{equation}
  z_i z_{i+k}=a_{t-k+j} y_j.
\end{equation}
Summing these $k$ terms gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-term}.

\textbf{3. Suffix-internal terms.} These are the indices $i$ for which both $i$ and $i+k$ lie in the unread suffix interval $[t,n-1]$. Writing $i=t+j$, this requires $0\le j\le u-k-1$. The contribution is then $y_j y_{j+k}$, and summing over all such $j$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:internal-term}. If $u<k$ there are no such indices and the sum is empty.

\textbf{4. Wrap terms from unread suffix back into the fixed left boundary.} These are the indices $i$ in the unread suffix for which $i+k\ge n$, so the second endpoint wraps to the beginning of the word. Such indices are exactly $i=n-k,\dots,n-1$. Because $n=t+u$, we can write them as $i=t+u-k+j$ with $0\le j\le k-1$. The wrapped endpoint is then
\begin{equation}
  i+k-n = j,
\end{equation}
which lies in the fixed left boundary because $j<k\le h<B$. Therefore the corresponding contribution is
\begin{equation}
  z_i z_{i+k\bmod n}=y_{u-k+j} p_j,
\end{equation}
and summing over $j=0,\dots,k-1$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:wrap-term}.

These four cases are exhaustive, because every cyclic pair either stays fully in the assigned region, crosses the cut from assigned to unread, stays inside the unread suffix, or wraps from the unread suffix back to the beginning. Their contributions are disjoint, so adding them gives the stated identity. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact residual-state sufficiency}
\begin{theorem}[Boundary-debt state determines the exact completion set]\label{thm:sufficient}
Let $P$ be a fixed tail panel, and let $x$ and $x'$ be two free prefixes on that panel. If
\begin{equation}
  \BD_P(x)=\BD_P(x'),
\end{equation}
then the two prefixes have the same remaining length, the same remaining weight, and the same exact completion set:
\begin{equation}
  \Comp_P(x)=\Comp_P(x').
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
  \Ext_P(x)=\Ext_P(x').
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $x$ have length $r$ and $x'$ have length $r'$. Equality of boundary-debt states includes equality of the first two coordinates $r$ and $t=B+r$, so $r=r'$ and the common unread-tail length is $u=m-r$. Equality of the remaining-weight coordinate also guarantees that the admissible completion tails for $x$ and $x'$ are drawn from the same set of length-$u$ binary words of the required weight.

Fix any such candidate completion $y\in\bits^u$. We will show that the full half-autocorrelation vectors of $pxy$ and $px'y$ coincide componentwise. Let $k\in\{1,\dots,h\}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:decomposition},
\begin{align}
  c_{pxy}(k) &= \alpha_x(k)
  + \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} a_{t-k+j} y_j
  + \sum_{j=0}^{u-k-1} y_j y_{j+k}
  + \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} y_{u-k+j} p_j, \\
  c_{px'y}(k) &= \alpha_{x'}(k)
  + \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} a'_{t-k+j} y_j
  + \sum_{j=0}^{u-k-1} y_j y_{j+k}
  + \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} y_{u-k+j} p_j.
\end{align}
Because $\BD_P(x)=\BD_P(x')$, the assigned pair vectors satisfy $\alpha_x=\alpha_{x'}$. Equality of boundary-debt states also includes equality of the right boundary words $\rho_x$ and $\rho_{x'}$, and the indices $t-k,\dots,t-1$ used in the crossing term are exactly the last $k$ entries of those right boundary words. Hence
\begin{equation}
  a_{t-k+j}=a'_{t-k+j}\qquad\text{for }0\le j\le k-1.
\end{equation}
The suffix-internal term and the wrap term depend only on the common suffix $y$ and the fixed left boundary $p_0,\dots,p_{h-1}$, which is the same for both prefixes because the panel itself is fixed. Therefore every summand on the right-hand side agrees, and so
\begin{equation}
  c_{pxy}(k)=c_{px'y}(k)
\end{equation}
for every $k\in\{1,\dots,h\}$.

Thus the full half-autocorrelation vectors of $pxy$ and $px'y$ are identical for every admissible completion tail $y$. Since the target vector $\tau$ is also fixed by the panel, $pxy$ satisfies the exact target if and only if $px'y$ does. Therefore the set of completion tails $y$ that solve the panel is the same for $x$ and $x'$, which proves
\begin{equation}
  \Comp_P(x)=\Comp_P(x').
\end{equation}
The extendability equivalence is immediate because a set is nonempty if and only if an equal set is nonempty. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No stack-only extendability split on the implemented encoding]\label{cor:no-stack-split}
For any fixed tail panel $P$, if two prefixes share the same boundary-debt state, then no refinement of those prefixes by dyadic cellar stack memory can change either their extendability predicate or their exact completion set.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The cellar state is an augmentation of the boundary-debt state; it carries strictly more information, not less. By Theorem~\ref{thm:sufficient}, once the boundary-debt state is fixed, the exact completion set is already fixed. Therefore changing only the stack component while keeping boundary debt unchanged cannot change the completion set or the extendability predicate. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite residual-state collapse for the executed panels]\label{cor:finite-state}
For any fixed executed tail panel $P$, the exact prefix-completion problem can be recognized by a finite-state machine whose states are the reachable boundary-debt states. In particular, visibly pushdown memory is unnecessary for exact correctness on the implemented encoding.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each component of $\BD_P(x)$ ranges over a finite set because the panel length, target, and weight are fixed. The prefix length $r$ ranges over $\{0,1,\dots,m\}$. The assigned and remaining weights are bounded by $w$. The vector $\alpha_x\in\Z^h$ is computed from a fixed-length binary word, so each coordinate lies in a finite integer interval. The left and right boundary words are binary strings of fixed length $h$.

Reading one more suffix bit updates these quantities deterministically: the prefix length increases by one, the weights update by adding the new bit, the zero-padded assigned pair vector is recomputed from the longer assigned word, and the right boundary shifts by one coordinate. Therefore there is a deterministic transition function on reachable boundary-debt states. By Theorem~\ref{thm:sufficient}, acceptance depends only on that state. Hence the executed panel family admits a finite-state recognizer and does not require pushdown memory for exact extendability. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Set-theoretic impossibility of feedback improvement on an exact static frontier]\label{prop:feedback-impossible}
Let $F\subseteq X$ be the exact extendable frontier of a control panel, and let $G\subseteq X$ be the frontier retained by a static filter. If $G=F$, then any additional filter $H\subseteq G$ that preserves all witnesses must satisfy $H=G$. Consequently no witness-preserving feedback layer can reduce frontier size below $|G|$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $G=F$, every element of $G$ is extendable and every extendable element lies in $G$. Let $H\subseteq G$ be any additional filter that preserves all witnesses. Preserving all witnesses means that every extendable element of $G$ must remain in $H$. But the extendable elements of $G$ are exactly the elements of $G$ itself, because $G=F$. Therefore every element of $G$ must belong to $H$. Since $H\subseteq G$ and $G\subseteq H$, the two sets are equal. Hence $|H|=|G|$, so no further reduction is possible without losing witnesses. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorems~\ref{thm:exposure}--\ref{thm:sufficient} are structural results about the implemented exact encoding. Proposition~\ref{prop:feedback-impossible} then converts the control-family measurements of Section~\ref{sec:results-cellar} into a formal impossibility statement for the repository's failed proof-carrying feedback objective.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What the evidence does and does not support}
The supported conclusion is precise:
\begin{quote}
\textit{For the exact tail-panel encoding implemented in \texttt{hadamard668/cellar.py}, literal cellar memory does not provide any surviving advantage over the matched static boundary-debt state on the solved control family or on the recorded order-$668$ anchor panels.}
\end{quote}
The conclusion is strong because it combines theory and measurement. Theorems~\ref{thm:sufficient} and \ref{cor:no-stack-split} show that boundary debt is already an exact residual summary for the implemented encoding. The control-family measurements show that the static frontier is not merely sound but exact on solved same-template panels. The real-anchor packet shows that the encoding also fails to surface any exact completion on the best recorded $167/80$ states or on degraded order-$668$ seed projections.

What the evidence does \emph{not} support is equally important. The paper does not prove that every weaker static summary is equivalent to the cellar stack. It does not prove that another tokenization, another completion oracle, or a proof-reuse channel could never help. It does not solve order $668$. It retires one exact encoding cleanly.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Why the no-go is informative}
The literal cellar branch fails for a more informative reason than the precursor branches. In $\mathrm{H1}$ and $\mathrm{H2}$ the negative evidence is comparative: the CA-style local dynamics lose or tie against a matched non-CA baseline. In the literal cellar branch the negative evidence is structural. The boundary-debt state already captures the exact completion information of the implemented encoding, so the dyadic stack has no exact work left to do. On the solved control family that structural claim is visible as frontier exactness and prefix-identifying static state; on the real-anchor packet it is visible as exhaustive zero frontier.

This kind of negative result is useful because it prevents the phrase ``cellar automata'' from acquiring evidentiary credit it has not earned. The executed branch really did change the representation and really did implement a pushdown-style memory discipline. It just did not buy a new exact information channel on the panels that matter here. That is a stronger conclusion than merely failing to solve the problem with a few heuristic runs.

\subsection{Limitations}
Several limitations must remain explicit.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item The sufficiency theorem applies to the implemented boundary-debt state, which is intentionally strong. It does not prove that every weaker non-stack summary would be equivalent.
  \item The real-anchor stress packet contains zero exact completions on all eight panels. That is meaningful as a no-go for this encoding, but it does not create a positive control for transfer or proof reuse.
  \item The precursor $\mathrm{H1}$ control packet still has exact-hit rate $0/16$ for both serious methods under the locked budget. It ranks heuristics but does not demonstrate solver competence under that budget.
  \item The structured $\mathrm{H2}$ packet remains underpowered on the real order-$668$ task because it contains only one degraded start. That is enough to reject the executed repair rule, not enough to classify all structured repair variants.
  \item The paper does not benchmark weaker static residual states or alternative symbolic frontiers. Those would be new branches and should not inherit credit from the current no-go.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What remains open}
The most important open question is not ``should we spend more compute on this stack?'' The answer to that is no. The open question is whether a future branch could change the value proposition away from raw stack memory and toward one of three narrower goals.

First, a weaker static summary might fail where the current boundary-debt state succeeds. That is mathematically possible because Theorem~\ref{thm:sufficient} is about one exact summary, not all summaries. Second, a future branch might target proof reuse rather than single-panel exactness: the present paper shows that extra feedback cannot help when the static frontier is already exact, but it does not exclude transfer across panels or orders. Third, a different tokenization could make pushdown structure meaningful. None of those possibilities is validated here, and each would require a new matched non-automaton comparator and a new solved same-template control packet.

\subsection{Societal and scientific implications}
The direct societal stakes are low because the paper concerns negative evidence in pure-math search. The scientific-process stakes are less trivial. AI-assisted discovery pipelines can generate novel labels much faster than they generate novel mathematical information. The repo's value here lies in its restraint: it tested a literal cellar branch, proved exactly what its residual state remembers, and stopped once the evidence showed that the stack added no surviving advantage. Cleanly benchmarked negative results of that kind can save downstream compute and reduce the chance that expressive terminology is mistaken for a new algorithmic channel.

\section{Conclusion}
The repository does not solve the Hadamard order-$668$ problem. Its earlier flat cellular-automata branches fail or tie under matched controls, and its later literal cellar reopen also fails, but for a more exact reason. On the implemented tail-panel encoding, the matched static boundary-debt summary already determines exact completion sets. The solved control family confirms that this static frontier is exact and prefix-identifying, and the real-anchor stress family shows no exact completion and no stack-only frontier on any recorded panel.

The resulting contribution is narrow but solid. It is not ``cellar automata solve Hadamard $668$.'' It is ``the executed literal cellar encoding collapses to a matched static residual state and should be retired.'' Any future reopen should therefore start by changing the representation or the value proposition, not by extending the current stack discipline or spending more budget on the same exact tail-panel oracle.

\section{Additional Proof Detail}
\subsection{Why the right boundary word is sufficient for crossing terms}
In Lemma~\ref{lem:decomposition}, the crossing term at shift $k$ uses the assigned indices $t-k,t-k+1,\dots,t-1$. The right boundary word $\rho_x$ has length $h$, and $k\le h$, so those $k$ indices are exactly the last $k$ entries of $\rho_x$. This is why the full assigned prefix does not need to be stored explicitly once $\alpha_x$ and $\rho_x$ are known.

\subsection{Why the wrap term only touches the fixed left boundary}
Because the panel fixes more than half of the word, $B>h$, every wrap target index $j\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}$ lies inside the fixed base prefix, not inside the unread suffix. Hence the wrap contribution depends on the fixed left boundary word $\lambda$ and the tail bits near the end of the unread suffix, but not on any deeper stack summary.

\section{Hyperparameter and Design-Scope Debt}
The literal cellar branch has no tunable move schedule analogous to the precursor CA rules, but it still has representation debt.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item The implemented state stores an especially strong boundary summary. That was the right conservative choice for a first falsification test, but it likely accelerates collapse to static exactness.
  \item The executed panels all use contiguous free tails of lengths $10$, $12$, or $14$. Another tokenization could behave differently.
  \item The exact oracle is exhaustive within each panel. A future transfer-oriented branch might seek to learn or approximate reusable constraints instead of recomputing exact completion from scratch.
\end{enumerate}
These are not excuses for the present branch. They are simply the boundaries of the conclusion proved here.
