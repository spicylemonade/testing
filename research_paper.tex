\begin{abstract}
We study Kimberling's prime-separator array, a multiplicative mex construction whose first row is OEIS A129259 and whose defining open problem asks whether the gap sequence $T(1,n+1)-T(1,n)$ is bounded. Our first contribution is exact and theoretical. We prove that at every step the new row and column terms are precisely the first two missing positive integers of the current restricted product set, that the two border sequences strictly interleave, that each row gap contains exactly one column term and therefore one forced axis-$1$ witness, that the archived \texttt{row\_immediate} rule is exactly the original recurrence, that the archived \texttt{column\_immediate} rule is the exact axis swap, and that every prime lies on exactly one axis. Our second contribution is computational and negative. The validated million-step run reaches record row gap $30$, and the matched-control novelty-deepening corpus adds one independent checker agreement through $10^5$ steps together with late record, control, and surrogate windows. These artifacts show that composite-only late record gaps persist, singleton-heavy coverage does not collapse into a bounded witness grammar, the anchor/offset lane fails as a bounded-memory mechanism, the border-prefix law fails for structural reasons, full-hypergraph leaf-fraction invariants are only near-misses, and small-modulus locking does not predict the late windows; see \artifact{results/experiments/run\_1000000/record\_gap\_summary.json}, \artifact{results/novelty\_deepening/item\_026\_anchor\_metrics.json}, \artifact{results/novelty\_deepening/item\_029\_hypergraph\_metrics.json}, and \artifact{results/novelty\_deepening/item\_030\_modular\_metrics.json}. The resulting paper is therefore deliberately narrow: it is a structural clarification and negative-result dossier around Kimberling's open problem, not a claimed mechanism paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Kimberling's prime-separator array is defined by a short recurrence with unexpectedly rigid geometry. Starting from $T(1,1)=1$, one chooses the next first-row term as the least positive integer missing from the current $n\times n$ multiplicative border square, then chooses the next first-column term as the least remaining miss, and finally fills the new row and column multiplicatively. The opening rows are
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2&4&7&9&13&15&18&23&25&29\\
3&6&12&21&27&39&45&54&69&75&87\\
5&10&20&35&45&65&75&90&115&125&145\\
8&16&32&56&72&104&120&144&184&200&232\\
11&22&44&77&99&143&165&198&253&275&319
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Every prime lies on exactly one axis, and the first-row difference sequence begins
\[
1,2,3,2,4,2,3,5,2,4,2,5,4,2,4,3,2,4,3,2,2,4,4,7,\ldots
\]
\citep{oeisA129258,oeisA129259}. Kimberling's public problem asks whether these differences remain bounded \citep{kimberling100conjectures}.

The repository studied here already contains more than a term dump. It includes a validated generator, witness-carrying record-gap logs, selected full witness hypergraphs, phase-4 million-step experiments, and a later phase of matched-control novelty-deepening audits. At the same time, the repository's own verification layer is explicit about scope: the supported contribution is not a theorem settling boundedness and not a new positive mechanism, but a tighter description of what is exact, what is reproducibly observed, and which plausible mechanism stories have now failed on the available corpus.

The paper adopts that narrower claim hierarchy. In our assessment, three comparison classes matter. First, OEIS A129258/A129259 and Kimberling's problem page already occupy the public provenance of the object and the question \citep{oeisA129258,oeisA129259,kimberling100conjectures}. Second, the closest live mathematical overlap risk is Ford's multiplication-table and divisor-in-an-interval branch, because any explanation phrased purely in local factor coverage threatens to drift into an established restricted-product-set literature \citep{ford2006divisor2y,ford2008divisorinterval,ford2011multiplicationtable}. Third, multiplicative-basis papers form a weaker comparison class that matters mainly as a guardrail against reframing the recurrence into a different sparse-product problem \citep{dressler1970newmultiplicativebases,nathanson1987multiplicativerepresentations,pus1992multiplicativebases,pach2017multiplicativebases}. The repo's novelty ledger reaches exactly the same conclusion: what survives cleanly is a structural cleanup plus a negative-result evidence package, not a broad mechanism claim; see \artifact{results/literature/prior\_art\_gap.md} and \artifact{results/verification/novelty\_report.md}.

Our contributions are therefore the following.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}, leftmargin=2.8em]
\item We prove exact recurrence identities that were previously scattered across code and notes: the original rule picks the first two missing positive integers of the current product set; the border sequences strictly interleave; each row gap contains exactly one column term; the \texttt{row\_immediate} rule is identical to the baseline; the \texttt{column\_immediate} rule is the exact axis swap; and every prime lies on exactly one axis.
\item We synthesize the validated baseline computation. The archived million-step run and fresh replay agree on the structural digest, reproduce the published small prefix, and extend the observed record row gap to $30$.
\item We integrate the repository's late matched-control evidence. An independent checker agrees with the baseline through $10^5$ steps; the shared corpus contains $5$ late baseline record windows, $45$ anchor-matched non-record controls, and $50$ affine surrogates; and the anchor/backbone, prefix, hypergraph, and modular candidate mechanisms all fail to produce a proof-quality, horizon-independent explanation.
\item We restate the open problem more sharply than the public provenance alone allows. The recurrence has a rigid local geometry, and some late-window observables are highly discriminative on the current corpus, but none of the present arguments proves boundedness or unboundedness of $T(1,n+1)-T(1,n)$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Public provenance}
The array itself is OEIS A129258 and the first row is OEIS A129259 \citep{oeisA129258,oeisA129259}. Kimberling's page ``100 Conjectures and/or Problems'' records the bounded-difference question explicitly \citep{kimberling100conjectures}. Accordingly, we do not treat reconstruction of the array, reproduction of the first-row prefix, or restatement of the prime-separation phenomenon as novel. The differentiated contribution of the present paper must therefore come from formal consequences of the recurrence or from a stronger validated evidence package than the public provenance provides.

\subsection{Closest overlap risk: restricted product sets}
In our assessment, the most important overlap branch is Ford's work on multiplication tables and divisor intervals \citep{ford2006divisor2y,ford2008divisorinterval,ford2011multiplicationtable}. The reason is structural rather than terminological: at step $n$, the recurrence asks which integers near the frontier are represented in the restricted product set $\R_n\Cset_n$. Any proposed explanation of long row gaps that appeals only to local factor coverage, divisor availability, or distinct-product density risks becoming a reformulation of an existing restricted-product-set question rather than a genuinely $T$-specific theorem. This is an author judgment, not a theorem of Ford's papers; it is also the explicit novelty boundary recorded in \artifact{results/literature/prior\_art\_gap.md} and \artifact{results/verification/novelty\_report.md}.

\subsection{More distant multiplicative-basis comparisons}
The multiplicative-basis literature provides a second comparison class \citep{dressler1970newmultiplicativebases,nathanson1987multiplicativerepresentations,pus1992multiplicativebases,pach2017multiplicativebases}. These papers study sparse sets whose products represent integers, but they do not address Kimberling's coupled mex dynamics. They are relevant here mainly as a warning against overreframing: if one drops the endogenous update order and treats the border sets only as a sparse multiplicative basis, the discussion moves away from Kimberling's recurrence and toward a different established topic.

\subsection{How this paper differs}
Relative to the public provenance, this paper adds complete proofs of exact recurrence properties, a digest-checked computation package through one million steps, and a matched-control negative-result audit. Relative to Ford-style local coverage, the paper is narrower: it does not claim a new asymptotic theory of restricted multiplication tables, and it does not claim a $T$-specific positive mechanism that separates cleanly from generic local coverage. Relative to the multiplicative-basis branch, the contribution remains tied to the actual recurrence rather than to an abstract basis problem.

\section{Background and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
\subsection{Definitions}
Let
\[
r_n := T(1,n), \qquad c_n := T(n,1)
\]
for $n\ge 1$, so $r_1=c_1=1$. Define the finite border sets
\[
\R_n := \{r_1,\dots,r_n\}, \qquad \Cset_n := \{c_1,\dots,c_n\},
\]
and their restricted product set
\[
\Pset_n := \R_n\Cset_n := \{xy : x\in \R_n,\ y\in \Cset_n\}.
\]
The recurrence is
\[
r_{n+1} = \mex(\Pset_n), \qquad
c_{n+1} = \mex\bigl(\Pset_n \cup \{r_{n+1}\}\bigr),
\]
with $T(m,n)=c_m r_n$ for all $m,n\ge 1$.

\begin{definition}[Row gap, offset, and row-gap window]
For $n\ge 1$, define the \emph{row gap}
\[
g_n := r_{n+1}-r_n,
\]
the \emph{offset}
\[
\delta_n := c_n-r_n,
\]
and the open \emph{row-gap window}
\[
W_n := \{r_n+1,\dots,r_{n+1}-1\}.
\]
If $g_n>1$, then $W_n$ is nonempty and contains the skipped values between two consecutive row terms.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Anchor and left/right depths]
When $g_n>1$, the \emph{anchor} of the row-gap window is the previous column term $c_n$. Define
\[
\ell_n := c_n-r_n-1, \qquad
u_n := r_{n+1}-c_n-1.
\]
Thus $\ell_n$ and $u_n$ are respectively the numbers of skipped values strictly to the left and right of the anchor inside $W_n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Witness]
Fix $n$ and a skipped value $v\in W_n$. A \emph{witness} for $v$ at step $n$ is a factor pair $(x,y)\in \R_n\times \Cset_n$ such that $xy=v$. The baseline corpus stores one chosen witness and its multiplicity for each skipped value in each record interval; selected late gaps and matched controls also store the full admissible witness-pair set.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Record gap]
A \emph{record gap} is an index $n$ such that
\[
g_n > \max_{1\le k<n} g_k.
\]
\end{definition}

\subsection{Matched-control corpus}
The novelty-deepening phase builds a single shared corpus from the baseline million-step run; see \artifact{results/novelty\_deepening/shared\_corpus.json}. The corpus contains:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
\item the five baseline record windows with $g_n\ge 20$,
\item forty-five anchor-matched non-record control windows drawn from the same late trajectory,
\item fifty affine size-matched surrogate windows.
\end{itemize}
For every such window, the corpus stores the anchor position, left/right depths, prime/composite counts, full witness-pair data, and several projected graph summaries. The point of this corpus is not to prove boundedness; it is to test whether candidate mechanism observables distinguish true record windows from comparable windows that are not records.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Validated generator}
The baseline implementation \artifact{scripts/prime\_separator.py} stores the current border sequences, maintains an extendable coverage array for $\Pset_n$, and updates that coverage incrementally when a new row or column term is appended. Prefix validation is built into the emitted contract: the script checks the given $5\times 11$ table, the first-row prefix, and the first-column prefix against the problem statement. The contract digest stored in \artifact{contract.json} is a SHA-256 hash of the border sequences and record-gap locations, so exact replay can be checked mechanically.

\subsection{Pseudocode}
\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
\small
\textbf{Algorithm 1. Baseline border generator}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Initialize $r_1=c_1=1$, $\R_1=\Cset_1=\{1\}$, and coverage $\Pset_1=\{1\}$.
\item For $n=1,2,\dots,N-1$:
  \begin{enumerate}[label*=\arabic*.]
  \item Set $r_{n+1}$ to the least positive integer not in $\Pset_n$.
  \item Set $c_{n+1}$ to the least positive integer not in $\Pset_n$ and different from $r_{n+1}$.
  \item If $g_n=r_{n+1}-r_n$ is a new record, enumerate each integer $v$ with $r_n<v<r_{n+1}$ and store:
    \begin{enumerate}[label*=\arabic*.]
    \item prime/composite status,
    \item one witness $(x,y)\in \R_n\times\Cset_n$ with $xy=v$,
    \item witness multiplicity inside $\R_n\times\Cset_n$.
    \end{enumerate}
  \item Append $r_{n+1}$ to $\R_n$ and mark all products $r_{n+1}c$ with $c\in\Cset_n$.
  \item Append $c_{n+1}$ to $\Cset_n$ and mark all products $rc_{n+1}$ with $r\in\R_{n+1}$.
  \end{enumerate}
\item Emit the border sequences, record-gap corpus, prefix checks, structural digest, and resource counters.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\subsection{Complexity}
Let
\[
W(L) := \#\{(i,j): r_i c_j \le L\}
\]
be the number of admissible border-factor incidences up to a coverage limit $L$. The implementation runs in time proportional to the total number of marked incidences plus linear candidate scans for the successive mex calls. Consequently the cost through step $N$ is
\[
O\bigl(W(L_N) + L_N\bigr),
\]
where $L_N$ is the largest internal coverage-array index reached during the run. This is an implementation bound rather than an asymptotic theorem about the sequence itself.

\subsection{Independent checker and matched-control pipeline}
The late phase of the project adds \artifact{scripts/novelty\_deepening.py}. It performs five tasks relevant to the final paper.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), leftmargin=2.2em]
\item It runs a separately written checker and compares its border sequences with the main generator on a substantial prefix.
\item It extracts late baseline record windows and matched non-record windows from the baseline million-step run.
\item It constructs affine surrogate windows of the same sizes.
\item It computes anchor, offset, recent-prefix, and full-witness graph observables on all windows.
\item It emits summary metrics for the anchor/backbone, schedule, border-prefix, hypergraph, and modular candidate mechanisms.
\end{enumerate}
This pipeline is deliberately bounded. It does not search for larger horizons or widen the hypothesis family beyond the three lanes already recorded in \artifact{results/swarm/hypotheses.json}.

\section{Theoretical Results and Proofs}
\label{sec:theory}
\subsection{Second-mex geometry}
\begin{lemma}[Frontier interval characterization]
\label{lem:frontier}
For every $n\ge 1$,
\[
[1,r_{n+1}-1] \subseteq \Pset_n
\]
and
\[
\Pset_n \cap [1,c_{n+1}-1] = [1,c_{n+1}-1]\setminus\{r_{n+1}\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By definition, $r_{n+1}=\mex(\Pset_n)$. This means exactly that every positive integer less than $r_{n+1}$ belongs to $\Pset_n$, which proves the first inclusion.

Now consider the second claim. By definition, $c_{n+1}$ is the least positive integer that is neither in $\Pset_n$ nor equal to $r_{n+1}$. Since $r_{n+1}\notin \Pset_n$, this means the positive integers missing from $\Pset_n$ and strictly smaller than $c_{n+1}$ consist of the single value $r_{n+1}$. Equivalently,
\[
\Pset_n \cap [1,c_{n+1}-1] = [1,c_{n+1}-1]\setminus\{r_{n+1}\}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Immediate row insertion fills the only hole below $c_{n+1}$]
\label{cor:fillhole}
For every $n\ge 1$,
\[
\R_{n+1}\Cset_n \cap [1,c_{n+1}-1] = [1,c_{n+1}-1].
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:frontier}, the only missing integer below $c_{n+1}$ in $\Pset_n=\R_n\Cset_n$ is $r_{n+1}$. After adjoining $r_{n+1}$ to the row border, that missing value becomes represented as
\[
r_{n+1} = r_{n+1}\cdot 1,
\]
because $1=c_1\in \Cset_n$. All values already represented in $\Pset_n$ remain represented in $\R_{n+1}\Cset_n$. Therefore every positive integer below $c_{n+1}$ lies in $\R_{n+1}\Cset_n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact update-rule identities}
\begin{proposition}[The \texttt{row\_immediate} rule is exactly the original recurrence]
\label{prop:rowimmediate}
Suppose one modifies the update order as follows: after choosing $r_{n+1}=\mex(\Pset_n)$, immediately adjoin $r_{n+1}$ to the row border and only then choose the next column term as the least positive integer missing from $\R_{n+1}\Cset_n$. The resulting row and column sequences are identical to $(r_n)$ and $(c_n)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $n$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:fillhole}, every positive integer less than $c_{n+1}$ belongs to $\R_{n+1}\Cset_n$. Also, $c_{n+1}\notin \R_{n+1}\Cset_n$, because if $c_{n+1}$ belonged to $\R_{n+1}\Cset_n$, then either it already belonged to $\Pset_n$ or it would equal $r_{n+1}\cdot 1$; the first possibility contradicts the definition of $c_{n+1}$, and the second is impossible because $c_{n+1}\neq r_{n+1}$. Therefore $c_{n+1}$ is precisely the least missing positive integer of $\R_{n+1}\Cset_n$.

This shows that the modified rule chooses exactly the same $c_{n+1}$ as the original rule, once the same $r_{n+1}$ has been chosen. Since both constructions start from the same initial data $(r_1,c_1)=(1,1)$, induction on $n$ proves that all subsequent row and column terms coincide.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Strict interleaving]
\label{prop:interleave}
For every $n\ge 2$,
\[
r_n < c_n < r_{n+1}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
1=r_1=c_1 < r_2 < c_2 < r_3 < c_3 < \cdots .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $n\ge 2$. The term $r_n$ is the first missing positive integer of $\Pset_{n-1}$, and $c_n$ is the second missing positive integer of the same set. Therefore $r_n<c_n$.

To prove $c_n<r_{n+1}$, observe first that by Proposition~\ref{prop:rowimmediate}, adjoining $r_n$ immediately does not change the next column choice. Hence $c_n$ is already represented in $\R_n\Cset_{n-1}$ and therefore also in $\Pset_n=\R_n\Cset_n$. Since $r_{n+1}=\mex(\Pset_n)$, every positive integer smaller than $r_{n+1}$ belongs to $\Pset_n$. In particular, because $c_n\in\Pset_n$, the mex $r_{n+1}$ cannot be less than or equal to $c_n$. Thus $c_n<r_{n+1}$.

The displayed chain follows by applying the same argument successively for $n=2,3,\dots$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unique column term in each row gap]
\label{cor:uniquecolumn}
For every $n\ge 2$, the open interval $(r_n,r_{n+1})$ contains exactly one column term, namely $c_n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:interleave}, one has $r_n<c_n<r_{n+1}$. Thus $c_n$ lies in the interval.

Now let $m<n$. Then the interleaving chain gives $c_m<r_n$, so $c_m\notin(r_n,r_{n+1})$. Let $m>n$. Then $c_m>c_n$ and, again by interleaving, $c_m>r_{n+1}$, so $c_m\notin(r_n,r_{n+1})$. Therefore $c_n$ is the only column term in that interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Forced unit witness]
\label{cor:unitwitness}
For every $n\ge 2$, the skipped interval $(r_n,r_{n+1})$ contains exactly one value with a forced witness of the form $1\cdot c$, namely $c_n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:uniquecolumn}, the only column term in the interval is $c_n$. Since $1=r_1\in \R_n$, the product $1\cdot c_n$ represents $c_n$ inside $\Pset_n$. No other value in the interval can have the form $1\cdot c_m$, because that would require a second column term $c_m$ in the same interval, which Corollary~\ref{cor:uniquecolumn} forbids.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Anchor decomposition]
\label{cor:anchor}
For every $n\ge 2$ with $g_n>1$, the row-gap window $W_n$ decomposes as
\[
W_n = \{r_n+1,\dots,c_n-1\}\,\cup\,\{c_n\}\,\cup\,\{c_n+1,\dots,r_{n+1}-1\}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\ell_n=\delta_n-1, \qquad u_n=g_n-\delta_n-1,
\]
and therefore
\[
g_n = \ell_n + u_n + 1.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:uniquecolumn} states that $c_n$ is the unique column term in $(r_n,r_{n+1})$. The open interval therefore splits uniquely at $c_n$ into the skipped values to the left of the anchor, the anchor itself, and the skipped values to its right. Counting integers gives
\[
\ell_n = (c_n-1) - (r_n+1) + 1 = c_n-r_n-1 = \delta_n-1
\]
and
\[
u_n = (r_{n+1}-1) - (c_n+1) + 1 = r_{n+1}-c_n-1 = g_n-\delta_n-1.
\]
Adding the three pieces of the decomposition yields $g_n=\ell_n+u_n+1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The \texttt{column\_immediate} rule is the axis swap]
\label{prop:swap}
Consider the update rule that first chooses the least missing positive integer as the new column term, immediately adjoins it to the column border, and then chooses the next row term from the updated product set. If the resulting sequences are denoted $(r_n',c_n')$, then for all $n\ge 1$,
\[
r_n' = c_n, \qquad c_n' = r_n.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We argue by induction on $n$. The initial values satisfy $r_1'=c_1'=1=c_1=r_1$.

Assume $r_k'=c_k$ and $c_k'=r_k$ for all $k\le n$. Then the product set of the swapped process at step $n$ is
\[
\R_n'\Cset_n' = \{r_i'c_j' : 1\le i,j\le n\}
  = \{c_i r_j : 1\le i,j\le n\}
  = \R_n\Cset_n
  = \Pset_n.
\]
Therefore the first missing positive integer of the swapped process is exactly $r_{n+1}$, but under the swapped naming convention this first choice is the new column term. Hence
\[
c_{n+1}' = r_{n+1}.
\]
After adjoining this term to the swapped column border, the updated product set is
\[
\R_n'(\Cset_n'\cup\{c_{n+1}'\}) = \Cset_n(\R_n\cup\{r_{n+1}\}),
\]
which is just $\R_{n+1}\Cset_n$ with the factors reversed. By Proposition~\ref{prop:rowimmediate}, the least missing positive integer of this updated set is $c_{n+1}$. Under the swapped naming convention this second choice is the new row term, so
\[
r_{n+1}' = c_{n+1}.
\]
This completes the induction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Prime separation}
\begin{proposition}[Every prime lies on exactly one axis]
\label{prop:primes}
Let $p$ be prime. Then there exists exactly one index $n\ge 2$ such that either $p=r_n$ or $p=c_n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $r_n$ is strictly increasing and unbounded, so is $c_n$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:interleave}. Hence there exists $n$ with $p<c_{n+1}$.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:frontier} at step $n$. Either $p=r_{n+1}$, in which case $p$ is a row term, or $p\in \Pset_n$. Suppose $p\in \Pset_n$. Then there exist $x\in \R_n$ and $y\in \Cset_n$ such that $xy=p$. Since $p$ is prime and $x,y$ are positive integers, one of the factors must be $1$. If $x=1$, then $p=y\in \Cset_n$. If $y=1$, then $p=x\in \R_n$. Thus in all cases the prime $p$ appears on at least one axis.

It remains to prove uniqueness. By Proposition~\ref{prop:interleave}, the row and column sequences are strictly increasing and disjoint except at the common initial value $1$. Since $p>1$, it cannot be both a row term and a column term. Therefore $p$ lies on exactly one axis.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:primes} recovers a public OEIS observation from the recurrence itself. The novelty here is the proof, not the fact that prime separation occurs.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What the theory does not yet imply}
The preceding results settle several exact structural questions, but they do not resolve boundedness of $(g_n)$. In particular:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
\item strict interleaving gives only $\delta_n < g_n$;
\item the forced unit witness identifies one covered point in each row gap, not the entire interval;
\item the anchor decomposition rewrites each row gap in terms of $\delta_n$ and $u_n$, but does not bound either quantity globally;
\item the variant identities prove that two nearby update-order modifications are symmetries rather than robustness tests, but they do not create new asymptotic information.
\end{itemize}
Thus the open problem still requires either a horizon-independent upper-bound invariant or a construction of arbitrarily long fully covered frontier intervals. Neither is presently known.

Several points in Table~\ref{tab:audits} are worth making explicit.

First, the independent checker closes one earlier verification gap. The repository now contains a separately written implementation that agrees with the baseline through $10^5$ steps, so the recurrence order is no longer justified only by repeated self-comparison.

Second, the anchor/backbone audit is informative precisely because it \emph{fails}. The anchor itself is theorem-level by Corollary~\ref{cor:uniquecolumn}, and the left/right depths are explicit functions of $(g_n,\delta_n)$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}. On the shared corpus, that geometry strongly distinguishes true record windows from matched controls. But the late held-out records require correction pairs outside the training support, so the apparent rule does not stabilize into a bounded alphabet. This is negative evidence against a compact memory mechanism, not a positive explanation.

Third, the schedule audit turns the old ``variant'' narrative into a consistency result. Proposition~\ref{prop:rowimmediate} and Proposition~\ref{prop:swap} already prove the exact identities globally. The audit in \artifact{results/novelty\_deepening/item\_027\_schedule\_network.md} checks the same fact on $1000$ late stored states and shows that the only schedule-independent predictive observable with strong AUROC is the right anchor depth $u_n$ already isolated by Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}. No new independent mechanism appears.

Fourth, the border-prefix obstruction is partly theorem and partly measurement. The theorem-level content is that the anchor value $c_n$ can only be represented as $1\cdot c_n$ inside the row gap. Therefore any witness rule restricted to only recent row-border terms must reach all the way back to the oldest row term. The measured depths in Table~\ref{tab:audits} show that this obstruction is not a corner case: it saturates all five late baseline record windows.

Fifth, the hypergraph and modular lanes remain negative. The full witness graphs are forests everywhere in the audited corpus, so raw cycle rank is true but useless as a discriminator. The best canonical leaf-fraction statistic is reasonably stable across canonicalizations and rejects the affine surrogates, but it still overlaps too heavily with matched non-record controls to justify a promoted invariant. The modular audit performs worse still: the best small modulus is barely above chance and cannot beat the anchor comparator.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What is now rigorous}
The present paper upgrades several features of the project from observation to theorem.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
\item The ``first two missing values'' viewpoint is exact.
\item The interleaving $r_n<c_n<r_{n+1}$ is exact.
\item Each nontrivial row gap contains exactly one column term and therefore exactly one forced axis-$1$ witness.
\item The anchor decomposition expresses the left and right depths of each row gap explicitly in terms of $(g_n,\delta_n)$.
\item The two archived update-order runs are exact symmetries of the recurrence.
\item Prime separation follows directly from the recurrence.
\end{itemize}
These statements matter because they separate genuine mathematical structure from implementation artifacts and because they explain several persistent motifs in the data, especially the single anchor marker inside each row gap.

\subsection{What the computational evidence now says}
The evidence package is also sharper than a simple longer run.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
\item The baseline generator is reproducible at one million steps and now has a separate checker that agrees through $10^5$ steps.
\item The late record-gap trajectory reaches $30$ in the baseline run and $31$ in the axis-swapped run, but those facts remain finite-horizon observations only.
\item Large late record gaps can be composite-only, so skipped primes are not the governing phenomenon.
\item The anchor geometry is genuine and strongly discriminative on the late matched corpus, yet it fails to stabilize into a bounded correction law.
\item The border-prefix and modular mechanisms fail outright.
\item The full hypergraph layer yields a descriptive near-miss but not a proof-quality invariant.
\end{itemize}
This is enough to support a careful negative-result paper. It is not enough to support a theorem-level mechanism claim.

\subsection{Remaining limitations}
Three limitations are still load-bearing.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Finite horizon.} The largest provenly observed baseline record gap is $30$, not infinity. No asymptotic claim follows from that alone.
\item \textbf{Checker horizon.} The independent checker validates the recurrence through $10^5$ steps, not through the full million-step horizon. That is a substantial improvement over the earlier state of the repo, but it is still not a fully independent million-step replication.
\item \textbf{Control coverage.} The matched-control and surrogate corpus is real and useful, but it is intentionally bounded: five late record windows, forty-five controls, and fifty surrogates. This is enough to reject several naive mechanism claims, but not enough to prove that every future candidate invariant must fail.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Open questions}
The sharpened picture leaves a more precise list of open questions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=2.2em]
\item Is the offset sequence $\delta_n=c_n-r_n$ bounded?
\item Can one control the right depth $u_n=r_{n+1}-c_n-1$ by a horizon-independent invariant extracted from the coupled mex dynamics?
\item Is there a full-witness invariant that genuinely uses the mex coupling and survives matched non-record controls, or is every such statistic destined to overlap with generic local product coverage?
\item Can one prove arbitrarily long covered frontier intervals in $\R_n\Cset_n$ by a recurrence-specific argument rather than by generic restricted-product heuristics?
\item Conversely, can one construct a rigorous lower-bound mechanism that forces unbounded growth of $g_n$?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Societal implications}
This is a pure mathematics and reproducible-computation project. Its direct societal stakes are low. The broader methodological point is that open-problem work around public sequences is especially vulnerable to rhetorical overreach. The repo's verification layer is valuable precisely because it forces the final paper to distinguish structural theorems, finite-horizon evidence, and failed mechanisms instead of treating all three as the same kind of progress.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The boundedness of the first differences of Kimberling's prime-separator array remains unresolved. What has changed is the precision of the mathematical and computational record. The recurrence has a rigid local geometry: it always chooses the first two missing values of the current product set, its border sequences strictly interleave, every row gap contains exactly one forced axis-$1$ witness, and the two archived update-order variants are exact symmetries rather than robustness probes. On the experimental side, the baseline million-step corpus extends the observed record-gap trajectory to $30$, while the later matched-control audits show that the most natural mechanism candidates fail in specific ways: the anchor/offset lane is descriptive but not bounded-memory, recent-prefix laws are structurally impossible, hypergraph invariants are near-misses rather than proofs, and small-modulus locking is nonpredictive.

The contribution of the paper is therefore narrower than the motivating open problem. It does not settle boundedness, but it does determine what the recurrence guarantees exactly, what the current evidence genuinely supports, and which mechanism stories future work must improve on rather than merely restate.

\section{Extended Proof Details}
\subsection{A direct proof that the row gaps always contain a column term}
For completeness we isolate the short argument behind Corollary~\ref{cor:uniquecolumn}. Because $r_n$ is the first missing value of $\Pset_{n-1}$ and $c_n$ is the second missing value of the same set, we have $r_n<c_n$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:rowimmediate}, once $r_n$ is adjoined, the value $c_n$ is already represented before the next row mex is taken. Therefore $c_n\in\Pset_n$, which forces $c_n<r_{n+1}$. Hence $c_n$ lies in $(r_n,r_{n+1})$. Strict interleaving then excludes all other column terms from the same interval.

\subsection{Why Proposition~\ref{prop:primes} is specific to primes}
The proof of prime separation uses only one arithmetic fact: if a prime $p$ factors as $xy$ with $x,y\in \mathbb{N}$, then one factor must be $1$. This argument does not extend to composites. In fact the whole difficulty of the boundedness question lies in the composite coverage: the late record intervals are frequently composite-only and yet almost fully represented in $\R_n\Cset_n$.

\section{Hyperparameter Sensitivity}
There are no tunable model hyperparameters in the mathematical recurrence. The only implementation-level parameters are the number of requested steps, the initial size of the extendable coverage array, and the figure-rendering resolution. The mathematical conclusions in this paper are insensitive to the initial array size because the generator dynamically extends the coverage structure as needed. The 600-DPI rendering choice affects only visual sharpness of the PNG exports and not the mathematics.
