# Cross-Domain Bridge Hypotheses For Hadamard 668

Overlap and pivot note:
The repo's verification packet already closed the flat `H1` support-repair CA and the flat `H2` defect-transport CA under matched controls. The recurrent state now promotes only bridges that change the information channel rather than the move scheduler. A targeted web check also finds `cellar automata` used in unrelated reservoir-computing and virtual-city papers, so phrase-driven reuse looks like lexical noise rather than a live Hadamard route. I therefore drop the old defect-transport and free-floating latent-space stories as primary candidates, keep reachability screening as a gatekeeper rather than a solver hypothesis, and focus on three bridges that remain surprising, testable, and materially different from the retired CA loops.

## 1. Canonical-Orbit Cellar Automaton With Deferred Autocorrelation Debt

- Title: Canonical-Orbit Cellar Automaton With Deferred Autocorrelation Debt
- Closest prior art: `Convolution numbers: the cyclic case` for the exact `167/80` obstruction, plus visibly pushdown automata as the imported formal-language mechanism.
- Why it is different: treat `cellar automata` literally as stack memory on the canonical orbit word of a weight-`80` support in `Z_167`, not as another local CA rule on raw bits. The stack carries deferred multiscale autocorrelation debt, parity debt, and symmetry obligations, so the frontier is defined by prefix extendability rather than by a heuristic score on full states.
- Falsifiable prediction: on solved same-template controls, the automaton should keep every true witness prefix while rejecting at least `99%` of random prefixes by mid-depth, and it should beat a matched non-automaton path-space search under the same exact-completion budget. If the gain disappears once stack memory is removed, the bridge fails.
- Required experiments: canonicalize weight-`80` supports to unique path or run-length words; implement a visibly pushdown prefix filter over `(position, used_weight, low-order debt, stack debt)`; benchmark witness retention and oracle calls against a matched path-space baseline on solved `4 x p` controls; then apply the same filter to the exact `167/80` target.

## 2. Proof-Carrying Cellar Automaton With SAT User-Propagator Feedback

- Title: Proof-Carrying Cellar Automaton With SAT User-Propagator Feedback
- Closest prior art: `Applying Computer Algebra Systems with SAT Solvers to the Williamson Conjecture`, `Enumeration of Complex Golay Pairs via Programmatic SAT`, and the repo's own `sat_user_propagator_ca` reserve concept.
- Why it is different: this keeps the cellar/pushdown frontier but turns exact failures into reusable local objects. Unsat cores or failed completions are translated back into short forbidden motifs, stack-transition vetoes, or weighted penalties, so the automaton learns from proof artifacts instead of remaining a static pruning layer.
- Falsifiable prediction: relative to a static pushdown filter and a same-representation exact search, the adaptive version should cut exact-solver calls and wall-clock time on held-out same-template controls without introducing false negatives on known witnesses. If the extracted conflicts stay global, nonlocal, or nonreusable, the bridge collapses into ordinary SAT+CAS plumbing.
- Required experiments: wrap the pushdown frontier around a small exact completion oracle; log unsat cores for failed completions; compress those cores into local motifs or transition guards; rerun held-out controls under a fixed oracle budget; then measure solver calls, witness retention, and end-to-end throughput against both a static filter and a non-automaton baseline.

## 3. Exact-Label Learned Cellar Automaton From Membership Queries

- Title: Exact-Label Learned Cellar Automaton From Membership Queries
- Closest prior art: `Learning Regular Sets from Queries and Counterexamples`, active learning of visibly pushdown automata, and the repo's held `learned_prefix_automaton` reserve bridge.
- Why it is different: instead of hand-designing every transition or jumping to a distant latent proposal space, use exact extendability queries on smaller same-template controls to teach a compact acceptor over path or run-length tokens. The learned automaton is still a literal automaton layer, but its state space is distilled from exact labels rather than guessed from local heuristics.
- Falsifiable prediction: a learned acceptor trained on smaller solved instances should transfer to the `167` template with near-zero false negatives on known positive prefixes and a smaller surviving frontier than handcrafted low-order bounds or explicit prefix tables. If it memorizes toy orders and does not transfer, the bridge fails.
- Required experiments: generate labeled prefixes from smaller solved cyclic instances and exact completability queries; fit a compact DFA, weighted automaton, or visibly pushdown acceptor; evaluate false-negative rate, frontier compression, and transfer on held-out controls; then compare against the handcrafted pushdown filter and simple run-length heuristics before any `167`-scale spend.
