# Cross-Domain Bridge Hypotheses for Arithmetic Kakeya via Cellular Automata

The frozen `H1_macrocell_substitution`, `H2_target_direction_abelian`, and reserve `H3_slope_bloom` branches are treated here as excluded baselines rather than live positive mechanisms. The three candidates below are narrower pivots: each keeps the cellular-automata layer only as a verifier-first search object, requires exact legal extraction to `(X; d_i; f_i; T; R)`, and is meant to be killed quickly if matched direct baselines explain the effect.

## 1. Boundary-Programmable Cellular-Automaton Controllers

- **Closest prior art:** *Regional Controllability of Cellular Automata as a SAT Problem*; SAT-based exact graph generation.
- **Why it is different:** This is an explicit pivot away from the killed autonomous macrocell story. The CA is not supposed to prove transfer by itself; it is a bounded-horizon controller whose boundary signals are synthesized so that the extracted object is already a legal arithmetic-Kakeya certificate. The imported claim is therefore about controllability of defect and boundary programs under a frozen extractor, not about CA self-similarity.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** On `2 x w` and `3 x w` low-height families with matched `X` budget, coordinate-height budget, and optimizer budget, SAT-synthesized boundary programs for a small multitrack CA should beat matched direct no-CA search in exact score frontier or exact hit rate. If every gain disappears once direct SAT search is allowed on the same certificate grammar, reject the bridge.
- **Required experiments:** Encode bounded-horizon local-rule evolution plus exact extraction into SAT or MaxSAT; compare against direct no-CA SAT search and direct random or exhaustive certificate search on the same geometry and same canonicalization rules; ablate boundary access, horizon, and state count; inspect UNSAT cores to distinguish genuine residue obstructions from weak controller budgets.

## 2. Affine-Core / Rewrite-Shell Residue Automata

- **Closest prior art:** *Simulation Limitations of Affine Cellular Automata*; *Rewrite Rule Inference Using Equality Saturation*.
- **Why it is different:** This is not an H2-style invariant relabeling and not a generic learned compiler. The bridge claim is that useful AK-flavored local rules should be mostly affine, with only a very small nonlinear shell placed at residue-isolation gates; equality saturation is used to infer the smallest exact rewrite basis needed for those gates. The pivot is from “find a clever CA” to “prove most CA families are too linear, then learn the minimum nonlinear residue logic that survives exact extraction.”
- **Falsifiable prediction:** For matched tiny exact instances, families with an affine core plus one or two inferred nonlinear gate types should outperform both fully affine rules and unconstrained nonlinear rules in verifier hit rate per search budget, while keeping extracted state complexity bounded. If the nonlinear shell keeps growing with scale, or if it gives no exact frontier lift over direct certificate search, reject the bridge.
- **Required experiments:** Classify candidate local rules by affine closure and eliminate families whose reachable residue spaces cannot isolate anti-diagonal mass; mine exact tiny certificates for recurring residue equalities and infer a minimal rewrite basis with e-graphs; compile that basis into local gate states; compare exact frontier, state-count growth, and held-out verifier agreement against fully affine controls, random nonlinear controls, and direct no-CA search.

## 3. Metastable Hyperedge-Delay Automata

- **Closest prior art:** *On the Running Time of Hypergraph Bootstrap Percolation*; *The maximal running time of hypergraph bootstrap percolation*.
- **Why it is different:** This is a pivot away from the stale critical-droplet framing. The imported object is not droplet threshold or final spanning, but maximal legal closure time in a dependency hypergraph extracted from exact local derivations. The bridge claim is that slow, bottlenecked local schedules may reuse relations more efficiently than fast-filling ones, so metastability rather than speed is the right CA-style signal.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Among exact-extracted families with matched `|X|`, coordinate height, geometry, and edge density, larger dependency-hypergraph running time should correlate with better exact score or better forced-vertex-per-seed ratio. If long transients are neutral or harmful after exact extraction, or if the apparent gain vanishes under direct no-CA baselines, reject the bridge.
- **Required experiments:** Build the dependency hypergraph whose hyperedges encode exact local derivations available before global linear closure; enumerate small families and measure maximal closure time, exact score, seed budget, and target-solvable count; compare long-running candidates against fast-spreading controls and direct no-CA search; ablate boundary defects to check whether any signal is genuine metastability rather than a saved boundary artifact.
