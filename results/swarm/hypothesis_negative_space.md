# Hypothesis Negative Space

Bootstrap note: `results/literature/gap_frontier.md` is still effectively empty, `results/swarm/director_brief.md` is absent, and there are no older swarm hypothesis artifacts to inherit. A local incremental probe of the table already sees record first-row gaps reaching `17` by index `2063`, so the family "probably bounded by a tiny constant because the early terms look tame" should be treated as derivative and weak.

The most important overlooked fact is structural, not numerical: the first row is not just a mex sequence. OEIS notes for the exact row sequence (`A127202`) show that row-1 values are multiplicatively generated from the row primes with odious exponents, and the row-prime indices (`A127220`) are themselves generated from the opposite prime-index side with the same odious-exponent rule. That makes the bounded-gap question a coupled prime-index fixed-point problem with a digital exponent constraint.

## 1. Coupled Prime-Index Fixed Point, Not Raw Mex Dynamics

Direction: attack boundedness through the recursive support set of row primes. Let `I_R` and `I_C` be the prime indices landing in row 1 and column 1. The row is the multiplicative semigroup generated by primes `p(i)` with `i in I_R`, using only odious exponents. Hypothesis: the right invariant is the coupled growth of `I_R(x)`, `I_C(x)`, and the induced row counting function, and bounded differences follow or fail according to whether this prime-index operator is contractive enough after the prime-number-theorem lift.

What prior work ignored: the prompt and existing repo artifacts treat the array as an opaque greedy table. They do not use the exact recursive factorization law on prime indices.

Testable next step:
- Estimate `I_R(x)` and `I_C(x)` from existing terms and compare them to a fixed-point density model.
- Write the row Dirichlet/Euler product conditional on `I_R` and ask whether the induced set should have bounded gaps under the predicted support density.
- Check whether new gap records correlate better with fluctuations in row-prime support than with visible prime gaps in the row itself.

Derivative overlap and pivot: this can drift into generic prime-gap folklore. The pivot is to model the recursively selected prime support, not the bare list of row primes.

Angle to avoid: any proof sketch that treats row 1 as "basically a prime subsequence with composites sprinkled in."

## 2. Interval Obstruction via Minimal Exclusion Witnesses

Direction: reframe a gap `[a_n+1, a_n+L]` as a certificate problem. Every missed integer in that interval must carry a short witness explaining why it is not in row 1: either one prime from the wrong support enters its factorization, or one exponent is evil rather than odious. Hypothesis: record gaps are governed by a small library of such local witnesses, and boundedness can be attacked by classifying how those witnesses can or cannot cover consecutive intervals. If that witness library cannot tile long intervals, the gaps are bounded; if CRT or valuation engineering can force arbitrarily long witness covers, the sequence is unbounded.

What prior work failed to test: current artifacts record terms, not exclusion mechanisms. No one is extracting minimal reasons that each skipped integer is disqualified.

Testable next step:
- For every current record gap, annotate each skipped integer with a minimal witness of exclusion.
- Cluster witnesses by low-prime valuation pattern and by first offending prime outside the row-prime support.
- Search for constructive interval covers using valuation constraints and CRT, rather than extending the table blindly.

Derivative overlap and pivot: this overlaps with multiplication-table and divisor-interval thinking. The pivot is that the witnesses must respect the odious/evil exponent law and the recursive prime-support split, not just generic product-set coverage.

Angle to avoid: Beatty/Farey/Sturmian analogies and any argument that only watches the next prime while treating the excluded composites as noise.

## 3. Witness-Carrying High-Scale Falsifier

Direction: stop paying quadratic or cubic cost for the wrong representation. The naive `n x n` product-table simulation stalls too early to distinguish "bounded but large" from "slowly growing without bound." Hypothesis: a generator built around the exact multiplicative rule, with cached prime-support state and witness-carrying exclusion logs, can push the experiment far enough to expose whether gap records keep arriving on a meaningful scale.

What prior work could not scale: low-`n` brute force gives term dumps but not reusable evidence. It also hides why a gap occurred.

Testable next step:
- Replace explicit product-table storage with support-aware factorization checks keyed by row-prime membership and odious exponents.
- Store, for every new record gap, a compact witness log for each skipped integer instead of only the final gap length.
- Set a milestone such as `10^5` first-row terms with full witness logs for every new record, then compare the record-gap trajectory against the fixed-point and witness-cover hypotheses above.

Derivative overlap and pivot: this can collapse into "compute more terms." The pivot is that every scaling step must emit proof-oriented artifacts: support statistics, exclusion witnesses, and interval-cover templates.

Angle to avoid: OEIS-style term dumping, unconstrained curve fitting, or more low-`n` polish without mechanism capture.
